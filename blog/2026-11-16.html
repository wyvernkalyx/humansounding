hard sentence caps turn prose choppy: this week's tell

This week in AI writing · November 16, 2026

Claude Fable 5.2 Obeys Style Rules Too Well, Anthropic Says

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9. Its own prompting guide says early testers rated its writing above Fable 5.1's and similar in clarity to Opus 5.5's. The same guide adds a warning for anyone who carries old prompts forward. The model follows style rules closely, so a hard limit such as one instruction per sentence in 20 words or fewer can make its prose choppy.

That is a useful reminder that a tell can come from your instructions as well as the model. A Claude Code tip suggests swapping a rigid cap for a plain request to write short, plain sentences. If your drafts read like a drumbeat of tiny sentences, check whether a rule you wrote years ago is the cause.

Outside reviews are thin and mixed. According to a BigGo summary of Every's launch-day test, Dan Shipper gave the writing a B to B-plus and said it buries the lead, while a colleague found it the easiest to read of any model she had tried. Those are two impressions, not tell counts, so no rows on our trend board changed.

Google also shipped Gemini 4 Carbon on November 10 and Hydrogen on November 12, and Inkfluence AI notes that Google's writing claims cover Argon, not the new pair. Wikipedia's guide and EQ-Bench's slop weights held still, and we could not confirm any GPTZero change. Meanwhile the Caine Prize said on November 11 that its review of its 2026 winner found no evidence of AI and did not rest on a detector score, per the Author's AI Rundown. Treat tells as clues, then use the draft checker and fix-your-ai guide.
