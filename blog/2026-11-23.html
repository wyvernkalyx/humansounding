over-compressed, hard-to-follow brevity: this week's tell

This week in AI writing · November 23, 2026

GPT-6.2 Astra Writes Shorter, and an Early Reviewer Says Too Short

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17. Its launch post says the writing is more direct and specific, with fewer generic takeaways and less filler. The developer guide adds that Astra is more concise and direct by default than earlier models, and it tells developers to revisit old "be concise" instructions. That matters to ordinary writers because a request for brevity that worked last month may now stack on top of a model that is already brief.

The early verdict is mixed. Dan Shipper of Every wrote on X that his team found Astra 6.2 a step back from Astra 6 on writing, because it seems trained to be so concise and actionable that it is hard to understand. It also gives short answers where long ones would fit. Nowrap reports that developer forum users describe stiffer writing and style instructions that do not stick. These are impressions, not tell counts.

Google launched Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, and its announcement says Neon writes clearly, with a natural, direct tone. Developer Flavio Copes liked its writing in a head-to-head test, and one X user said it needed no extra setup to drop AI tells. Nobody has published per-model phrase counts for Neon or for GPT-6.2, so our trend board only changed in one place: the markdown row now carries OpenAI's concision guidance.

The other trackers held still. Wikipedia's guide still shows its October 2026 label, EQ-Bench's Slop Score keeps its 60, 25, and 15 percent weights, and we could not confirm a GPTZero vocabulary change. The practical lesson is that brevity is a setting, not a cure. Tell the model who the reader is and how much that person already knows, then check the draft with the draft checker or the fix-your-ai guide.
