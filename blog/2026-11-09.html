This week in AI writing · November 9, 2026

A New Study Finds AI Fiction Tells You How the Room Feels

A paper accepted to the main conference of EMNLP 2026 compared 8,000 stories from four language models, in English and German, with human fiction from Project Gutenberg. According to the ACL Anthology abstract, human authors mostly ground a scene in action, with characters moving through and touching their surroundings. The models overproduce what the authors call perceived space, which stresses atmosphere and affect. In plain terms, the machine keeps telling you how the room feels.

A summary at Pith reports that GPT 4.1 reaches about 0.47 for perceived space in English story openings, against a human baseline near 0.19, and that all four models exceed the baseline in every narrative section. Pith's summary is automated, so check the paper before quoting figures. The authors also say the size of the gap varies by model and language, which is why our new trend row leaves its model column blank.

For a fiction writer, the fix is concrete. When a draft says the house felt heavy with memory, ask what a person inside it would do: cross the floor, trip on the chair, pocket the letter. The Author's AI Rundown put the study in those terms for novelists this week, and added that no new general prose model made its test bench. That also means no fresh per-model tell counts arrived.

The usual trackers held still. Wikipedia's guide still carries its October 2026 list of common signs, and EQ-Bench's Slop Score keeps its 60, 25, and 15 percent weights. We found no new multipliers on GPTZero's vocabulary page. A reported OpenAI model refresh had not shipped as of November 7, per AIVerso.
