This week's tell: Claude's "load-bearing" everything

This week in AI writing · August 31, 2026

Claude Opus 5's New Tic: Everything Is "Load-Bearing"

Claude Opus 5 has picked up a verbal tic: it calls almost everything load-bearing, a metaphor borrowed from architecture. Developers started collecting examples after one Reddit post called an ordinary software build step load-bearing rather than tidy, and the thread that followed on Hacker News passed 1,700 points. A separate analysis of tens of thousands of real Opus 5 replies found the model also writes about three times as long as its predecessor, with 2.3 times more em dashes, while leaning on simpler individual words (AlphaSignal, Aug 2026).

The tic has a name now too. Developer educator Matt Pocock coined "seamslop" after asking Opus 5 to explain a topic and getting something technically correct yet unmistakably machine-made, an over-reliance on one early metaphor that gets stretched through an entire answer. The term went properly viral in the last week of August (explainx.ai, Aug 2026), joining "load-bearing" as shorthand for a style problem that outlives any single banned word.

None of this is really about one phrase. Our own em-dash chart already shows Claude sitting above the human punctuation rate while GPT-5.4 has fallen below it, and the new Opus 5 data suggests that gap may be widening even as other labs tune dashes out. See the current numbers on our trend board.

For everyday writers the lesson is not to fear one word. If your drafts pass through Claude for editing, expect load-bearing and seamslop-style metaphor stacking, plus heavier em-dash use, more than with other assistants.
