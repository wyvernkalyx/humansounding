We switched to a four-day workweek.

Not because we wanted to squeeze five days of work into four, but because we wanted to rethink how we work in the first place.

The change pushed us to be more deliberate about meetings, priorities, and where our time goes. We’ve cut low-value routines, protected focus time, and become clearer about what actually needs to get done.

Our goals are simple:
- Give people more time to rest and recharge
- Maintain high standards for our customers
- Focus on outcomes rather than hours
- Build a way of working that’s sustainable over the long term

A four-day week isn’t automatically better. It takes discipline, experimentation, and a willingness to adjust when something isn’t working.

We’ll keep measuring the impact on productivity, customer experience, and team wellbeing—and sharing what we learn along the way.

For us, this isn’t about working less. It’s about making the time we spend working count.
