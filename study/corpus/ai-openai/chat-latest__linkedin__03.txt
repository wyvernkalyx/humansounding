One of my biggest lessons came from a project that didn’t work out.

We had a solid plan, capable people, and clear goals. But we made one critical mistake: we stayed committed to our original assumptions for too long.

When the early signals suggested we needed to change direction, we treated them as problems to overcome rather than information to learn from.

What I took away from the experience:
- Test assumptions early, especially the ones everyone agrees on.
- Make it easy for people to raise uncomfortable concerns.
- Define what would cause you to change course before you start.
- Don’t confuse persistence with progress.

The project failed, but the lessons changed how I approach every project that followed.

Success teaches you what worked. Failure can teach you what you missed.
