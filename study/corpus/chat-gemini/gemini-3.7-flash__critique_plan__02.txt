Here is a direct, critical breakdown of the blind spots and failure points in that plan.

---

### 1. "Signups" is the wrong metric to decide on
* **Signups ≠ Value:** Cheap ads can drive hundreds of low-intent signups who never complete onboarding, never return, and will never pay. 
* **The fix:** Measure **Activated Signups** (e.g., used the core feature, set up a profile, added team members) or **Qualified Leads**, not raw account creations.

### 2. Six weeks is meaningless without a budget/volume target
* **Time doesn’t equal data:** If your budget is \$20/day, six weeks will give you very little statistically significant data. If your budget is \$1,000/day, six weeks is needlessly long and expensive to learn the basics.
* **Platform learning phase:** Algorithms (Meta, Google) need ~50 conversions per ad set *per week* to optimize properly. If your budget doesn't support that threshold, the algorithm will flail for the entire six weeks.

### 3. "Decide from there" is a trap
* If you don't define **exact go/no-go thresholds** beforehand, you will fall victim to confirmation bias. When the 6 weeks are up, you’ll be tempted to rationalize mediocre numbers: *"Well, signups were low, but engagement looks okay, let's run it for another month."*
* **The fix:** Define your limits today:
  * Maximum acceptable Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC / CPA).
  * Minimum conversion rate on the landing page.
  * The exact number at which you **kill**, **pivot**, or **double down**.

### 4. You won’t know *where* the funnel broke
If at week six the campaign fails, what failed?
* Was the **ad creative/copy** bad? (Low Click-Through Rate)
* Was the **audience targeting** wrong? (High bounce rate)
* Was the **landing page/messaging** confusing? (Low visitor-to-signup rate)
* Was the **offer/pricing** unappealing?
* **The risk:** Without tracking every step of the funnel, you might abandon a great product simply because you had a bad headline on your landing page.

### 5. You risk testing only one channel instead of the product
* If you test Meta ads and it fails, you haven’t proven people don’t want your product—you’ve only proven they don’t want it *on Meta*. 
* B2B products often fail on Instagram but thrive on Google Search or LinkedIn. High-intent search traffic behaves completely differently from passive social feed traffic.

---

### How to patch this plan:

1. **Define target unit economics upfront:** *"We will spend \$X to test. A successful test means achieving a CPA under \$Y with a minimum 15% activation rate."*
2. **Split the six weeks into agile sprints:**
   * **Weeks 1–2:** Test 3–5 hooks/audiences to find high CTR.
   * **Weeks 3–4:** Optimize landing page conversion rate (CRO).
   * **Weeks 5–6:** Measure downstream activation and retention of those users.
3. **Track micro-conversions:** Monitor Ad CTR $\rightarrow$ Landing Page View $\rightarrow$ Signup $\rightarrow$ Core Action Taken.
