**Short answer:** No to *corporate-style* formal reviews, but absolutely **yes to a structured, regular alignment process.**

Running traditional, bureaucratic performance reviews (lengthy forms, 1-to-5 rating scales, 360-degree feedback tools) in a two-person company is overkill. It wastes time and introduces artificial corporate distance when you need maximum agility and trust.

However, the biggest mistake two-person teams make is assuming that *talking all day* means you are *aligned*. You still need an intentional, dedicated space to discuss performance, expectations, and the relationship.

Here is how you should handle it instead:

---

### If you are Two Co-Founders: The "Partnership Health Check"
Do not review each other like a boss reviews an employee. Treat this as a co-founder retrospective every **quarter**. 

Book a half-day off-site (even just at a coffee shop) and cover four areas:
1. **Division of Labor:** Are we stepping on each other’s toes? Are critical tasks falling through the cracks?
2. **Personal Sustainability:** What is your burnout/energy level (1–10)? What are you hating working on right now?
3. **Behavioral Feedback:** "What is one thing I am doing that makes your job harder, and one thing I’m doing that helps the most?"
4. **Vision & Alignment:** Are we still trying to build the exact same company at the exact same pace?

---

### If you are Founder + First Employee: The "Lightweight Review"
If there is a power dynamic (boss and employee), you need a system that ensures the employee feels supported, knows where they stand, and understands their future.

Run a lightweight review **every 3 to 6 months** using a simple agenda:

#### 1. Retrospective (Looking Back)
* **What went well:** Highlight 2–3 major wins.
* **What could go better:** Discuss 1–2 specific areas for improvement. (No surprises—issues should have been flagged in real-time, not saved up for months).

#### 2. Forward-Looking (Next 90 Days)
* **Top 3 Priorities:** What does success look like over the next quarter?
* **Resource Gaps:** "What do you need from me (tools, decisions, autonomy) to hit these goals?"

#### 3. Growth & Compensation
* **Career Trajectory:** How is their role evolving as the company grows?
* **Compensation/Equity:** Are they hitting the milestones tied to pay increases, bonuses, or equity vesting?

#### 4. Upward Feedback (Crucial)
* "How can I be a better manager for you?" 
* "Is there anything about how we operate that feels inefficient or frustrating?"

---

### The Rules of Feedback for a 2-Person Team

* **Never save tactical feedback for the "review":** If someone writes bad code, handles a client poorly, or misses a deadline, talk about it that day. 
* **Write down the takeaways:** Even if it’s just a 3-bullet-point summary in a shared Notion/Google Doc, document what you agreed on so you have a baseline for the next check-in.
* **Separate "Day-to-Day Status" from "Big Picture":** Your weekly check-in is for *tasks*; your quarterly check-in is for *people and performance*.

### When should you go "formal"?
Stick to this lightweight, conversation-first model until you hit **around 6 to 10 employees**, or when you hire managers who need a standardized framework to evaluate others fairly.
