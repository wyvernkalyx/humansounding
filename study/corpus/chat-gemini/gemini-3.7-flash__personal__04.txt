The key to handling neighbor tree issues without sparking a feud is to **frame the conversation around shared safety, property care, and storm prep**, rather than placing blame. 

Here is a step-by-step approach, complete with scripts, to handle this smoothly:

---

### 1. Timing and Setting
* **Don't** bring it up in the middle of a storm or right after throwing a pile of branches back over the fence.
* **Do** catch them casually when they’re outside doing yard work or on a calm weekend afternoon. An in-person conversation is much harder to misinterpret than a text or note.

---

### 2. The Mindset: "Team vs. The Weather"
Frame the tree as a shared environment issue, not *their* personal weapon against your lawn. If you treat it like an act of nature that you both need to manage, they won’t feel defensive.

---

### 3. Choose a Script That Fits Your Relationship

#### Option A: The "Safety & Preventative" Approach (Best overall)
> *"Hey [Name], that last storm was pretty wild. I noticed a few large branches came down on my side of the fence, and it got me a little worried about the roof/kids/cars if we get another big one. Have you had an arborist look at that tree recently to see if it needs some deadwood cleared out?"*

#### Option B: The "Collaborative Solution" Approach (If you're willing to help)
> *"Hey [Name], I’m planning to get someone out to trim back some branches on my side of the property before the next storm season. Since that [oak/pine/maple] is hanging over the fence line, would you be open to having the tree guy look at the whole canopy? We could potentially split the cost to clean up the dead branches on both sides."*

#### Option C: The Low-Conflict "Just Asking Permission" Approach
In most places, you legally have the right to trim branches hanging directly over your property line (as long as it doesn't kill the tree).
> *"Hey [Name], I love the shade from your tree, but the storms keep knocking down limbs in my yard. I’m thinking of hiring someone to trim back just the parts overhanging my fence line so they don't break off. Just wanted to give you a heads-up so you’re not surprised by the tree crew!"*

---

### 4. What to Do with the Branches
* **Do not** throw the branches over their fence. Even though it feels like "their" wood, in most jurisdictions, debris that falls onto your property due to weather is technically your responsibility to clean up (legally considered an "Act of God"). Throwing them back is the fastest way to start a war.
* If a massive limb falls, it's okay to ask: *"Hey, a pretty big limb from your tree came down in the yard—do you have a chainsaw, or could you help me haul it to the curb?"*

---

### 5. If They Are Dismissive or Defensive
If they brush you off with, *"Trees drop branches, that’s life,"* stay calm:
1. **Take photos** of every fallen branch and the state of the tree canopy.
2. **If the tree is clearly dead or rotting:** You can politely send a certified letter noting the hazard. If an owner is officially notified that their tree is a hazard and they ignore it, they become legally liable for future damage caused to your property. (Save this as a last resort).

---

### Summary Checklist
* [ ] **Talk in person** (not via text/note).
* [ ] **Focus on future safety/roof damage**, not the annoyance of picking up sticks.
* [ ] **Offer a solution** (getting an estimate, trimming your side).
* [ ] **Never throw wood over the fence.**
