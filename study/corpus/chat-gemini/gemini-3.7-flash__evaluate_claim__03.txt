**Yes, it is completely true.** You should **never** put a hot laptop or its battery in the refrigerator or freezer. 

While it might seem logical to cool down an overheating device quickly, doing so can permanently ruin your laptop and poses a serious safety hazard. Here is why:

---

### 1. Condensation (The Biggest Danger)
When you place a warm or hot object into a cold, humid environment like a fridge, moisture from the air rapidly condenses into liquid water. 
* This water will form **inside** the battery casing and all over the laptop’s delicate internal circuit boards.
* Once the laptop is turned back on or plugged in, this moisture can cause **short circuits, corrosion, permanent hardware failure, or even an electrical fire**.

### 2. Thermal Shock
Modern lithium-ion batteries and computer chips expand slightly when hot. Exposing them to freezing or near-freezing temperatures causes the materials to contract rapidly and unevenly. This "thermal shock" can:
* Crack internal seals, allowing battery chemicals to leak.
* Damage microscopic solder joints on the motherboard.
* Crack the laptop screen.

### 3. Damage to Battery Chemistry
Lithium-ion batteries are designed to operate best between **50°F and 95°F (10°C to 35°C)**. Extreme cold disrupts the chemical reactions inside the battery:
* It can permanently reduce the battery’s capacity to hold a charge.
* If a battery is very cold and plugged into a charger, it can cause **lithium plating** on the anode, which increases the risk of a dangerous short circuit and fire.

---

### What to Do Instead to Cool Down a Hot Laptop

If your laptop or battery is overheating, cool it down safely at room temperature:

1. **Turn it off:** Shut down the laptop immediately to stop the processor and graphics card from generating more heat.
2. **Unplug it:** Disconnect the charger, as the charging process generates significant heat.
3. **Move to a hard, flat surface:** Take it off your lap, bed, or couch. Soft fabrics block the cooling vents underneath the laptop.
4. **Elevate the back:** Prop up the back edge of the laptop with a small book or bottle cap to increase airflow beneath the chassis.
5. **Use a regular fan:** Aim a standard desk fan at the laptop to blow ambient air across it.
6. **Clean the vents:** Once cool, use a can of compressed air to clear out dust and pet hair from the intake and exhaust vents.
