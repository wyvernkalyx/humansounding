Your coworker is **partially right about the hype, but wrong that they do "nothing."** 

Standing desks are not the miracle health fix that early marketing claimed—they won’t replace the gym, and they won’t make you lose significant weight. However, they do provide distinct, scientifically proven benefits **if used correctly**.

Here is what the science actually says:

---

### Where Your Coworker Is Right (The Overhyped Myths)

1. **They don't burn many calories:**
   Standing only burns about **8 to 10 more calories per hour** than sitting. Standing for four hours a day might burn roughly 30–40 extra calories—about the equivalent of half an apple. It is not a weight-loss tool.
2. **They don't replace exercise:**
   Standing is still a "static" posture. It does not provide the cardiovascular or muscular benefits of walking, running, or strength training.
3. **Standing all day is also bad for you:**
   Swapping 8 hours of sitting for 8 hours of standing just trades one set of problems for another (varicose veins, foot pain, swollen ankles, and lower back fatigue).

---

### Where Your Coworker Is Wrong (The Real Benefits)

1. **Massive relief for back and neck pain:**
   This is the strongest, most well-documented benefit. Studies consistently show that workers who alternate between sitting and standing report significantly less lower back and neck pain compared to those who sit all day, largely because it changes spinal loading.
2. **Better blood sugar regulation:**
   Standing or moving for a bit after eating reduces blood sugar spikes compared to sitting completely still after a meal.
3. **Energy and alertness:**
   Many studies note that people feel less lethargic in the afternoon when they stand for parts of their shift, likely due to improved circulation.
4. **It prompts more movement:**
   When you are already standing, the barrier to taking a quick stretch, pacing while on a phone call, or walking to grab water is much lower than when you are settled deep into a chair.

---

### The Verdict: It’s About *Changing* Positions

The human body hates being in **any** single position for 8 hours. The real value of a standing desk isn't the *standing*—it’s the **adjustability**.

* **The ideal ratio (The 20-8-2 Rule):** Ergonomists often recommend that for every 30 minutes at work, you spend **20 minutes sitting, 8 minutes standing, and 2 minutes moving/stretching.** 

**Bottom Line:** A standing desk won't save your life or give you a six-pack, but it is one of the best tools available for reducing workplace aches, stiffness, and the fatigue that comes from sitting frozen in a chair all day.
