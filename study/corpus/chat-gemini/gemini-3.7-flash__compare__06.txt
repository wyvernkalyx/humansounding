Driving **12,000 miles per year** puts you right at the average for an American driver (roughly 33 miles per day). At this mileage, both Electric Vehicles (EVs) and Hybrids (HEVs) are excellent choices that will save you money compared to a traditional gas car, but they shine in different areas.

Here is a side-by-side comparison based on costs, lifestyle, and practical ownership.

---

### 1. Fuel / Energy Costs (For 12,000 Miles)

*Note: Calculations use U.S. national averages (Gas: $3.50/gal | Home Electricity: $0.16/kWh).*

| Vehicle Type | Efficiency Assumption | Annual Cost for 12,000 Miles |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Traditional Hybrid** (e.g., Toyota Prius, Honda CR-V Hybrid) | 45 MPG | **~$933 / year** (267 gallons of gas) |
| **Electric Vehicle (EV)** (e.g., Tesla Model Y, Hyundai Ioniq 5) | 3.5 miles per kWh | **~$548 / year** (3,428 kWh, charged at home) |

* **Winner:** **EV** (saves about **$350 to $400 per year** over a hybrid).
* *Caveat:* If you do not have home charging and rely solely on public DC fast chargers (which cost $0.35–$0.45/kWh), the EV will cost about the same or slightly more than the hybrid.

---

### 2. Maintenance and Repairs

* **EV:** 
  * **What you pay for:** Tires, cabin air filters, windshield wiper fluid, and brake fluid checks. 
  * **What you avoid:** Oil changes, spark plugs, timing belts, transmission fluid, and exhaust systems. Regenerative braking means brake pads can last 100,000+ miles.
* **Hybrid:** 
  * Combines a gas engine with an electric motor. It still requires regular oil changes, engine air filters, and mechanical transmission service.
  * However, hybrid engines experience less wear than pure gas cars, and brakes last much longer due to regenerative braking.
* **Winner:** **EV** (typically saves $200–$300/year on routine maintenance).

---

### 3. Upfront Price & Depreciation

* **Hybrid:** 
  * Generally cheaper to buy upfront ($25,000–$35,000 for standard models). 
  * Standard hybrids rarely qualify for federal tax credits.
  * Hold their resale value exceptionally well because the technology is proven and familiar.
* **EV:** 
  * Sticker prices are generally higher ($35,000–$50,000+), though prices have dropped significantly.
  * Eligible for up to a **$7,500 Federal Tax Credit** (plus state incentives) if you and the vehicle qualify, which narrows the price gap.
  * You will need to budget **$500–$1,500** for a Level 2 home charger installation.
  * EV depreciation has been steeper recently due to rapid advancements in battery technology.
* **Winner:** **Hybrid** for lower initial cash outlay; **EV** can tie if you qualify for full tax credits.

---

### 4. Convenience and Lifestyle

* **The EV Lifestyle:**
  * **The Best Part:** If you have a driveway or garage, you "fuel up" while you sleep. You will practically never visit a gas station for daily driving.
  * **The Drawback:** Long road trips require route planning around 20- to 30-minute charging stops. Cold winter weather can temporarily reduce range by 20–30%.
* **The Hybrid Lifestyle:**
  * **The Best Part:** Complete flexibility. You get 500–600 miles per tank, fill up in 5 minutes at any gas station, and don't need any home electrical upgrades.
  * **The Drawback:** You are still tethered to gas stations and fluctuating oil prices.
* **Winner:** **EV** if you own a home; **Hybrid** if you live in an apartment or take frequent long road trips.

---

### Summary: Which should you choose?

#### **Buy an Electric Car (EV) if:**
1. **You can charge at home** (garage or dedicated driveway).
2. Most of your 12,000 miles come from daily commuting and local errands.
3. You want the lowest possible running costs (fuel + maintenance).
4. You qualify for federal or state EV tax incentives.

#### **Buy a Hybrid if:**
1. **You street-park or live in an apartment** without easy access to a plug.
2. You take frequent, spontaneous road trips and don’t want to plan around chargers.
3. You want a lower purchase price without relying on tax credits.
4. You want to double your fuel economy compared to a standard gas car with zero change to your daily habits.

*(Note: If you want the best of both worlds, consider a **Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV)**, which gives you ~30–40 miles of pure electric driving for daily use, with a gas engine that kicks in for road trips).*
