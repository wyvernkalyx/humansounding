First, it is important to hear this: **feeling hesitant does not make you a bad parent.** 

It is completely normal to cherish your independence, privacy, and the routine you’ve built once your children have grown up. You have already completed the 18+ year phase of active, day-to-day parenting. 

To figure out how to move forward, it helps to break this down into three parts: understanding your hesitation, understanding his situation, and deciding on your boundaries.

---

### 1. Clarify why you are hesitating
Take a moment to pinpoint what is causing your reluctance:
* **Lifestyle/Privacy:** Do you enjoy having your space, quiet time, and autonomy?
* **Relationship Dynamics:** Are you worried you will fall back into old parent-child patterns (nagging, cleaning up after him, managing his life)?
* **Financial Stress:** Will having him there increase your utility bills, grocery costs, or financial burden?
* **Enabling vs. Helping:** Is he moving back due to a genuine temporary crisis (job loss, breakup, medical issue), or is he avoiding independence and adult responsibility?

---

### 2. Understand his circumstances
The nature of his request matters:
* **Is this a temporary emergency?** (e.g., *“I have 3 months between leases”* or *“I just lost my job and need 60 days to find work.”*)
* **Is it open-ended?** (e.g., *“Rent is too high, so I want to save money indefinitely.”*)
* **Does he have a realistic plan?** Does he know what steps he needs to take to move back out, or is he looking for a safety net without an exit strategy?

---

### 3. Your Options

You essentially have three paths to choose from:

#### Option A: Say "No" (with love and alternative support)
If your gut tells you that living together will damage your mental health, finances, or your relationship with him, it is completely valid to say no. 

* **How to frame it:** Focus on your relationship and his long-term independence rather than his shortcomings.
* **Sample wording:**  
  > *"I love you and want to support you, but living together isn't going to work for me at this stage in my life. I value our adult relationship and my own space. Let’s sit down and brainstorm other ways I can help you figure out your next steps."*
* **Alternative ways to help:** Offer to help him look for a cheaper apartment, find a roommate, pay for a month of groceries, or help polish his resume.

#### Option B: Say "Yes, but with strict conditions and a timeline"
If you are willing to let him stay temporarily, it must be on **adult-to-adult terms**, not parent-to-child terms. Treat it like a formal agreement:

1. **A Firm End Date:** Agree on a specific move-out date from day one (e.g., 3 months, 6 months).
2. **Financial Contribution:** Even a small amount of rent or paying for his own groceries/utilities creates healthy accountability.
3. **Chore Division:** He must manage his own laundry, clean up after himself, and contribute to shared chores.
4. **House Rules & Respect:** Clarify expectations around guests, quiet hours, and communication.
5. **Regular Check-ins:** A monthly conversation to check progress on his savings, job search, or housing search.

#### Option C: A Short-Term Trial
If you aren't sure, offer a very short period (e.g., 30 to 60 days) to re-evaluate. 

---

### A Good Next Step
Before giving him an answer, give yourself permission to pause. You can tell him:  
> *"I hear that you'd like to move back, but I need some time to think about whether that's workable for me and what that would look like. Let's talk about it this weekend."*
