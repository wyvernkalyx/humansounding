**My immediate reaction:** I love the radical energy, but as a strategy, "slashing and waiting for explosions" works better for server infrastructure (Chaos Engineering) than for human teams. 

If you do this blindly, things **will** break, but they won't break cleanly. They will break silently first—through misalignment, isolation, and resentment—before they break visibly.

Here is a breakdown of what will happen, the blind spots to watch out for, and a slightly smarter way to execute the same goal.

---

### What will go right
1. **Instant relief:** Your team will immediately feel a surge of agency and focus time.
2. **Exposing the theater:** Status-update meetings and "FYI" round-robins will die instantly, and nobody will miss them.
3. **Forced documentation:** People will be forced to write down proposals, decisions, and updates, creating a better paper trail.

---

### What will quietly break (The Hidden Costs)
1. **The Slack/Teams Explosion:** If you cut meetings without establishing an async communication culture, those meetings just turn into chaotic, 80-message Slack threads where decisions take three days instead of 15 minutes.
2. **Junior and New Team Members Suffer Most:** Senior people thrive without meetings because they already have the network and context to get things done. Junior or newly hired folks rely on "ambient context" from meetings; without it, they stall.
3. **Silent Drift:** Teams won't crash dramatically. Instead, two teams will spend three weeks building features that don't integrate properly because they assumed they were aligned.
4. **Relational Erosion:** In remote/hybrid setups, meetings are often the *only* human contact people have. Cutting them by 50% can turn work purely transactional, which tanks morale and retention over a full quarter.

---

### A Better Way: Surgical Aggression
Instead of cutting 50% at random, apply ruthless rules that achieve the 50% reduction while protecting context:

#### 1. Kill meetings by category, not quota
* **Information Sharing (Status syncs, project updates):** Cut 100%. Replace with weekly async written bullets or 3-minute screen recordings (Loom).
* **Connection (1:1s, mentorship):** Keep 100%. Never cut 1:1s; they are your early-warning radar.
* **Decisions & Brainstorms:** Keep, but put them on a diet.

#### 2. The 50% Time Rule (Shorter, not fewer)
Change default calendar settings across the team:
* 60-minute meetings become **30 minutes**.
* 30-minute meetings become **15 minutes**.
* *Result:* You cut meeting *time* by 50% without losing alignment touchpoints.

#### 3. Implement "Document or Cancel"
Require a 1-page memo or agenda sent 24 hours prior for any decision meeting. If there is no doc, the meeting is automatically deleted. (This alone kills ~30% of unnecessary meetings).

#### 4. Declare "No-Meeting Days"
Block Tuesday and Thursday entirely. It forces all meetings to compete for space on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, naturally squeezing out the fluff.

---

### The Verdict
**Run the experiment, but announce the hypothesis.** Tell the team: *"We are running a 60-day experiment to reclaim focus time. We are halving meeting volume, shifting to written updates, and we will retro on what we missed at the end of the month."*

That way, when something breaks, they view it as part of the test rather than leadership going rogue.
