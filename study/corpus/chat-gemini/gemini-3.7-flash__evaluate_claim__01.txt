The short answer is **no, "never" is too extreme.** 

While there is a very strong **mathematical argument** for keeping a low-interest mortgage, there is also a compelling **psychological and risk-management argument** for paying it off. 

Whether you should pay it off depends on your numbers, your discipline, and your personality. Here is a breakdown of both sides so you can decide what makes sense for you.

---

### The Argument for NOT Paying It Off (The Math)

The person who gave you this advice was looking purely at the numbers and the concept of **opportunity cost**.

1. **You Can Earn More Elsewhere (Arbitrage):**
   If your mortgage rate is fixed at 3%, and you have extra cash, putting that cash into a High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA), Certificate of Deposit (CD), or Treasury bills currently yields around 4% to 5%. Investing in a broad-market index fund (like the S&P 500) has historically returned an average of 7% to 10% annually over long periods. 
   * *The math:* Earning 5% to 8% beats "saving" 3%.

2. **Inflation Works in Your Favor:**
   With a fixed-rate mortgage, your monthly payment never increases, but inflation makes future dollars worth less. You are effectively paying back your loan with "cheaper" money over time, while your home value and (presumably) income rise.

3. **Liquidity and Flexibility:**
   Money paid toward a mortgage is locked inside the walls of your house (home equity). To get it out in an emergency, you have to sell the house or qualify for a loan (like a HELOC), which can be difficult if you lose your job. Keeping extra money in liquid savings or brokerage accounts gives you more flexibility.

---

### The Argument FOR Paying It Off (Psychology and Risk)

Personal finance is not just math; it is behavior and risk management.

1. **Guaranteed, Risk-Free Return:**
   Paying off a 3% mortgage gives you a guaranteed 3% return on your money, free of state and federal income taxes. The stock market might average 8%, but it can also drop 20% in a single year. Debt payoff is certain.

2. **Peace of Mind:**
   Owing nothing on the roof over your head provides a level of emotional freedom and security that a spreadsheet cannot measure. For many people, eliminating debt significantly reduces life stress.

3. **Reduced Monthly Overhead:**
   Without a mortgage payment, your required cost of living drops dramatically. This makes career changes, starting a business, or retiring early much easier, because you need significantly less monthly income to survive.

4. **The Reality of Human Behavior:**
   The math of keeping a low-interest mortgage only works if you **actually invest the difference**. If you don't pay extra on your mortgage and instead spend that money on vacations, dining out, or lifestyle upgrades, you would have been far better off paying down the house.

---

### A Good Framework to Decide

Before putting a single extra dollar toward a low-interest mortgage, you should ensure you have followed these steps first:

1. **Pay off high-interest debt** (credit cards, personal loans, high-rate auto loans).
2. **Build an emergency fund** (3 to 6 months of living expenses in a high-yield account).
3. **Capture employer retirement matches** (e.g., getting your full 401(k) match).
4. **Max out tax-advantaged accounts** (IRAs, HSAs, 401(k)s).

### The Verdict

* **Keep the mortgage** if you are disciplined, comfortable with market fluctuations, actively investing your extra cash, and want to maximize total net worth over the long run.
* **Pay off the mortgage** if you are nearing retirement, hate the feeling of debt, or value guaranteed security over maximizing investment returns.
