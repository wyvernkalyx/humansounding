The short answer is **no, "never" is too absolute.** 

However, from a purely mathematical perspective, the advice is very strong. When you have a low-interest mortgage (generally considered 4% or lower), there are powerful financial reasons to keep it—but personal finance is as much about **psychology and risk** as it is about math.

Here is a breakdown of why people say to never pay it off, followed by why you still might want to.

---

### The Mathematical Case: Why You Shouldn’t Pay It Off Early

If you have a 3% mortgage rate, putting extra money toward the principal only earns you an effective **3% return on investment** (by avoiding that interest). 

Here is why keeping the mortgage makes mathematical sense:

1. **The "Risk-Free" Arbitrage:**
   Currently, High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSAs), Certificates of Deposit (CDs), and U.S. Treasury bills pay between **4% and 5%**. If you put your extra cash into a Treasury bond at 4.5% instead of paying down a 3% mortgage, you make a guaranteed profit on the spread—even after paying taxes on the interest.
2. **Long-Term Market Returns:**
   Historically, broad stock market indexes (like the S&P 500) average roughly **7% to 10%** annual returns over long periods. Over 20 or 30 years, investing extra money in an index fund will almost certainly yield far more wealth than paying off a low-rate debt.
3. **Inflation Works in Your Favor:**
   With a fixed-rate mortgage, your monthly payment never changes. As inflation rises over the next 10 to 20 years, your salary will likely increase, but your mortgage payment stays the same. In real terms, **your debt becomes cheaper every year**.
4. **Liquidity:**
   Money paid into a house is locked in illiquid home equity. If you lose your job or face an emergency, you cannot easily access that money without selling the house or qualifying for a loan (which is hard to do without income). Money in a brokerage or savings account gives you safety and flexibility.

---

### The Psychological & Strategic Case: Why You Might Pay It Off Anyway

Math assumes humans are emotionless spreadsheets. In reality, there are very valid reasons to pay off a low-interest mortgage:

1. **Guaranteed Peace of Mind:**
   Living 100% debt-free provides an emotional and psychological benefit that cannot be measured on a balance sheet. Eliminating your largest monthly expense reduces stress significantly.
2. **Lowering Fixed Monthly Overhead (Retirement):**
   If you are nearing retirement, eliminating the mortgage payment means you need significantly less monthly income to survive. This lowers your required portfolio withdrawal rate and protects you against market downturns ("sequence of returns risk").
3. **Behavioral Reality (The "Spend It" Trap):**
   The mathematical argument only works **if you actually invest the difference**. If you don't pay down the mortgage and instead spend that extra money on vacations, dining out, or lifestyle creep, you would have been better off paying down the house.
4. **Zero Risk:**
   While the stock market *usually* beats 3% over the long run, it is volatile. Paying off your mortgage is a **guaranteed, risk-free return**.

---

### How to Decide What's Right for You

Before putting an extra dollar toward a low-interest mortgage, make sure you have:
1. Paid off all high-interest debt (credit cards, personal loans, high-rate auto loans).
2. Built a 3- to 6-month emergency fund in cash.
3. Maxed out any employer 401(k) match (that’s a 100% immediate return).
4. Funded other tax-advantaged accounts (Roth IRA, HSA).

**The Bottom Line:**
If your goal is **maximum net worth**, keeping a low-interest mortgage and investing your extra cash is the better move. 
If your goal is **maximum security and peace of mind**, paying off the house early is a perfectly valid personal choice.
