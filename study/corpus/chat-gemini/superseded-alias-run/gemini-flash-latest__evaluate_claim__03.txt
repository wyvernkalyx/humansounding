**Yes, it is entirely true.** You should **never** put a hot laptop or laptop battery in the fridge (or freezer). 

While it might seem logical to cool down an overheating device as quickly as possible, putting electronics in the fridge creates severe risks:

---

### 1. Condensation (The Biggest Danger)
Refrigerators are cold and humid environments. When you place a hot object into a cold, moist environment, **condensation immediately forms**—not just on the outside of the laptop, but deep inside the components. 
* Liquid water will condense directly onto the motherboard, battery terminals, and delicate circuitry.
* Once the laptop is turned back on (or even while the battery holds a charge), this moisture will cause **short circuits, corrosion, and permanent hardware failure**.

### 2. Thermal Shock
Materials expand when heated and contract when cooled. If a hot battery or laptop is rapidly exposed to near-freezing temperatures, the materials contract unevenly and too fast. This "thermal shock" can:
* Crack delicate internal micro-soldering.
* Warp the battery casing.
* Damage the internal chemical layers of the lithium-ion battery.

### 3. Lithium-Ion Battery Chemistry
Lithium-ion batteries hate extreme heat, but they also perform poorly in extreme cold:
* Rapid chilling alters the internal resistance of the battery.
* Sub-freezing or near-freezing temperatures can cause internal stress, permanently reducing the battery’s capacity and overall lifespan.

---

### What You Should Do Instead
If your laptop or battery is dangerously hot, follow these safe steps:

1. **Turn it off immediately:** Shut down the laptop to stop the processor and battery from generating more heat.
2. **Unplug the charger:** Charging a battery generates a significant amount of heat.
3. **Elevate it:** Place the laptop on a hard, flat surface (not a bed or blanket) and prop up the back feet with a book or bottle cap to increase airflow underneath.
4. **Use a standard room fan:** Point an ordinary desk fan at the laptop. Room-temperature air moving quickly is the fastest and safest way to cool it down without risking condensation.
5. **Clean the vents:** Once cool, use a can of compressed air to blow accumulated dust out of the cooling vents and fans.
