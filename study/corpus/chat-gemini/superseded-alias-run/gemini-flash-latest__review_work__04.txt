Generally, **it is better to rephrase it** before sending. 

While the tone is honest and friendly, telling a client "things have been crazy" can unintentionally make you or your business look disorganized, overwhelmed, or like this client wasn't a priority.

Here are a few better ways to say it, depending on your relationship with the client:

---

### 1. Professional & Polished (Best for most clients)
> *"Thank you for your patience. I apologize for the delay in getting back to you."*

### 2. Friendly & Casual (If you have an established, warm relationship)
> *"Thanks for your patience! It’s been an unusually busy week on our end, but I’m glad to get back to you now."*

### 3. Direct & Action-Oriented (Skip the excuses, focus on the work)
> *"Thank you for waiting—here is [the update / the deliverable / the information you requested]."*

---

### Pro-Tip:
Switching from **"Sorry for the delay"** to **"Thank you for your patience"** shifts the psychology of the email:
* "Sorry for the delay" focuses on **your mistake**.
* "Thank you for your patience" compliments **their good behavior**.
