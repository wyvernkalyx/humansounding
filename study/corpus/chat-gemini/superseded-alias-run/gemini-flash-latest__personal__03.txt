It is completely normal to reach a milestone like eleven years and feel this exact tension. 

The line between **stability** and **being stuck** doesn't depend on the number of years you’ve been there; it depends on **growth, intent, and how the job makes you feel.**

Here is a framework to help you figure out which side of the line you are on:

---

### 1. It is **Stability** if:
* **You are choosing to stay for a clear reason:** That reason might be great compensation, incredible work-life balance, low stress, or flexibility that allows you to focus on your family, hobbies, or health.
* **You are still evolving:** Even in the same company or role, your responsibilities have adapted, your judgment is sharper, and you are trusted with higher-level decisions.
* **Work enables your life:** You don’t need your job to be your entire identity because it provides the security that fuels the rest of your life.
* **You could leave if you wanted to:** You feel confident that your skills are still relevant, your network is intact, and you *could* land another job—you just prefer the setup you have.

### 2. You are **Stuck** if:
* **You are staying out of fear or inertia:** You stay because the thought of updating a resume, interviewing, or starting over feels terrifying, not because you love where you are.
* **You have "one year of experience repeated eleven times":** Your day-to-day hasn't changed in years, you run on pure autopilot, and your skills are slowly becoming outdated.
* **Apathy or cynicism has set in:** You feel chronically bored, resentful, or disengaged. You’ve mentally "checked out," but your body still shows up.
* **Your world has gotten smaller:** The comfort zone of your current role has made you doubt your ability to thrive anywhere else.

---

### Three Diagnostic Questions to Ask Yourself

1. **"Am I resting, or am I hiding?"**  
   Stability feels like a solid foundation you stand on; being stuck feels like a shelter you’re afraid to leave.
2. **"If a recruiter offered me a lateral move tomorrow with the exact same pay and zero risk of failure, would I take it?"**  
   If your immediate gut reaction is *“Yes, get me out of here,”* you are stuck. If your reaction is *“No, I really like my current setup,”* you are enjoying stability.
3. **"Has this job been stability for 8 years, and 'stuck' for the last 3?"**  
   It doesn't have to be one or the other for the entire decade. Many people thrive in stability for a long time until, almost without noticing, the stability curdles into stagnation.

---

### What to do next (without making any sudden moves)

* **Don't judge yourself:** Staying somewhere for 11 years is a testament to loyalty, reliability, and competence. You haven't "wasted time"; you've built deep domain knowledge.
* **Dip a toe outside the bubble:** You don't need to quit tomorrow. Just update your LinkedIn, look at job postings in your industry to see what looks interesting, or have a coffee chat with someone outside your company. 
* **Gauge your curiosity:** When you look at other possibilities, do you feel a spark of excitement? If you do, that’s your answer.
