**It is not fine. It needs to be fixed.**

In fact, that exact phrase is widely considered one of the most generic, overused clichés in resume writing. Recruiters see it hundreds of times a week, and it tells them almost nothing about you.

---

### Why it doesn't work:
1. **"Results-driven":** This is an empty buzzword. No one puts *"activity-driven"* or *"uninterested in results"* on a resume. Show your results through metrics, don't just state that you care about them.
2. **"Professional":** Too vague. Are you a Software Engineer, a Supply Chain Manager, an Accountant, or an HR Director?
3. **"10+ years of experience":** Years of experience are fine to mention, but without context (industry, key achievements, specific domain expertise), it adds zero value.

---

### The Formula for a High-Impact Summary
A great summary should be **2–3 punchy sentences** following this structure:
> **[Specific Job Title]** with **[X years]** of experience in **[Specialty / Industry]**. Proven track record of **[1–2 biggest, quantifiable achievements]**. Skilled in **[Top 2–3 hard skills/tools relevant to the target role]**.

---

### Before vs. After Examples

#### Example 1: Sales / Business Development
* **Bad:** *Results-driven professional with 10+ years of experience.*
* **Good:** *Senior B2B SaaS Account Executive with 10+ years of experience managing enterprise sales cycles. Consistently exceeded quotas by an average of 135%, generating over $18M in net-new ARR across FinTech and Healthcare sectors.*

#### Example 2: Project / Operations Management
* **Bad:** *Results-driven professional with 10+ years of experience.*
* **Good:** *Operations Manager with 10+ years of experience optimizing supply chains for global retail brands. Led cross-functional teams of 40+ to cut operational overhead by 22% while maintaining a 99.4% on-time delivery rate.*
