Your coworker is **partially right, but mostly wrong**. 

The misconception comes from the early hype: standing desks were marketed as magic tools that would help you lose weight, live forever, and replace exercise. **They don't do that.**

However, claiming they "don't do anything" ignores a large body of ergonomic and physiological research. Here is what the science actually says:

---

### Where your coworker is **RIGHT** (The Myths)
1. **They won’t make you lose weight:** Standing burns only about **8 to 10 extra calories per hour** compared to sitting. Over an entire workday, that’s about the equivalent of half an apple. 
2. **Standing all day is also bad for you:** Prolonged static standing brings its own health risks, including varicose veins, joint pain, foot fatigue, and lower-back strain.
3. **They don't replace exercise:** Using a standing desk does not compensate for a lack of cardiovascular exercise or strength training.

---

### Where your coworker is **WRONG** (The Real Benefits)
1. **Reduces back and neck pain:** This is the most well-documented benefit. Multiple studies (including the CDC's "Take-a-Stand Project") found that alternating between sitting and standing reduced upper back and neck pain by up to **54%**.
2. **Better blood sugar regulation:** Standing after a meal reduces blood sugar spikes. A study published in the *British Journal of Sports Medicine* showed that alternating sitting and standing every 30 minutes reduced post-meal glucose spikes by up to 34% compared to continuous sitting.
3. **Boosts energy and reduces fatigue:** Studies consistently show that workers who switch between sitting and standing report less afternoon fatigue, improved mood, and better focus compared to those who sit all day.
4. **Breaks up sedentary time:** Prolonged unbroken sitting is linked to cardiovascular disease. The act of simply changing postures shifts spinal loads and stimulates blood flow.

---

### The Verdict: It’s not a *standing* desk, it’s an *alternating* desk
The health benefit does not come from standing still for 8 hours—it comes from **posture variation**. 

If you want to get actual benefits from a standing desk, ergonomics experts recommend the **"Sit-Stand" approach**:
* **Stand for 15–30 minutes out of every hour**, and sit for the rest.
* Always wear supportive shoes or use an anti-fatigue mat.
* Ensure proper ergonomics (screen at eye level, elbows at a 90-degree angle).
