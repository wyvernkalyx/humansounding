How you answer depends entirely on **how solid your new offer is** and **what you want to happen next**. 

Here is how to handle the situation based on your current status:

---

### Scenario 1: The offer is signed, sealed, and background check is done (You are ready to leave)
If you are 100% leaving, **do not lie**. Lying today and handing in your two weeks' notice tomorrow makes you look dishonest and burns a bridge. Instead, rip off the band-aid and turn this into your resignation conversation.

* **What to say:**  
  *"I wasn't aggressively looking, but an exceptional opportunity came to me that aligns with my long-term career goals. I actually just accepted an offer, and I was planning to schedule time with you today/tomorrow to discuss a smooth transition plan."*

---

### Scenario 2: You have an offer, but it’s NOT finalized yet (Pending background check, written contract, or you're still deciding)
**Never admit you are leaving until you have a signed, finalized agreement.** If you admit it too early, you risk being fired before your new job is secured—especially since it’s with a **competitor** (many companies will walk you out the door immediately for security/IP reasons).

* **What to say (The Pivot):**  
  *"Like most professionals, I occasionally get reached out to by recruiters, but right now my focus is on delivering [Current Project/Goal]. Did something specific prompt that question?"*
* **Why this works:** It acknowledges reality (recruiters reach out) without admitting you are actively interviewing, and it immediately throws the ball back into their court to see why they’re asking.

---

### Scenario 3: You want to stay at your current job IF they match/beat the competitor's offer
If you want to use this as leverage for a promotion or raise, you can be slightly more transparent, but keep it professional.

* **What to say:**  
  *"I’ve been approached by a recruiter recently with a very competitive opportunity. While I love working here and value our team, the compensation/role they offered made me pause. I’d love to have an open conversation with you about my future and growth path here."*

---

### ⚠️ 2 Crucial Warnings for Your Situation:

1. **Competitor Risk (Immediate Escort):**  
   Because you are going to a *competitor*, be prepared for the possibility that your current company will revoke your system access and ask you to leave **immediately** once they find out. Clear any personal files/contacts from your work devices *today* (do not take proprietary company data).
2. **Never give a flat "No" if you are leaving soon:**  
   If you say, *"No, absolutely not, I love it here!"* and then resign three days later, your boss will remember the lie. Use a deflecting answer (Scenario 2) instead of a direct lie.
