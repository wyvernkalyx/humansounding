You should read that claim with a healthy dose of **strategic skepticism**. 

"Cuts onboarding time by 50%" is one of the most common sales tropes in B2B tech. While it *might* be possible, it is usually a best-case marketing aggregate, not an out-of-the-box guarantee for your specific business.

Here is how you should break down and evaluate that claim:

---

### 1. Clarify the Definition of "Time"
Vendors often conflate two very different metrics:
* **Active Working Hours (Effort):** The time a human spends manually typing data, sending emails, or provisioning accounts (e.g., from 4 hours of manual data entry down to 2 hours).
* **Calendar Duration (Elapsed Time):** The time it takes from Day 1 to a user/employee being fully functional (e.g., from 30 days down to 15 days).

*A tool can easily reduce manual admin effort by 50%, but that rarely translates to cutting total elapsed onboarding time in half, because people still learn, read, and adapt at human speeds.*

### 2. Identify the Vendor’s "Baseline"
Ask where this 50% number came from:
* **From chaos to order:** Did their reference customer go from *paper forms, spreadsheets, and manual emails* to automated software? If so, anyone would see a 50% drop. If you already have a modern stack, your gains will be much smaller.
* **Selection bias:** Is this stat from a cherry-picked case study of their single best-performing enterprise client, or an average across hundreds of typical users?

### 3. Account for Bottlenecks Software Can’t Fix
Software only automates the **transactional** parts of onboarding. It does not fix:
* Background checks, legal approvals, or compliance reviews.
* The time it takes a human to internalize complex training or culture.
* Waiting on third parties (e.g., IT hardware shipping, client-side data migrations).
* Internal cross-functional delays (e.g., waiting for manager approvals).

If your onboarding takes 30 days, but only 2 days of that is software-related admin, cutting that software time in half saves you **1 day**, not 15.

---

### Questions to Ask the Vendor to Test the Claim

1. **"Can you break down the 50% reduction by phase?"**  
   *(Force them to show where the savings actually occur: Admin/IT setup? Training? Form completion?)*
2. **"What did the customer’s previous process look like to achieve that?"**  
   *(Determine if their baseline matches your current reality.)*
3. **"What was the customer’s internal time investment to set up the tool to get those results?"**  
   *(If you save 100 hours a year on onboarding, but it takes 300 hours of technical setup and maintenance, you're at a net loss.)*
4. **"Can I speak with a customer in our industry with a similar team size who saw this result?"**  
   *(If they can't provide a reference, the stat is marketing fluff.)*

---

### The Bottom Line
Read "cuts onboarding time in half" as: **"We automate several manual steps in the process."** 

Treat it as an aspirational upper bound, and assume your real-world calendar time savings will likely be closer to **10% to 20%** once human behavior and implementation friction are factored in.
