The best way to decline is to **make the refusal about your personal financial rules, not about the viability of her idea.** 

Critiquing a friend’s business idea often makes them defensive and damages the friendship. By using a clear personal boundary, you remove the business from the debate entirely.

Here are a few ways to handle it, depending on your communication style:

---

### Option 1: The "Friends & Money" Rule (Safest & Most Effective)
This is the cleanest approach because it is completely neutral and leaves no room for negotiation.

> *"I’m so proud of you for taking this leap and pursuing this! However, I have a strict personal rule never to mix investments or business with close friendships—I value our relationship way too much to ever let money complicate it. I won't be able to invest, but I’m really rooting for you."*

---

### Option 2: The "Financial Limits / Fixed Strategy" Approach
If she knows you invest elsewhere, frame it around your strict investment criteria or current budget.

> *"Thank you for thinking of me and sharing your vision. Right now, all of my extra capital is tied up in [long-term savings / index funds / other commitments], and I'm not in a position to take on angel investing or high-risk ventures. I won't be able to participate financially, but I’m wishing you the absolute best with the launch."*

---

### Option 3: If she specifically asks, *"What do you think of the idea?"*
If she genuinely asks for your honest feedback, focus on **market challenges and risks**, not her ability to execute. Use the "compliment sandwich":

> *"I love your drive and how passionate you are about this. My main hesitation from an investor standpoint is [e.g., how competitive the space is / the high customer acquisition costs / how long it might take to reach profitability]. Because of those market risks, it’s not an investment I feel comfortable making, but I know how hard you're working on it."*

---

### Key Rules to Remember:

1. **Be clear and direct:** Avoid saying things like *"Maybe in the next round"* or *"I don't have the money right now,"* as this invites her to ask again in six months.
2. **Offer non-monetary support:** If you want to show you still care, offer low-stakes help:
   * *"I can't invest, but I'd love to share your launch on social media."*
   * *"I’m happy to be a sounding board if you ever want to practice your pitch."*
3. **Don't over-explain:** Keep your "no" short. The more reasons you give, the more points she will try to counter or solve.
