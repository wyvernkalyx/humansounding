How you handle this depends entirely on **where you are in the decision process** and **whether the offer is finalized in writing**. 

Here are the three main scenarios and exact scripts for each:

---

### Scenario 1: You are definitely taking the offer and have signed
If you already have a signed, finalized offer in hand with a clear start date, use this moment to transition directly into your resignation.

> **What to say:**  
> *"I actually wasn't actively looking, but an opportunity was presented to me that aligns with my next career steps, and I recently decided to accept it. I was planning to set up a formal meeting with you tomorrow, but since you brought it up, I’d like to give my two weeks' notice."*

---

### Scenario 2: You want to buy 24–48 hours (offer not signed yet, or still deciding)
Do **not** lie flatly (saying *"No, absolutely not"*) if you might resign 48 hours later—it burns the bridge unnecessarily. Instead, acknowledge external interest without confirming an imminent departure.

> **What to say:**  
> *"I haven't been aggressively applying everywhere, but given the current market, I’ve had recruiters reach out and I have had a conversation to see what’s out there. But why do you ask? Is there something specific on your mind regarding my role here?"*

*Why this works:* It shifts the focus back to the boss so you can find out *why* they asked (e.g., Did a reference check leak? Did your performance slip? Are they planning re-orgs?).

---

### Scenario 3: You want to stay if your boss matches/beats the offer
If you want to stay, frame it around career growth and market value rather than an ultimatum.

> **What to say:**  
> *"I was recently approached by another company with an opportunity that offers significantly higher compensation and leadership scope. I really enjoy my team and the work here, but the offer is substantial enough that I have to consider it. I’d love to know what my growth path looks like here before I make a final decision."*

---

### 3 Critical Things to Keep in Mind (Especially with Competitors):

1. **Be prepared to be walked out immediately:**  
   When an employee resigns to join a direct competitor, it is standard corporate policy to revoke system access and escort the employee out the same day for data security reasons. Make sure you don’t have personal files or sensitive information on your work machine *before* you have this conversation.
2. **Do not name the competitor right away:**  
   You are under no obligation to reveal the company's name in this initial conversation. If asked, you can say:  
   *"Out of respect for their process, I’d prefer to keep the company name confidential until everything is finalized."*
3. **Check your contracts:**  
   Double-check your offer letter, employee handbook, or any agreements you signed regarding non-competes, non-solicitation, or mandatory notice periods before confirming you're going to a direct rival.
