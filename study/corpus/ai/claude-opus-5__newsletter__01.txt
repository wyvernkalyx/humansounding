**Subject: Your 5 a.m. routine is failing you (and that's fine)**

---

Hey there,

Every January, a fresh wave of people decide they're going to become a Morning Person. They buy the journal. They set the alarm for 5:15. They last eleven days.

I've been that person more times than I'd like to admit. What finally worked wasn't more discipline — it was a smaller, smarter design. Here's what actually sticks.

**1. Start with the feeling, not the schedule**

Before you copy a CEO's 17-step routine, ask: how do I want the first hour to feel? Calm? Alert? Unhurried? Purposeful?

Someone who wants *calm* needs quiet and slack in the schedule. Someone who wants *momentum* needs movement and a clear first task. These are completely different mornings. Borrowing a stranger's routine is how you end up doing cold plunges you secretly hate.

**2. Make version one embarrassingly small**

The most common failure isn't laziness — it's ambition. A 90-minute routine has 90 minutes of chances to collapse.

Pick one keystone action and shrink it until it's almost silly:
- Not "meditate 20 minutes" → sit and breathe for 90 seconds
- Not "write morning pages" → write three sentences
- Not "work out" → put on the shoes and stretch

You're not trying to transform your life this week. You're trying to prove to yourself that you're someone who does the thing. Volume comes later, and it comes easily once the identity is in place.

**3. Anchor it to something that already happens**

Your brain resists new sequences but loves extending old ones. Attach your new habit to an existing, unmissable one.

*After I pour my coffee, I write three sentences.*
*After I brush my teeth, I do 90 seconds of stretching.*

Specific trigger, specific action. "In the morning, I'll journal" is a wish. The formula above is a plan.

**4. Your morning routine starts the night before**

This is the one nobody wants to hear. A good morning is mostly the byproduct of a decent bedtime and five minutes of prep.

Lay out the clothes. Fill the water bottle. Put the book on the pillow and the phone across the room. Write tomorrow's first task on a sticky note so you don't wake up negotiating with yourself.

Every decision you remove from 6 a.m. you is a decision that can't go wrong.

**5. Build in a bad-day version**

Perfect routines break on the first sick kid, late flight, or terrible night's sleep. Then you feel like a failure and quit.

So decide now: what's the two-minute version you do when everything is on fire? Maybe it's just drinking a glass of water by the window. Keep the chain intact, even at 5% strength.

The rule that matters: **never miss twice.** One skipped day is life. Two is a new pattern.

**This week's experiment**

Choose one habit. Shrink it to under two minutes. Anchor it to something you already do. Run it for seven days and don't add anything else.

Then write back and tell me how it went — I read every reply.

Talk soon,
Jamie

*P.S. If you tried and bailed by Wednesday, you didn't fail. Your version one was too big. Cut it in half and start again Monday.*
