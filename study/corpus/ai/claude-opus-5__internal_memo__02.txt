**MEMORANDUM**

**To:** All Staff
**From:** [Name], [Title]
**Date:** [Date]
**Subject:** Moving to a Three-Day In-Office Schedule Beginning [Month Day]

---

I'm writing to share an update on our working arrangements. Starting **Monday, [Month Day]**, we will move to a hybrid schedule in which all staff based near a company office are expected on site **three days per week**.

**The schedule**

Our anchor days will be **Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday**. We chose fixed anchor days rather than a "pick any three" model because the value of being together depends on actually overlapping — a flexible count too often results in half-empty floors and meetings that are hybrid anyway.

Monday and Friday remain remote by default. Teams that need a different pattern for operational reasons should raise it with their director, who can approve alternatives at the team level.

**Why we're making this change**

Over the past [period], we've learned a great deal about what works well remotely and what doesn't. Focused individual work, documentation, and routine check-ins have gone smoothly. What has become harder is the less structured work: early-stage problem solving, cross-team coordination, informal feedback, and — importantly — the way newer colleagues learn by observation and proximity. Onboarding surveys and exit conversations have both pointed clearly in this direction.

We also want to be candid that this is a judgment call, not a conclusion drawn from perfect data. We think the balance of evidence favors regular, predictable time together, and we'd rather commit to a clear arrangement than keep adjusting it every quarter.

**Exceptions and accommodations**

Some colleagues have formal remote agreements, live outside a commutable distance, or have accommodations in place for health, disability, or caregiving reasons. Those arrangements stand and will be honored. If your situation isn't covered and this change creates a genuine hardship, please contact your manager or [HR contact] before [date]. We will handle these conversations individually and confidentially.

**What we're doing to make the days worth the commute**

Being in the office should feel different from being at home, not identical to it with a longer commute. Between now and [Month Day] we will:

- Reconfigure [Floor/Area] to add collaboration space and more bookable focus rooms, since open-plan calls have been a persistent complaint.
- Expand desk availability so no one arrives without somewhere to sit; booking opens [date] via [system].
- Provide [lunch/coffee/subsidy] on anchor days and [transit benefit / parking update].
- Ask managers to schedule team meetings, workshops, planning sessions, and 1:1s on anchor days where possible, and to protect Monday and Friday for focused work.

If there's something specific that would make in-office days more productive for your team, tell us — [feedback channel] is open.

**A word on how we'll manage this**

Managers will treat attendance as a shared expectation, not a tracking exercise. Life happens: illness, appointments, a delayed train. Talk to your manager, adjust, and move on. What we're asking for is a genuine, consistent effort, not perfect compliance.

We'll review how this is working at the [quarter/month] mark and share what we learn, including anything we decide to change.

I know this news will land differently for different people, and I'd rather hear your concerns than not. I'll host an open Q&A on [date, time, location/link], and my door is open before then.

Thank you for your flexibility and for the work you continue to do.
