# The Compound Effect

**Issue #14 — Money in Your Twenties: The Decade That Does the Heavy Lifting**

---

Hey there,

Let's start with the uncomfortable math.

If you invest $200 a month starting at 25 and stop entirely at 35 — just ten years of contributions, $24,000 total — you'd have roughly $340,000 by 65, assuming a 7% average return. If you wait until 35 and invest that same $200 every single month until 65, contributing $72,000, you'd end up with about $245,000.

Three times the money in, less money out. That's not a trick. That's just time doing what time does.

Your twenties aren't about earning a lot. Most of us don't. They're about giving your money the longest possible runway.

## Four things that actually move the needle

**1. Build a small buffer before you build anything else.**
Not six months of expenses — that's an intimidating target when rent eats half your paycheck. Start with $1,000. That single number converts a flat tire from a crisis into an annoyance, and it stops you from reaching for a credit card at 24% APR. Once you're there, work toward one month of expenses, then three.

**2. Take the free money. Immediately.**
If your employer matches retirement contributions and you're not contributing enough to get the full match, you are declining part of your salary. A 50% match on 6% of a $55,000 salary is $1,650 a year you're leaving behind. Log in this week. It takes eleven minutes.

**3. Kill high-interest debt with something close to violence.**
Credit card debt compounds against you at rates no investment reliably beats. Pay minimums on everything, then throw every spare dollar at the highest interest rate. Student loans at 4–6% are a different animal — steady payments are fine there. Don't let low-interest debt scare you into paralysis while a 22% balance quietly grows.

**4. Watch the fixed costs, not the coffee.**
The internet loves to blame lattes. But a $300/month difference in rent is $3,600 a year — roughly 500 lattes. Your rent, car payment, and subscriptions determine your financial life far more than any daily habit. Audit them once a year. Negotiate what you can. Cancel what you forgot about.

## The trap nobody warns you about

Lifestyle creep. You get a raise, and within two months your spending has quietly absorbed all of it. You feel exactly as broke as before, just with nicer furniture.

The fix is simple, if not easy: when income rises, commit half of the increase to savings or investments *before* it hits your checking account. Automate it. You'll never miss money you don't see.

## This week's one thing

Open your banking app and list every recurring charge. Every one. Most people find $40–80 a month they'd genuinely forgotten about.

Cancel two of them. Redirect that money to a savings account you don't carry a card for.

That's it. That's the whole assignment.

---

Your twenties are messy, underpaid, and frequently chaotic. You don't need to optimize everything. You just need to avoid the big mistakes and start early — because starting early is the only advantage that gets more valuable the less money you have.

See you in two weeks.
