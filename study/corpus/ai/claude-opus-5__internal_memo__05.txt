**MEMORANDUM**

**To:** All Employees
**From:** Dana Whitfield, Vice President, People Operations
**Date:** January 8
**Subject:** Changes to the Annual Review Process, Effective This Cycle

---

Beginning with the current cycle, we are making several changes to how we conduct annual performance reviews. These changes come directly from feedback gathered in last year's engagement survey and from a working group of managers and individual contributors across all five business units. Two themes came through clearly: reviews felt disconnected from day-to-day work, and the process took too long to produce too little useful information.

**What's changing**

**1. A shorter, more focused review form.** The self-assessment and manager assessment have been reduced from fourteen questions to five. Each focuses on outcomes, collaboration, and growth rather than restating job duties. The form is now available in Workday under "My Performance."

**2. Goals replace competency checklists.** Instead of rating employees against a generic competency matrix, reviews will assess progress against the three to five goals set with your manager at the start of the year. If your goals shifted mid-year — as many did — document the change in the "Context" field so your review reflects reality.

**3. No forced distribution.** We are eliminating the requirement that managers assign a fixed percentage of ratings to each performance level. Ratings will instead be reviewed in calibration sessions, where managers within a department discuss their assessments together to ensure consistency. Calibration is about fairness, not quotas.

**4. A simplified rating scale.** The five-point scale is replaced with four levels: *Developing, Meeting, Exceeding,* and *Exceptional*. "Meeting" reflects strong, reliable performance and is expected to describe the majority of employees. It is not a warning sign.

**5. Compensation decisions decoupled by two months.** Reviews will conclude in March; merit increases will be communicated in May. Separating the conversations gives managers room to discuss development honestly without the discussion collapsing into a negotiation about pay.

**6. Mandatory mid-year check-in.** A documented mid-year conversation is now required for every employee. It is lightweight — no forms, just a logged confirmation that the conversation happened — but it is not optional.

**Timeline**

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| January 20 | Self-assessments open |
| February 7 | Self-assessments due |
| February 21 | Manager assessments due |
| Feb 24 – Mar 7 | Calibration sessions |
| Mar 10 – Mar 28 | Review conversations held |
| May 5 | Compensation decisions communicated |

**What is not changing**

Review conversations remain a manager-employee discussion, not a one-way delivery of judgment. Employees may still submit a written response to any review they disagree with, and the escalation path through your HR business partner is unchanged. Performance improvement plans continue to follow the existing policy.

**What you need to do**

*Employees:* Complete your self-assessment by February 7. Review your goals beforehand and gather specific examples — the shorter form rewards concrete detail over general statements.

*Managers:* Attend one of the four calibration training sessions offered January 13–17. Attendance is required before you can participate in calibration.

**Questions**

A detailed FAQ and a sample completed review are posted on the People Operations intranet page. I will host two open office hours on January 15 and January 22 at 11:00 a.m., and your HR business partner is available for team-specific questions.

Thank you for the feedback that shaped these changes. We will survey participants after the cycle closes and continue adjusting.
