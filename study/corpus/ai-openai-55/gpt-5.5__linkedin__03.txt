A project I worked on failed.

Not “we missed a small target” failed — it didn’t deliver the outcome we expected, and we had to make the hard call to stop.

At first, it felt frustrating. We had invested time, energy, and a lot of good intentions. But looking back, the failure taught me more than a smooth success probably would have.

Here’s what I learned:

1. **Alignment matters more than activity.**  
   A busy team isn’t always a focused team. We were moving fast, but not always in the same direction.

2. **Assumptions need to be tested early.**  
   We believed we understood the problem, the user, and the solution. In reality, we should have validated some of our biggest assumptions much sooner.

3. **Communication can’t be an afterthought.**  
   Small misunderstandings became bigger issues over time. Regular, honest communication could have helped us spot risks earlier.

4. **Stopping is not the same as quitting.**  
   Sometimes the smartest decision is to pause, reassess, or walk away before investing even more in the wrong path.

5. **Failure is only wasted if you don’t learn from it.**  
   The project didn’t succeed, but the lessons have changed how I plan, communicate, and make decisions.

Failure is uncomfortable, but it can be incredibly valuable when you’re willing to look at it honestly.

The biggest takeaway for me: success isn’t just about having a great idea — it’s about building the right feedback loops, asking the hard questions early, and staying open to what the data is telling you.
