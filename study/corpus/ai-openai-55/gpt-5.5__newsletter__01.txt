**Subject:** Build a Morning Routine That Actually Sticks

Hi there,

Most morning routines fail for one simple reason: they’re designed for an imaginary version of ourselves.

The version who wakes up energized, never hits snooze, has 90 uninterrupted minutes, and somehow wants to journal, meditate, work out, hydrate, stretch, read, make a smoothie, and plan the day before 7 a.m.

Real life is messier.

A morning routine that sticks is not about creating the “perfect” morning. It’s about building a repeatable start to your day that supports your energy, priorities, and actual schedule.

Here’s how to make one that lasts.

---

## 1. Start Smaller Than You Think

The biggest mistake people make is changing too much at once.

If your current morning is rushed, chaotic, or inconsistent, don’t begin with a 12-step routine. Start with one or two anchors.

For example:

- Drink a glass of water
- Make your bed
- Take a 10-minute walk
- Write down your top three priorities
- Stretch for five minutes
- Avoid your phone for the first 15 minutes

The goal is not to impress yourself. The goal is to build trust with yourself.

A five-minute routine you do every day is more powerful than a one-hour routine you abandon by Wednesday.

---

## 2. Choose a “Why” That Matters

A routine won’t stick just because it sounds nice. It needs a purpose.

Ask yourself:

**What do I want my mornings to help me feel or do?**

Maybe you want to feel calmer.
Maybe you want more energy.
Maybe you want to stop starting the day in reaction mode.
Maybe you want time for your health, creativity, or planning.

Your morning routine should serve that purpose.

If your goal is calm, your routine might include quiet, breathing, or journaling.

If your goal is energy, it might include movement, sunlight, and a real breakfast.

If your goal is focus, it might include reviewing your priorities before checking messages.

Don’t copy someone else’s routine without understanding what yours is supposed to do for you.

---

## 3. Build Around Your Real Life

A good morning routine fits your season of life.

If you have young kids, a long commute, unpredictable work hours, or you’re not naturally a morning person, your routine needs to reflect that.

Instead of asking, “What’s the ideal morning routine?” ask:

**What is the most realistic version I can repeat?**

That might mean:

- A 10-minute routine instead of a 60-minute one
- Preparing things the night before
- Doing part of your routine after school drop-off
- Having a weekday version and a weekend version
- Starting with your wake-up time as it is, not as you wish it were

Consistency comes from designing for your actual life, not fighting against it.

---

## 4. Use the “First 10 Minutes” Rule

If you’re not sure where to begin, focus only on the first 10 minutes after waking.

Those first minutes often set the tone for the rest of the morning.

Try this simple structure:

1. **No phone immediately**
2. **Drink water**
3. **Do one grounding action**

That grounding action could be stretching, breathing, prayer, journaling, stepping outside, or simply sitting quietly.

You don’t need to optimize the whole morning at once. Improve the first 10 minutes, and the rest often gets easier.

---

## 5. Remove Friction the Night Before

A sticky morning routine usually begins the evening before.

Make the desired action easier by preparing your environment.

Examples:

- Put your workout clothes next to your bed
- Fill your water bottle before sleeping
- Place your journal and pen on the table
- Prep breakfast ingredients
- Charge your phone outside the bedroom
- Write tomorrow’s first priority before bed

Morning willpower is unreliable. Morning preparation is not.

The easier you make the first step, the more likely you are to take it.

---

## 6. Create a Minimum Version

Life will interrupt your routine. You’ll oversleep. You’ll feel tired. Something unexpected will happen.

That’s why you need a minimum version.

This is the shortest possible version of your routine that still keeps the habit alive.

For example:

**Full routine:**
- 20-minute workout
- Shower
- Breakfast
- Review priorities
- 10 minutes of reading

**Minimum version:**
- 10 squats
- Drink water
- Write down one priority

The minimum version prevents an all-or-nothing mindset.

You’re not trying to be perfect. You’re trying to keep showing up.

---

## 7. Don’t Add Too Much Too Soon

Once your routine starts working, it’s tempting to add more.

Resist the urge to overload it.

A routine should give you energy, not become another source of pressure.

Before adding something new, ask:

- Is this helping or just making the routine look better?
- Do I have enough time for this consistently?
- What would I remove to make space for it?

A sustainable routine is often simple, even boring. That’s a good thing.

---

## 8. Track the Feeling, Not Just the Habit

Checking off habits can be useful, but don’t forget to notice the result.

At the end of the week, ask:

- Did my mornings feel less rushed?
- Did I have more energy?
- Was I more focused?
- Did this routine make my day easier?
- What felt forced or unnecessary?

Your routine is not a fixed identity. It’s a tool. Adjust it based on what works.

---

## A Simple Morning Routine Template

If you want a place to start, try this:

**The 20-Minute Reset**

- **Minute 1–3:** Drink water and avoid your phone
- **Minute 4–8:** Stretch or move your body
- **Minute 9–13:** Sit quietly, breathe, pray, or journal
- **Minute 14–20:** Review your calendar and choose your top three priorities

That’s it.

Simple. Repeatable. Useful.

Once this feels natural, you can expand it if needed.

---

## This Week’s Challenge

Choose one small morning anchor and do it for the next five days.

Not five habits. Not a complete life makeover. Just one anchor.

Examples:

- “I will drink water before coffee.”
- “I will not check my phone for the first 10 minutes.”
- “I will write down my top priority before opening email.”
- “I will stretch for five minutes after waking.”
- “I will step outside for morning light.”

Make it so easy that it almost feels too small.

That’s where consistency begins.

---

**Final thought:**  
A morning routine that sticks isn’t built on discipline alone. It’s built on clarity, simplicity, and repetition.

Design a morning that helps you become a little more grounded before the world starts asking things from you.
