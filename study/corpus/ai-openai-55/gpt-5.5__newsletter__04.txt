**Subject:** Burnout Isn’t a Badge of Honor — Here’s How to Recover

Hi everyone,

Work can be meaningful, challenging, and energizing. But when pressure keeps piling up without enough recovery, even the most motivated people can hit a wall.

That wall is often called **burnout** — and it’s more than just having a bad week.

Burnout can leave you feeling emotionally drained, disconnected from your work, less effective, and constantly overwhelmed. The good news: burnout is not a personal failure. It’s a signal that something needs to change.

---

## How to Spot Burnout

Burnout can look different for everyone, but common signs include:

- Feeling exhausted even after rest
- Losing motivation or interest in work
- Becoming more cynical, impatient, or detached
- Trouble focusing or making decisions
- Feeling like nothing you do is enough
- Increased headaches, tension, sleep issues, or irritability
- Dreading the workday before it even starts

If these feelings are persistent, it may be time to pause and reassess your workload, routines, and support systems.

---

## Start with One Honest Question

Ask yourself:

**“What is draining me the most right now?”**

It might be too many meetings, unclear priorities, constant notifications, a difficult project, lack of recognition, or simply too much work with too little recovery.

Naming the source doesn’t fix everything immediately, but it gives you a place to start.

---

## Practical Ways to Reduce Burnout

### 1. Reset Your Priorities

Not everything can be urgent.

Try identifying:

- What truly needs to be done today
- What can wait
- What can be delegated
- What needs clarification

If your workload feels unrealistic, talk with your manager about priorities. A helpful question is:

**“Given everything on my plate, what should I focus on first?”**

---

### 2. Build Small Recovery Moments Into the Day

Recovery doesn’t only happen after work or during vacations. Small pauses matter.

Try:

- Taking a short walk between tasks
- Stepping away from your screen for five minutes
- Eating lunch without checking messages
- Blocking focus time on your calendar
- Ending meetings five minutes early when possible

These moments may seem small, but they help your brain reset.

---

### 3. Protect Your Boundaries

Burnout often grows when work expands into every available space.

Consider setting boundaries around:

- After-hours email or messaging
- Back-to-back meetings
- Taking on additional tasks without discussing capacity
- Skipping breaks or lunch
- Saying “yes” automatically

A simple boundary might sound like:

**“I can take this on, but I’ll need to move another deadline. Which should take priority?”**

---

### 4. Reconnect With What Matters

Burnout can make work feel meaningless. Take time to reconnect with the parts of your role that still matter to you.

Ask:

- What work feels most valuable?
- Where do I feel most useful?
- What skills do I want to use more often?
- What part of my job gives me energy?

Even small adjustments toward more meaningful work can help reduce feelings of detachment.

---

### 5. Don’t Isolate

Burnout often makes people withdraw, but support is important.

Talk to someone you trust — a colleague, manager, mentor, friend, or professional. You don’t need to have the perfect words. You can simply say:

**“I’ve been feeling overwhelmed lately and could use some support.”**

If burnout is affecting your health, sleep, relationships, or daily functioning, consider reaching out to a healthcare or mental health professional.

---

## For Managers: Burnout Is a Team Signal

Burnout is not just an individual issue. Managers play a major role in preventing it.

Helpful actions include:

- Clarifying priorities
- Watching for chronic overload
- Encouraging real breaks and time off
- Reducing unnecessary meetings
- Checking in regularly, not just during crises
- Recognizing effort and progress
- Creating psychological safety around asking for help

A simple check-in question can go a long way:

**“What’s one thing we could adjust to make your workload more sustainable?”**

---

## This Week’s Challenge

Choose one small action to protect your energy this week:

- Decline or reschedule one nonessential meeting
- Take a real lunch break
- Ask for clarity on priorities
- Log off on time one day
- Talk to someone about what feels unsustainable
- Block one hour of focused, interruption-free work

Burnout recovery doesn’t happen overnight. But small changes, repeated consistently, can help you regain energy, clarity, and a sense of control.

Take care of yourself — your well-being is not separate from your work. It makes your work possible.
