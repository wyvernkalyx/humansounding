We recently switched to a four-day week.

Not because we wanted to “work less,” but because we wanted to work better.

Like many teams, we noticed the same patterns: packed calendars, constant context switching, and too much time spent being busy rather than making meaningful progress. So we asked a simple question:

What would change if we designed our week around focus, energy, and outcomes — instead of hours?

A few things we’ve learned so far:

✅ Clear priorities matter more than long to-do lists  
✅ Meetings need a real purpose — or they don’t need to happen  
✅ Deep work requires protected time  
✅ Rest is not the opposite of productivity; it supports it  
✅ Trusting people to manage their time raises the bar for ownership

The four-day week has forced us to be more intentional. We’re communicating better, planning more clearly, and being more disciplined about where our time goes.

It’s not about squeezing five days of work into four. It’s about removing what doesn’t serve the work — and creating a healthier, more sustainable rhythm for the team.

We’re still learning, but one thing is already clear:

When people have more space to recharge, they bring more energy, creativity, and focus to the work that matters.

Curious to hear from others: has your team tried a four-day week? What changed?
