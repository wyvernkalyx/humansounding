Yes—but keep them **lightweight and practical**, not corporate.

For a two-person company, a “formal performance review” shouldn’t mean long forms, ratings, or bureaucracy. But it is still worth having a regular, structured conversation about how things are going.

A good approach:

- **Meet quarterly or twice a year**
- Spend 30–60 minutes
- Discuss:
  - What went well?
  - What didn’t?
  - Are roles and expectations clear?
  - What should change?
  - What are the priorities for the next period?
  - Are compensation, equity, workload, or responsibilities still appropriate?
- Write down a short summary afterward

Why it helps:

1. **Prevents resentment**
   In a tiny company, misunderstandings can build quickly because everything is personal and close-range.

2. **Clarifies expectations**
   “I thought you owned sales” or “I assumed you were handling operations” can become serious problems if not addressed.

3. **Creates a record**
   If one person is an employee, documentation matters for compensation, promotions, termination risk, and general fairness.

4. **Improves communication**
   A scheduled review gives permission to discuss harder topics that may not come up in day-to-day work.

5. **Supports growth**
   Even in a two-person company, people need feedback, development, and clarity about where the business is heading.

If both people are equal co-founders, call it a **founder alignment meeting** rather than a performance review. Focus less on “evaluating” each other and more on responsibilities, decision-making, contribution, burnout, equity fairness, and company priorities.

If one person manages the other, then a simple performance review is more appropriate.

Bottom line: **Yes, do it—but make it simple.** A one-page check-in every few months is usually enough for a two-person company.
