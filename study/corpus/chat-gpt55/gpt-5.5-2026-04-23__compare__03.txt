A **controller** is mainly responsible for making sure the company’s financial records are accurate. A **CFO** is responsible for using financial information to guide the company’s strategy, funding, and long-term financial health.

In simple terms:

> **Controller = “What happened?”**  
> **CFO = “What should we do next?”**

### Controller

A controller oversees the accounting function. Their focus is accuracy, compliance, and financial reporting.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Managing the accounting team
- Closing the books monthly/quarterly/yearly
- Preparing financial statements
- Ensuring GAAP/tax/compliance requirements are met
- Managing audits
- Overseeing payroll, accounts payable, accounts receivable
- Maintaining internal controls
- Producing budgets or reports, depending on the company

The controller is usually the senior accounting person.

### CFO

A CFO is an executive responsible for the company’s overall financial strategy and direction.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Financial planning and forecasting
- Cash flow strategy
- Fundraising, debt, equity, and investor relations
- Budgeting at a strategic level
- Pricing, margin, and profitability analysis
- M&A, acquisitions, or exits
- Risk management
- Working with the CEO and board on company strategy
- Deciding how to allocate capital

The CFO is usually part of the executive leadership team.

### Key difference

The **controller makes sure the numbers are right**.  
The **CFO uses those numbers to make business decisions**.

### Example

If revenue is down:

- The **controller** makes sure revenue was recorded correctly and explains what the reports show.
- The **CFO** asks why revenue is down, what it means for cash flow, whether costs need to be cut, whether pricing should change, and how it affects investors or lenders.

### In smaller companies

The roles can overlap. A controller may act like a CFO, or a CFO may handle controller duties. But as companies grow, the distinction becomes clearer:

- **Controller:** accounting and reporting
- **CFO:** strategy, finance, capital, and leadership
