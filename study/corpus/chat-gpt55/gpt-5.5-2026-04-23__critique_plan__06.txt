Yes—moving backups to cloud storage can be the right direction, especially if your external-drive rotation is manual, error-prone, or not reliably offsite.

But I would not necessarily stop using local backups entirely. A better target is usually:

**Local backup + cloud/offsite backup**

That gives you both:

- **Fast restores** from local storage if a server, workstation, or file share fails
- **Offsite protection** from cloud storage if there is theft, fire, flood, or ransomware
- **Less manual handling** than rotating external drives

Before retiring the drive rotation, make sure the cloud backup plan has:

1. **Versioning / retention**
   - You need to recover older file versions, not just the latest encrypted or deleted copy.

2. **Ransomware protection**
   - Ideally immutable backups, object lock, or protected snapshots.

3. **Encryption**
   - Data should be encrypted in transit and at rest.
   - Decide who controls the encryption keys.

4. **Restore testing**
   - Do at least one real restore test before trusting it.

5. **Clear recovery time expectations**
   - Restoring terabytes from the cloud can be slow.
   - Some providers charge for egress/downloads.

6. **Monitoring and alerts**
   - You should know immediately if backups stop running.

7. **Retention policy**
   - Define daily/weekly/monthly retention based on your recovery needs.

My recommendation: **move to cloud backup, but keep at least one local backup target if fast recovery matters.** Once the cloud backups are proven and tested, you can stop the physical drive rotation if it is no longer adding meaningful protection.
