You can be kind, clear, and avoid debating the business. A good answer is direct but supportive:

**Option 1: Simple and firm**
> “Thank you for thinking of me and sharing your plans. I’ve thought about it, and I’m not comfortable investing. I really value our friendship and want to be honest rather than commit to something I’m unsure about.”

**Option 2: If you don’t want to critique the idea**
> “I’m not the right person to invest, but I’m cheering you on and I hope it goes well.”

**Option 3: If she asks why**
> “I don’t feel confident enough in the business as an investment, and I don’t want money to create tension between us.”

**Option 4: Offer non-money support**
> “I can’t invest financially, but I’d be happy to look over your pitch, share your page, or introduce you to someone who might have relevant experience.”

Try not to say “I don’t think it will work” unless she specifically asks for honest feedback. Even then, frame it gently:  
> “Since you asked, my concern is that the market/plan/financials don’t yet convince me. I could be wrong, but it’s not something I’m comfortable investing in.”

The key is: **decline clearly, don’t over-explain, and protect the friendship.**
