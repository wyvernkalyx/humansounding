For a 60-person company, the choice between an in-house help desk and an outsourced help desk usually comes down to cost, service quality, control, and how complex your IT environment is.

## Quick comparison

| Area | In-house help desk | Outsourced help desk |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Higher fixed cost: salaries, benefits, tools, training | Usually lower and more predictable monthly cost |
| Responsiveness | Can be very fast, especially for onsite issues | Good for remote support, but onsite support may cost extra or be slower |
| Company knowledge | Strong understanding of users, systems, and priorities | Takes time to learn your environment |
| Coverage | Limited by staff hours and availability | Can offer extended hours, 24/7, holidays |
| Control | More direct control over priorities and service style | Less direct control; depends on contract and SLA |
| Scalability | Harder to scale without hiring | Easier to scale up or down |
| Security and compliance | More control internally | Must carefully vet provider security practices |
| User experience | More personal and familiar | Can feel less personal if not well managed |
| Expertise | Limited to the skills of hired staff | Access to broader technical expertise |
| Continuity risk | Risk if one IT person leaves or is unavailable | Provider has backup staff and processes |

## In-house help desk

### Advantages
- Better knowledge of your company’s people, workflows, and systems.
- More personal service for employees.
- Faster handling of physical issues such as printers, Wi-Fi, laptops, meeting room equipment, and desk setups.
- Greater control over priorities, security practices, and internal processes.
- Useful if your company has specialized software, strict compliance needs, or complex internal systems.

### Disadvantages
- Expensive for a 60-person company if you need full coverage.
- A single IT employee may become a bottleneck or single point of failure.
- Limited support outside business hours unless you hire more staff.
- Recruiting, training, and retaining IT talent takes management effort.
- Harder to provide deep expertise across networking, cybersecurity, cloud tools, hardware, and applications.

### Typical fit
An in-house help desk makes sense if:
- You need frequent onsite support.
- Your systems are specialized or sensitive.
- Employees require high-touch service.
- You already have enough IT workload to justify at least one full-time IT person.

## Outsourced help desk

### Advantages
- Lower fixed cost and easier budgeting.
- Access to a team rather than relying on one person.
- Better coverage options, including evenings, weekends, or 24/7 support.
- Broader technical expertise.
- Easier to scale as the company grows.
- Provider typically brings ticketing systems, documentation, monitoring, and standard processes.

### Disadvantages
- Less familiarity with company culture and individual users.
- Quality varies significantly by provider.
- Some issues may require escalation, causing delays.
- Onsite support may not be included or may cost extra.
- Security, privacy, and access controls need close management.
- Employees may be frustrated if the service feels impersonal or scripted.

### Typical fit
An outsourced help desk makes sense if:
- Most employees work remotely or use standard cloud tools like Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Slack, Zoom, and common SaaS apps.
- You want predictable costs.
- You need coverage beyond normal office hours.
- You do not have enough IT work to justify multiple internal IT employees.

## Cost considerations for a 60-person company

### In-house
A small company usually needs at least one IT generalist. Costs may include:

- Salary and benefits
- Ticketing/help desk software
- Device management tools
- Security tools
- Training and certifications
- Backup coverage during vacation or sick leave

The main problem is coverage. One person may be enough most days, but not when they are unavailable or when multiple issues occur at once.

### Outsourced
Outsourced help desks often charge:

- Per user per month
- Per device per month
- Fixed monthly retainer
- Per-ticket or hourly support

For 60 employees, outsourced support is often cheaper than hiring a full internal team, though not always cheaper than one junior or mid-level IT hire.

## Best option for many 60-person companies: hybrid model

A hybrid approach is often the most practical:

- Keep one internal IT manager, systems administrator, or technically strong operations person.
- Outsource Tier 1 help desk support, monitoring, patching, and after-hours coverage.
- Escalate complex or strategic issues internally or to a specialist provider.

This gives you:
- Internal ownership and accountability
- Better vendor management
- Lower support burden
- Broader expertise
- Backup coverage
- Better scalability

## Recommendation

For a typical 60-person company, an outsourced or hybrid help desk is usually the best choice.

Choose **outsourced** if your IT environment is standard, mostly cloud-based, and you want predictable support costs.

Choose **in-house** if you need frequent onsite support, have specialized systems, or require tight control over security and user experience.

Choose **hybrid** if you want the best balance: internal IT ownership with outsourced day-to-day support and backup coverage.
