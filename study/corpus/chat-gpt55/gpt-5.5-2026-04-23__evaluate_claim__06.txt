Not exactly — but cereal companies *did* help popularize it.

The phrase “breakfast is the most important meal of the day” is often traced to early-20th-century health writing, including a 1917 article in *Good Health*, a magazine associated with John Harvey Kellogg’s health movement. Kellogg and other cereal makers were major promoters of breakfast foods, and the breakfast-cereal industry benefited a lot from the idea that everyone should eat breakfast daily.

So the claim has a kernel of truth: **breakfast was heavily marketed by cereal companies and health-food entrepreneurs.** But it’s too strong to say they simply “invented” the concept. People had long eaten breakfast, and doctors/nutrition reformers also promoted morning meals for reasons unrelated to cereal advertising.

Scientifically, the modern view is more nuanced:

- Breakfast can be helpful if it improves your energy, mood, concentration, or prevents overeating later.
- A high-protein, high-fiber breakfast is generally more beneficial than sugary cereal or pastries.
- Skipping breakfast is not automatically unhealthy.
- Claims that breakfast “boosts metabolism” or is essential for weight loss are overstated.
- Observational studies often find breakfast-eaters are healthier, but that may be because they also tend to have other healthy habits.

So the short answer: **the slogan was strongly amplified by cereal marketing, but the broader idea wasn’t purely invented by cereal companies. Breakfast can be useful, but it is not universally the “most important” meal for everyone.**
