It’s grammatically fine, but it’s too generic to be effective. Phrases like “results-driven professional” and “10+ years of experience” don’t tell employers what you do, what you’re good at, or what results you’ve achieved.

A stronger version should include:

- Your field or role
- Key skills/specialties
- Measurable impact or accomplishments
- The type of value you bring

Example improved version:

> Results-driven operations professional with 10+ years of experience improving processes, leading cross-functional teams, and delivering measurable efficiency gains. Skilled in project management, stakeholder collaboration, and data-driven decision-making.

If you want a sharper version, use something like:

> Experienced [job title/field] with 10+ years of success in [key responsibility], [skill/area], and [business outcome]. Proven ability to [specific achievement or value].

For example:

> Results-driven marketing leader with 10+ years of experience building brand strategies, managing campaigns, and increasing customer engagement across digital channels.

So: it’s “fine” as a placeholder, but not strong enough for a resume. You should customize it to your role and add specifics.
