Usually: **take the three-year discount only if you’re highly confident you’ll still need the software for all three years and the contract has protections.** Otherwise, **go year-to-year** and preserve flexibility.

### Choose the 3-year renewal if:
- The software is **mission-critical** and hard to replace.
- Usage/seats are stable or growing.
- You have no planned vendor evaluation, migration, acquisition, restructuring, or major tech-stack change.
- The discount is meaningful — often **15–25%+** is where it starts becoming compelling.
- You can negotiate protections:
  - Price increase cap after the term
  - Ability to reduce seats annually
  - Termination for breach/security failure
  - SLA/support commitments
  - Data export rights
  - Product roadmap or feature commitments, if relevant
  - Assignment/change-of-control protection

### Go year-to-year if:
- You may reduce headcount or usage.
- You’re considering alternatives.
- The product has quality, support, security, or roadmap concerns.
- Your business needs may change significantly.
- The discount is small, e.g. **under 10%**, unless price increases are expected.
- The vendor is trying to lock you in because renewal risk is high.

### Simple way to compare

Calculate the value of the discount:

> **Three-year savings = annual list price × discount % × 3**

Example:  
If the software costs **$100,000/year** and the vendor offers a **15% discount**, the three-year savings are:

> $100,000 × 15% × 3 = **$45,000**

Then ask:  
“Is the flexibility to cancel, renegotiate, switch vendors, or reduce seats during the next three years worth more than $45,000?”

If yes, go year-to-year.  
If no, take the three-year deal.

### One important distinction

A three-year contract can mean two different things:

1. **Three years prepaid upfront**  
   - Higher cash-flow cost.
   - You should demand a larger discount.
   - Compare against your cost of capital.

2. **Three-year commitment, paid annually**  
   - Better than prepaying.
   - Still creates lock-in.
   - Try to negotiate annual seat adjustments.

### My practical recommendation

If the software is essential and you’re confident you’ll use it for three years, I’d push for a **three-year term paid annually**, with a discount and flexibility on seat counts.

If there’s any serious uncertainty, go **year-to-year** or negotiate a middle ground: **one-year renewal with a two-year price lock** or **three-year pricing with annual opt-out rights**.
