Airlines overbook flights because some passengers usually don’t show up.

If an airline sells exactly as many tickets as there are seats, the plane may depart with empty seats due to missed connections, cancellations, last-minute changes, or no-shows. Empty seats mean lost revenue, while the flight’s operating cost is mostly the same whether the seat is filled or not.

So airlines use historical data and forecasting models to estimate how many passengers are likely not to appear, then sell a small number of extra tickets. This helps them:

- **Maximize revenue**
- **Keep planes fuller**
- **Offer more flexible tickets and rebooking options**
- **Reduce the cost impact of no-shows**

The risk is that sometimes more passengers show up than expected. When that happens, the airline may ask for volunteers to take a later flight in exchange for compensation. If not enough people volunteer, some passengers may be involuntarily denied boarding, usually with legally required compensation depending on the country and circumstances.

In short: airlines overbook because statistically, not everyone shows up, and filling otherwise empty seats is financially valuable.
