A drop from “all day” to ~3 hours in one month usually points to one of these:

1. **Battery health has degraded or a cell is failing**
   - Lithium batteries normally decline gradually, but a weak/failing cell can cause a sudden capacity drop.
   - If the laptop is 2–4+ years old, this becomes more likely.

2. **Something is using much more power than before**
   - A recent OS update, new app, browser tabs/extensions, antivirus scan, cloud sync, or background process can keep CPU/GPU active.
   - Video calls, gaming, high brightness, or external monitors can also cut battery life sharply.

3. **Power settings changed**
   - Performance mode, high refresh rate, keyboard backlight, max brightness, or disabled battery saver can reduce runtime a lot.

4. **Battery calibration/reading issue**
   - Sometimes the laptop misreports battery percentage, making it seem like capacity collapsed.

5. **Heat**
   - Dusty fans, blocked vents, or a hot environment can force fans/CPU to work harder and degrade battery faster.

### What to check first

**On Windows:**
1. Open Task Manager → sort by **CPU** and **Power usage**.
2. Go to Settings → System → Power & battery → check **Battery usage by app**.
3. Run a battery report:
   ```cmd
   powercfg /batteryreport
   ```
   Open the generated report and compare:
   - **Design Capacity**
   - **Full Charge Capacity**
   
   If Full Charge Capacity is far below Design Capacity, the battery has degraded.

**On macOS:**
1. Apple menu → System Settings → Battery → check usage.
2. Open Activity Monitor → Energy tab.
3. Check battery health:
   - System Settings → Battery → Battery Health
   - Or System Information → Power → look at **Cycle Count** and **Maximum Capacity**

### Quick fixes to try

- Restart the laptop.
- Update the OS and drivers.
- Lower brightness.
- Turn on Battery Saver/Low Power Mode.
- Close heavy background apps.
- Check for browser extensions or many tabs.
- Make sure vents are clean and fans are working.
- If available, switch from “Best performance” to “Balanced” or “Battery saver.”

### When to suspect the battery itself

If battery health shows low capacity, “Service recommended,” swelling, sudden shutdowns, or the laptop drops from e.g. 40% to 5%, the battery likely needs replacement. If it’s under warranty, contact the manufacturer.
