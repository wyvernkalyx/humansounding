Yes — **“circle back” is generally acceptable in a work email**, especially in business settings where informal corporate language is common.

That said, it can sound a bit like **office jargon**, so whether it’s best depends on tone and audience.

Examples:
- “I’ll circle back with you next week after I have more information.”
- “Let’s circle back on this after the client meeting.”

If you want a clearer or more polished alternative, you could write:
- “I’ll follow up with you next week.”
- “Let’s revisit this after the client meeting.”
- “I’ll get back to you once I have an update.”

For most professional emails, **“follow up”** or **“get back to you”** is a safer, more direct choice.
