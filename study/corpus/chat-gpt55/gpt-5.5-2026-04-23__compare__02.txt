For someone who moves every four years, **renting is often financially and practically better**, but buying can still make sense in certain markets or if you plan to keep the home as a rental.

## Renting: Advantages for a frequent mover

### Pros
- **Flexibility:** Easier to relocate for work, family, or lifestyle changes.
- **Lower upfront costs:** Usually security deposit and moving costs, not a down payment plus closing costs.
- **No selling costs:** You avoid realtor commissions, transfer taxes, staging, repairs, and months of uncertainty.
- **Less maintenance responsibility:** Major repairs are typically the landlord’s problem.
- **Lower risk:** You are less exposed to a housing downturn right when you need to move.

### Cons
- **No equity building:** Monthly rent does not create ownership.
- **Rent can rise:** Especially in high-demand areas.
- **Less control:** Restrictions on pets, renovations, and long-term stability.

## Buying: Advantages and disadvantages with a 4-year timeline

### Pros
- **Potential equity growth:** Mortgage payments may build equity, and the home may appreciate.
- **Stable housing payment:** A fixed-rate mortgage protects against rent inflation.
- **Control over the home:** Renovations, pets, and customization are easier.
- **Possible tax benefits:** Mortgage interest and property taxes may help, depending on your situation and tax rules.

### Cons
- **High transaction costs:** Buying and selling are expensive. Closing costs, agent commissions, taxes, and repairs can easily consume much of your appreciation.
- **Short ownership period risk:** In the first few years, mortgage payments are mostly interest, not principal.
- **Market timing risk:** If prices are flat or down when you move, you may lose money.
- **Maintenance costs:** A common rule of thumb is 1%–2% of the home value per year.
- **Less flexibility:** Selling or renting out the property can complicate your move.

## The key issue: transaction costs

Buying usually becomes more attractive the longer you stay. A four-year stay is often near or below the break-even point because:

- Buyer closing costs may be around **2%–5%** of the purchase price.
- Seller costs, especially agent commissions, may be around **5%–8%**.
- Maintenance, repairs, insurance, property taxes, and HOA fees can add significantly.
- Early mortgage payments build equity slowly.

So if you buy a $400,000 home, total round-trip transaction costs could easily be **$30,000–$50,000+**, before considering maintenance or market changes.

## When renting is probably better

Renting is usually better if:

- You are confident you will move in about four years.
- Home prices are high relative to rents.
- You do not want to manage a rental property after moving.
- Your job or location plans are uncertain.
- You have better uses for the down payment, such as investing or keeping liquidity.
- The local market has slow appreciation or high property taxes/HOA costs.

## When buying might make sense

Buying could make sense if:

- The monthly cost of owning is close to or lower than renting.
- You are in a market with strong expected appreciation.
- You can buy below market value or avoid major transaction costs.
- You plan to keep the home and rent it out after moving.
- You value stability, customization, or school district access.
- You have a large down payment and can handle repairs and vacancy risk.

## Simple rule of thumb

For someone moving every four years:

- **Renting is usually the safer choice.**
- **Buying may work if the local rent-versus-buy math is very favorable or if you are willing to become a landlord.**

The best decision depends heavily on the local market. Compare the **total four-year cost of renting** against the **total four-year cost of owning**, including closing costs, selling costs, taxes, insurance, maintenance, mortgage interest, expected appreciation, and investment returns on the down payment.
