Yes — in general, **don’t put a hot laptop battery in the fridge or freezer**.

Why not:

- **Condensation** can form when you take it back out, which can cause corrosion or short circuits.
- **Rapid temperature changes** can stress the battery and electronics.
- **Charging or using lithium-ion batteries while cold** can damage them and increase risk.
- If the battery is actually overheating or failing, a fridge is not a safe containment method.

What to do instead:

1. **Shut the laptop down and unplug it.**
2. If the battery is removable and safe to handle, **remove it**.
3. Put it on a **non-flammable surface** like tile, concrete, or metal, away from papers/fabric.
4. Let it cool naturally at room temperature with good ventilation.
5. If it’s **swollen, hissing, leaking, smoking, smells sweet/chemical, or gets too hot to touch**, keep away from it and consider it hazardous — contact the manufacturer, a battery recycler, or emergency services if there’s smoke/fire risk.

If it’s only mildly warm after normal use, that’s usually fine. If it regularly gets very hot, the battery or cooling system may need service.
