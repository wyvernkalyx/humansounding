Your meeting likely has two separate but related failures:

## Diagnosis

### 1. The meeting has no clear decision structure
If nobody remembers what was decided, the meeting is probably functioning as a discussion forum, not a decision-making forum.

Common symptoms:
- Agenda items are vague: “Discuss project X”
- People talk through issues but do not close them
- Decisions are implied, not explicitly stated
- No one owns the final call
- The meeting ends with “sounds good” instead of “we decided X”

### 2. The agenda is overloaded
Running over usually means one or more of these is true:
- Too many topics are included
- Topics are too broad
- Updates are being given live instead of shared beforehand
- Hard conversations are discovered during the meeting rather than prepared for
- No one is time-boxing discussions

### 3. The meeting lacks a facilitator
If everyone participates but no one manages the process, the meeting will drift.

A facilitator should:
- Keep the group on the topic
- Cut off rabbit holes
- Clarify when a decision is needed
- Summarize decisions out loud
- Assign owners and deadlines
- Park unresolved issues

### 4. Decisions are not being recorded in real time
If notes are written later — or not at all — people will leave with different interpretations.

The problem is not memory. The problem is lack of a visible decision log.

### 5. Too much of the meeting is probably status reporting
Weekly meetings often become verbal status updates. That is usually a bad use of group time.

Status can often be handled asynchronously:
- Written updates before the meeting
- Dashboard
- Project tracker
- Short “red/yellow/green” summaries

The live meeting should focus on:
- Decisions
- Blockers
- Tradeoffs
- Cross-functional coordination
- Escalations

---

## Likely Root Cause

Your weekly meeting does not have a strong operating system.

It probably lacks:
1. A decision-focused agenda  
2. Time boxes  
3. A facilitator  
4. A note-taker or decision recorder  
5. Clear owners for action items  
6. A post-meeting recap  

---

## Fix It With This Format

### Before the meeting
Send an agenda with three categories:

| Item | Desired outcome | Owner | Time |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Project timeline issue | Decide whether to delay launch | Maya | 15 min |
| Budget request | Approve/reject extra spend | Chris | 10 min |
| Hiring blocker | Identify next action | Priya | 10 min |

Avoid agenda items like:
- “Discuss roadmap”
- “Talk about hiring”
- “Review project”

Use outcome-based wording:
- “Decide X”
- “Approve Y”
- “Choose between A/B”
- “Assign owner for Z”

---

### During the meeting
Use this rule for every topic:

1. What is the issue?
2. What decision or action is needed?
3. Who decides?
4. What did we decide?
5. Who owns the next step?
6. By when?

At the end of each topic, someone should say:

> “To confirm, we decided X. Sarah owns Y by Friday.”

If you cannot say that, you did not finish the topic.

---

### End the meeting 5 minutes early
Reserve the final 5 minutes for a recap:

- Decisions made
- Action items
- Owners
- Deadlines
- Open issues
- Parked topics

This is non-negotiable. If you do not recap, people will leave with different versions of reality.

---

## Rule of Thumb

If an item does not require live discussion, remove it.

Keep the weekly meeting for:
- Decisions
- Risks
- Dependencies
- Blockers
- Escalations

Move these out:
- General updates
- FYIs
- Detailed reviews
- Work that only involves two people
- Topics with no owner or desired outcome

---

## Fast Experiment for Next Week

For the next meeting, try this:

1. Cap the agenda at 3–5 items.
2. Assign a desired outcome to each item.
3. Time-box every item.
4. Appoint one facilitator.
5. Record decisions live.
6. End with a recap.
7. Send notes immediately afterward.

If the meeting still runs over, your agenda is too big or the wrong people are in the room.
