It can be both.

Stability usually feels like: “This job supports the life I want, and I’m choosing it.”

Being stuck often feels like: “I’m staying because leaving feels too hard, risky, or unclear.”

A few questions may help separate the two:

- If you were offered this same job today, with what you know now, would you choose it again?
- Are you still learning, growing, or feeling respected?
- Do you stay because you like the work, the people, the pay, the balance — or mostly because change feels intimidating?
- When you imagine being in the same role three years from now, do you feel calm, proud, bored, trapped, or sad?
- Is the job giving you stability for something meaningful outside work, or is it quietly shrinking your sense of possibility?

Eleven years in one job is not automatically a problem. It can mean loyalty, expertise, reliability, and a solid foundation. But if you’ve stopped feeling challenged, appreciated, or alive there, it may be time to explore — not necessarily quit immediately, but start gathering options.

You don’t have to decide “stay or leave” all at once. A gentler first step might be asking: “What would need to change for me to feel like I’m choosing this job, not just defaulting to it?”
