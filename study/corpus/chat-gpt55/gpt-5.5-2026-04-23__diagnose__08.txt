Believe neither **by default**. Believe the evidence.

Sales and Support often see different realities:

- **Sales** may be seeing demos, happy prospects, feature checklists, or a product that works well enough to sell.
- **Support** sees real users, messy workflows, edge cases, integrations, bugs, and repeated complaints.

So both can be “right” in their own context.

The question to answer is not “who is correct?” but:

> **For which customers, in which workflows, how often, and with what business impact is the product broken?**

A practical way to handle it:

1. **Define “fine” and “broken.”**  
   Is the issue usability, reliability, missing features, performance, onboarding, integrations, or actual defects?

2. **Look at support data.**  
   Number of tickets, severity, repeat issues, affected accounts, churn risk, time to resolution.

3. **Look at sales data.**  
   Lost deals, objections, demo failures, feature gaps, win/loss notes, customer expectations set during sales.

4. **Segment customers.**  
   The product may be fine for small customers but broken for enterprise users, or fine for new users but broken at scale.

5. **Reproduce the complaints.**  
   Have Product/Engineering sit with Support and watch the issue happen.

6. **Join customer calls.**  
   Listen directly to frustrated users and successful users. Reality is usually clearer there.

7. **Quantify impact.**  
   A bug affecting 2% of users but causing enterprise churn may be more serious than a noisy low-impact issue.

My bias: **Support should be taken very seriously**, because they are closest to post-sale reality. But Support can also overrepresent negative experiences because happy customers rarely file tickets.

So the best answer is:

> Believe Support about the pain, believe Sales about market perception, and use data to determine the scale and priority.

If Support says it is broken, do not dismiss it. Investigate until you know whether it is a minor complaint, a major reliability issue, a bad-fit customer segment, or a mismatch between what Sales promises and what the product actually does.
