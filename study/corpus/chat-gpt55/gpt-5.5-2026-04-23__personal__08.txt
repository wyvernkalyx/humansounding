That’s a very understandable hesitation. Letting an adult child move back home can be loving and practical—but it can also affect your peace, finances, routines, and relationship. You’re allowed to take your own needs seriously.

A helpful way to think about it is: **under what conditions would this feel okay—or not okay—for you?**

Consider these areas:

- **Reason for moving back:** Is this a temporary setback, job loss, breakup, health issue, debt, or a pattern of instability?
- **Timeline:** Would this be for 3 months, 6 months, a year? Open-ended arrangements often become stressful.
- **Money:** Would he pay rent, utilities, groceries, or contribute in another way?
- **Responsibilities:** Chores, cleaning, cooking, errands, shared spaces.
- **Lifestyle rules:** Guests, noise, smoking/drinking/drugs, pets, privacy, coming and going.
- **Independence plan:** Job search, saving money, therapy/treatment if relevant, debt plan, housing plan.
- **Your limits:** What would make you regret saying yes? What behaviors are non-negotiable?

You don’t have to answer immediately. You could say something like:

> “I love you and I want to understand what you need, but I need time to think about whether this will work for me. If we consider it, we’ll need to agree on expectations, timeline, money, and responsibilities in writing.”

If you do allow it, I’d strongly suggest making it **specific and temporary**, such as:

> “You can stay for 90 days while you look for work/housing. We’ll reassess at the end of each month.”

And if your gut is saying no, it’s okay to offer other support instead:

> “I’m not able to have you move in, but I can help you make a budget, look for rentals, or contribute a limited amount toward a deposit.”

The most important thing is not whether a “good parent” would say yes or no. It’s whether the arrangement is healthy, respectful, and sustainable for you.
