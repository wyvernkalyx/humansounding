Not necessarily. “Never pay off a low-interest mortgage early” is too absolute. A better rule is: **compare the guaranteed return from paying it down with your best alternative uses for the money, adjusted for taxes, risk, liquidity, and peace of mind.**

### Why keeping a low-rate mortgage can make sense
If your mortgage rate is low—especially a fixed rate—you may be better off using extra cash for:

- **Emergency savings**: Liquidity matters. Money used to pay down the mortgage is hard to access unless you refinance, sell, or use a HELOC.
- **High-interest debt**: Credit cards, personal loans, etc. usually come first.
- **Retirement contributions**: Especially if you have an employer match.
- **Investing**: Over long periods, diversified investments may return more than a low mortgage rate, though not guaranteed.
- **Inflation advantage**: A fixed mortgage gets “cheaper” in real terms over time if inflation/wages rise.

Example: If your mortgage is 3%, paying it down gives you a roughly 3% guaranteed return before tax considerations. If you expect long-term investments to earn more than that, investing may be financially better—though riskier.

### Why paying it off early can still be reasonable
Paying down the mortgage can be attractive if:

- You dislike debt and value peace of mind.
- You’re nearing retirement and want lower fixed expenses.
- You already max retirement accounts and have enough liquidity.
- Your mortgage rate is not that low.
- You want a guaranteed return with no market volatility.
- You don’t benefit much from the mortgage interest deduction.

### Tax wrinkle
If you itemize deductions and can deduct mortgage interest, your **after-tax mortgage rate** may be lower than the stated rate. But many people don’t itemize, so the deduction may not matter.

### Practical priority order
A common approach is:

1. Keep an emergency fund.
2. Pay off high-interest debt.
3. Get full employer retirement match.
4. Fund major goals and retirement appropriately.
5. Then decide between extra mortgage payments vs. taxable investing or other goals.

### Bottom line
If you have a **very low fixed-rate mortgage**, it’s often financially optimal not to rush paying it off. But “never” is wrong. Paying it off early can be a rational choice if you value certainty, lower monthly obligations, or being debt-free more than the potential upside of investing elsewhere.
