You have several options—and you’re allowed to choose the one that protects your time and sanity. Saying “yes” doesn’t mean you’re trapped.

### 1. **Renegotiate the role**
Instead of organizing everything, shift to coordinating only one piece.

**Script:**  
“Hey everyone, I realized I can’t take on the whole reunion after all. I’m happy to handle the date poll and group chat, but we’ll need other people to take food, venue, activities, and lodging.”

### 2. **Delegate aggressively**
Make a simple list of tasks and assign them. Don’t ask vaguely for “help”; ask for specific ownership.

Examples:
- Venue/location
- Food/catering/potluck coordination
- Invitations/RSVPs
- Budget/payments
- Activities/games
- Lodging/travel info
- Cleanup

**Script:**  
“I can’t do this solo. If we want the reunion to happen, I need one person to own each category by Friday.”

### 3. **Scale it way down**
A reunion doesn’t need to be a huge production. It can be:
- Picnic at a park
- Potluck at someone’s house
- Restaurant reservation
- One-day gathering instead of a weekend
- “Bring your own everything” casual meetup

**Script:**  
“To keep this manageable, I’m changing the plan to a simple park potluck. No formal schedule, no big extras.”

### 4. **Postpone it**
If the timing is bad, delay it.

**Script:**  
“I underestimated what this would take, and I don’t have the bandwidth right now. I think we should move it to later in the year unless someone else wants to take over.”

### 5. **Hand it off completely**
If someone else is more enthusiastic, let them lead.

**Script:**  
“I need to step back from organizing the reunion. I’m happy to share what I’ve done so far, but someone else will need to take the lead.”

### 6. **Cancel, if necessary**
If no one helps and you don’t want to do it, cancellation is valid.

**Script:**  
“I’m sorry, but I can’t organize the reunion after all. Since no one else has taken over, we’ll need to cancel or revisit it another time.”

### 7. **Use money to reduce labor**
If there’s a budget, outsource:
- Restaurant private room
- Caterer
- Event space with staff
- Online RSVP tool
- Shared payment app
- Hotel block coordinator

This can turn “massive planning job” into “make one reservation and send one message.”

### A good next step
Send one clear message soon, before expectations grow:

> “I need to be honest: I overcommitted when I said yes to organizing the reunion. I can’t handle the whole thing myself. I’m willing to help with [specific task], but we’ll need others to take over the rest. If nobody can, we should simplify, postpone, or cancel.”

The key is to stop silently carrying it. You don’t need to justify endlessly—just be clear about what you can and cannot do.
