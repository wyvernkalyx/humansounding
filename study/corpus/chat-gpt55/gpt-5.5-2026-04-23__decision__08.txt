Not necessarily—but it’s often a mistake if your buyers expect self-serve evaluation.

A missing pricing page creates friction. Many buyers use pricing to decide whether a product is even worth considering. If they can’t find it, they may assume it’s expensive, overly enterprise-focused, or not transparent, and leave.

That said, there are cases where launching without a pricing page is reasonable.

## It can make sense to skip pricing if:

- You sell large enterprise contracts with highly variable pricing.
- The product is early and you’re still learning willingness to pay.
- Pricing depends heavily on usage, integrations, compliance, services, or custom implementation.
- You want every lead to go through sales because qualification matters.
- You’re running a private beta or limited launch.

In those cases, “Contact sales” can be acceptable—especially if your audience is used to it.

## It is usually a mistake if:

- You are selling to SMBs, startups, creators, developers, or self-serve buyers.
- Competitors publish pricing.
- Your product has clear tiers or usage-based pricing.
- Buyers can sign up without talking to sales.
- You want inbound conversion from your website.

For self-serve or product-led companies, not having pricing often reduces trust and lowers conversion.

## A good compromise

If you’re not ready to publish exact pricing, you can still give buyers useful signals:

- “Plans start at $99/month”
- “Typical customers pay $500–$2,000/month”
- “Free plan available”
- “Usage-based pricing based on seats/events/API calls”
- “Contact us for enterprise pricing”
- A pricing range or calculator
- A comparison of tiers without exact prices

This helps visitors self-qualify without locking you into a perfect pricing model.

## Rule of thumb

If your customer asks, “Can I afford this?” before they ask, “Can I talk to sales?”—you probably need a pricing page.

If your customer asks, “Can this be customized for our organization?” before they ask about price, a contact-sales approach may be fine.

So: launching without a pricing page isn’t automatically a mistake, but launching without any pricing signal often is.
