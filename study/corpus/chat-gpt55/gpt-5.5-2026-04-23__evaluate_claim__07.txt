Yes — **in most cases, your IT guy is right**.

The old rule “change passwords every 90 days” is now widely considered outdated for normal user accounts **unless there is evidence the password may be compromised**. Modern guidance from organizations like **NIST** recommends *against* forced periodic password changes because they often make security worse.

Why? Forced rotation tends to make people choose predictable passwords like:

- `Summer2024!` → `Fall2024!`
- `Password1!` → `Password2!`
- Writing passwords down
- Reusing similar passwords across systems

Instead, current best practice is:

1. **Use long, unique passwords/passphrases**
   - Example: `correct-horse-laptop-river-73`
2. **Use a password manager**
   - So every site/app gets a unique password.
3. **Enable MFA/2FA**
   - Especially for email, VPN, admin accounts, banking, cloud apps.
4. **Change passwords when there’s a reason**
   - Suspected breach
   - Phishing incident
   - Malware on a device
   - Employee departure
   - Password was reused elsewhere
   - Vendor/application compromise
5. **Block known-compromised passwords**
   - Many modern systems can check against breach databases.
6. **Use monitoring and account lockout/rate limiting**
   - Detect suspicious logins instead of relying on calendar-based resets.

There are exceptions. Password rotation may still be required for:

- Certain compliance frameworks or legacy policies
- Shared/admin accounts
- Service accounts/API keys
- Environments without MFA or proper monitoring
- High-risk systems

So the short version: **Routine 90-day password rotation for ordinary users is generally outdated. “Change when compromised” plus MFA and strong unique passwords is the modern approach.**
