For a small brand, the main difference is:

**A trademark protects your brand identity.**  
**A copyright protects your creative content.**

### Trademark
A **trademark** protects things that identify your business as the source of goods or services, such as:

- Brand name
- Logo
- Slogan
- Product name
- Packaging look, in some cases

Example: If your brand is called **“Blue Fox Coffee”**, the name and logo could be trademarks.

The purpose of trademark law is to prevent customer confusion. It helps stop another business in a similar market from using a name or logo that makes people think they are connected to you.

For a small brand, trademark protection is especially important if you want to protect your business name, product names, or logo as you grow.

### Copyright
A **copyright** protects original creative works, such as:

- Website text
- Product photos
- Illustrations
- Videos
- Music
- Blog posts
- Graphic designs
- Packaging artwork

Example: If you design a unique illustration for your product label, the artwork may be protected by copyright.

Copyright does **not** protect the brand name itself. It protects the creative expression, not the business identity.

### Simple example
If you have a skincare brand:

- The name **“Glow Fern”** = trademark
- The logo that says **Glow Fern** = potentially trademark and copyright
- The product photos on your website = copyright
- The wording in your product descriptions = copyright
- The slogan **“Clean Skincare for Everyday Glow”** = possibly trademark
- The formula for the skincare product = neither trademark nor copyright; that may involve trade secrets or patents

### Do you need to register?
You can have some rights without registration, but registration gives stronger protection.

**Trademark registration** can help you:
- Stop others from using a confusingly similar brand name
- Expand nationally with stronger rights
- Protect your logo or product names
- Build brand value

**Copyright registration** can help you:
- Enforce your rights more easily
- Seek statutory damages and attorney’s fees in some cases
- Prove ownership of creative work

### Quick comparison

| Issue | Trademark | Copyright |
|---|---|---|
| Protects | Brand names, logos, slogans | Creative works |
| Purpose | Prevents customer confusion | Prevents copying of creative expression |
| Examples | Business name, product name, logo | Photos, videos, artwork, text |
| Requires use in business? | Usually yes | No |
| Can last forever? | Potentially, if used and renewed | No, but lasts a long time |
| Best for | Protecting your brand identity | Protecting your content and designs |

### Bottom line
If you are building a small brand, you usually care about both:

- Use **trademark** protection for your brand name, logo, and product names.
- Use **copyright** protection for your photos, designs, website copy, videos, and other creative assets.

If you can only prioritize one early on, many small brands start by checking and protecting their **brand name as a trademark**, because changing names later can be expensive and confusing.
