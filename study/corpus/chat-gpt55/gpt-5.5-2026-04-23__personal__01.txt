Don’t lie, but don’t overshare. Keep it calm, brief, and focused on your current work.

### If you haven’t decided yet
Say:

> “Yes, I’ve been having some conversations externally. I wasn’t looking to be disruptive, and I’m still evaluating what makes sense. I’m committed to handling my responsibilities professionally, and I’ll keep you informed if anything changes.”

If they ask where:

> “I’d rather keep the details private while I’m still evaluating.”

If they ask whether you have an offer:

> “I do have something I’m considering, but I’m not ready to discuss specifics yet.”

### If you want to stay but only under better conditions
Say:

> “Yes, I’ve been exploring the market, and I did receive an offer. My preference would be to stay if we can address some things that are important to me, like compensation/growth/scope. Would you be open to discussing that?”

Only do this if you’re genuinely prepared to leave.

### If you’re leaning toward taking the competitor offer
Say:

> “Yes, I’ve been interviewing and I’ve received an offer. I’m still finalizing my decision, but I wanted to be respectful and transparent. I’ll make sure anything I’m responsible for is handled properly.”

### What not to say
Avoid:

- Naming the competitor unless you choose to.
- Sharing the offer letter or exact compensation too early.
- Apologizing excessively.
- Saying “I’m definitely staying” if you’re not.
- Using it as a threat: “Match this or I’m gone.”

A strong default answer is:

> “Yes, I’ve been exploring opportunities. I haven’t made a final decision, and I’d prefer to keep the details private for now. I’m committed to doing my work professionally and will be transparent if anything changes.”

That is honest, measured, and protects your leverage.
