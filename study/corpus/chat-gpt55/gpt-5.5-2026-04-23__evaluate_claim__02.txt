Not exactly. **Standing desks aren’t a fitness “hack,” but they can do some useful things—mainly if they help you alternate positions and move more.**

What the evidence generally suggests:

- **They reduce sitting time**: Sit-stand desks often cut workplace sitting by roughly **30–120 minutes/day**, especially with reminders or workplace support.
- **They don’t burn many extra calories**: Standing burns only a little more than sitting, so it’s **unlikely to cause meaningful weight loss** by itself.
- **They may help some pain/discomfort**: Some people report less **lower-back, neck, or shoulder discomfort**, though results vary and ergonomics matter.
- **They may modestly improve blood sugar after meals**: Standing or light movement after eating can help compared with sitting, but **walking is better**.
- **They’re not a substitute for exercise**: Cardiovascular and metabolic benefits are much stronger from regular activity.

Also, **standing all day isn’t ideal** either. Prolonged standing can cause foot pain, leg fatigue, varicose vein issues, or back discomfort.

Best take: use it as a **sit-stand-move desk**, not a “standing desk.” A practical approach is:

- Alternate sitting and standing every **30–60 minutes**
- Take short walking/stretch breaks
- Keep monitor at eye level, elbows around 90°, wrists neutral
- Use supportive shoes or an anti-fatigue mat if standing

So your coworker is partly right if they mean “standing desks don’t replace exercise.” But “they don’t do anything” is too strong—they can reduce sedentary time and improve comfort for some people.
