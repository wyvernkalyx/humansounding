Practically speaking, both a VPN and a proxy can make your internet traffic appear to come from another location, but they work at different levels and provide different protections.

## Proxy

A proxy is an intermediary server that an app or browser connects through.

### What it usually does
- Changes your visible IP address for specific traffic
- Often works per app, per browser, or per protocol
- Common for web browsing, scraping, bypassing simple geo/IP restrictions

### What it usually does not do
- It does not necessarily encrypt your traffic
- It usually does not protect all apps on your device
- DNS requests may still go through your normal ISP unless configured carefully

### Common proxy types
- **HTTP proxy**: mainly for web traffic
- **SOCKS proxy**: more general-purpose, can carry many types of traffic
- **Transparent proxy**: often used by networks/ISPs; you may not configure it yourself

## VPN

A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a VPN server.

### What it usually does
- Routes most or all device traffic through the VPN
- Encrypts traffic between your device and the VPN provider
- Changes your visible IP address
- Usually handles DNS through the VPN to reduce leaks
- Protects traffic on untrusted Wi-Fi from local snooping

### What it does not do
- It does not make you anonymous by itself
- The VPN provider can potentially see metadata and, depending on traffic, some destination information
- It does not protect you from malware, phishing, or logging into identifiable accounts

## Practical comparison

| Feature | Proxy | VPN |
|---|---|---|
| Changes visible IP | Yes | Yes |
| Encrypts traffic | Sometimes, not guaranteed | Usually yes |
| Covers all apps | Usually no | Usually yes |
| Easy per-app use | Yes | Less commonly |
| Protects on public Wi-Fi | Limited | Better |
| Can hide activity from ISP | Partially, depending on setup | More effectively |
| Speed | Often faster/lighter | Can be slower due to encryption |
| Setup | Simple in browser/app | Usually system-level app/config |

## Simple analogy

A **proxy** is like asking someone else to fetch a website for you.

A **VPN** is like putting all your internet traffic into a private tunnel to another place, then letting it exit from there.

## Which should you use?

Use a **proxy** if you only need to:
- Change IP for one browser or app
- Access region-specific web content
- Route specific traffic through another server
- Do lightweight automation or testing

Use a **VPN** if you want to:
- Protect your connection on public Wi-Fi
- Route most device traffic through one encrypted tunnel
- Reduce what your ISP or local network can see
- Use a simpler “whole device” privacy setup

In short: **a proxy is usually for routing specific app traffic through another server; a VPN is usually for encrypting and routing most or all of your device’s traffic through another server.**
