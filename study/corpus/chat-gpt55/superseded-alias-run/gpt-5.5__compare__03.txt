A **controller** is primarily responsible for the company’s **accounting accuracy and financial reporting**.

A **CFO** is responsible for the company’s **overall financial strategy, planning, and capital decisions**.

Put simply:

> **The controller explains what happened. The CFO decides what to do next.**

### Controller: accounting and controls

A controller typically manages the accounting function. Their job is to make sure the numbers are correct, timely, and compliant.

Common controller responsibilities include:

- Closing the books monthly/quarterly/yearly
- Preparing financial statements
- Managing accounts payable and accounts receivable
- Payroll oversight
- Budget-to-actual reporting
- Internal controls
- Audit support
- Tax coordination
- Ensuring compliance with GAAP or other accounting standards

The controller is usually focused on **historical financial information**: revenue, expenses, cash balances, margins, and reporting accuracy.

### CFO: strategy and financial leadership

A CFO looks at the broader financial direction of the company. They use the accounting data, but their focus is more forward-looking.

Common CFO responsibilities include:

- Financial strategy
- Forecasting and budgeting
- Cash flow planning
- Fundraising or debt financing
- Investor and board communication
- M&A planning
- Pricing and margin strategy
- Risk management
- Banking relationships
- Long-term financial planning
- Helping the CEO make major business decisions

The CFO is focused on **future financial outcomes**: growth, profitability, capital needs, valuation, and financial risk.

### Hierarchy

In many companies, the controller reports to the CFO.

A typical finance structure might look like:

**CEO → CFO → Controller → Accounting team**

The CFO leads the finance function overall. The controller leads the accounting department specifically.

### Example

If sales were lower than expected last quarter:

- The **controller** makes sure the financial statements accurately show the revenue shortfall.
- The **CFO** analyzes why it happened, how it affects cash flow, whether costs need to be reduced, and what strategic changes the company should make.

### In smaller companies

The distinction can blur. A controller may handle CFO-like work, or a CFO may be deeply involved in accounting. Some companies also use a fractional CFO while having a full-time controller.

But the core difference remains:

| Role | Main Focus | Time Horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Controller | Accounting, reporting, compliance | Past and present |
| CFO | Strategy, forecasting, financing, decision support | Future |

So the real difference is that the **controller owns the integrity of the numbers**, while the **CFO owns the financial direction of the business**.
