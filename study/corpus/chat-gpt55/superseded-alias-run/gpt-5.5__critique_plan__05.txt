Strong instinct — but I’d frame it less as “cut meetings by half and see what breaks” and more as “remove low-value coordination load while protecting decision quality, alignment, and trust.”

Reactions:

1. **Good goal**
   - Most orgs have meeting bloat.
   - Cutting meetings forces clearer ownership, better writing, and fewer default syncs.
   - It can expose where meetings were compensating for unclear roles or weak processes.

2. **Risk: invisible damage**
   Some things may “break” quietly before they break visibly:
   - Cross-functional alignment
   - Morale and belonging
   - Early detection of risks
   - Mentoring/coaching
   - Decision transparency
   - Team trust, especially in remote/hybrid settings

3. **Don’t cut evenly**
   A blanket 50% cut may remove valuable meetings and preserve bad ones. Better categories:
   - **Kill**: status updates, low-agenda recurring meetings, meetings with no decisions.
   - **Shrink**: too many attendees, too long, too frequent.
   - **Protect**: decision forums, 1:1s, retros, planning, critical cross-functional syncs.

4. **Replace meetings with operating mechanisms**
   If meetings go away, something else needs to carry the load:
   - Written weekly updates
   - Decision logs
   - Clear DRIs
   - Async status dashboards
   - Slack/Teams norms
   - Office hours instead of recurring group meetings

5. **Run it as an experiment**
   I’d suggest:
   - Baseline current meeting hours per person/team.
   - Define what “breaks” means: slower decisions, missed handoffs, rework, employee sentiment, customer impact.
   - Cut for 4–6 weeks.
   - Review data and anecdotes.
   - Reintroduce only meetings that prove value.

6. **Give people permission to say no**
   The biggest unlock may be cultural:
   - No agenda, no meeting.
   - Optional means optional.
   - Default to 25/50 minutes.
   - Meeting owner must state desired outcome.
   - Attendees can decline if they’re not needed for input or decision.

My take: **yes, do it — but don’t make “50% fewer meetings” the real goal. Make “faster execution with less coordination tax” the goal.** Cutting meetings is the tactic.
