You have more options than “do it all” or “flake.” You can renegotiate the role.

### 1. Scale it way down
If you’re overwhelmed, simplify the reunion:
- Potluck instead of catering
- Park/picnic instead of rented venue
- One afternoon instead of a full weekend
- No elaborate games, shirts, decorations, etc.
- “Everyone books their own lodging/transportation”

**Script:**  
“Hi everyone — I’m happy to help make the reunion happen, but I need to keep it simple. I’m planning a low-key potluck/picnic rather than a big event.”

### 2. Ask for a planning committee
You don’t have to be the sole organizer. Assign concrete tasks:
- Venue
- Food
- RSVPs
- Budget/collecting money
- Activities
- Cleanup
- Lodging info

**Script:**  
“I realized this is too much for one person. I can coordinate, but I need help. I’m looking for 4 people to each take one task. If we don’t have volunteers by Friday, we’ll need to simplify or postpone.”

### 3. Hand off the role
If you truly don’t want to do it, it’s okay to step back—especially if you do it early.

**Script:**  
“I’m sorry, but I need to be honest: I can’t organize the reunion this year. I know I initially said yes, but my capacity has changed. I’m happy to share any info I’ve gathered, but someone else will need to take the lead.”

### 4. Postpone it
This is a good option if people want a reunion but planning has become rushed or stressful.

**Script:**  
“I don’t think we can do this well without more time and help. I suggest we postpone to a later date and form a small planning group.”

### 5. Set firm boundaries
If you keep the role, decide what you are *not* doing:
- No fronting money
- No chasing adults repeatedly
- No managing everyone’s travel drama
- No absorbing complaints without volunteers

**Script:**  
“I’m not able to front costs or individually coordinate travel. I’ll send the details, and everyone can make their own arrangements.”

### 6. Use a “minimum viable reunion”
Pick the simplest version that still counts:
- Date
- Location
- Food plan
- RSVP deadline
- One message thread/email

That’s it. The reunion does not need to be Pinterest-worthy.

### 7. Be honest but not overly apologetic
You can acknowledge the change without giving a huge explanation.

**Simple version:**  
“I overcommitted. I want to be fair to everyone and say now that I can’t take this on at the level expected.”

A good middle-ground approach might be: **send one message saying you need a co-organizer or committee by a specific date, otherwise you’ll either scale it down or step back.** That gives the family a chance to help instead of silently expecting you to carry it.
