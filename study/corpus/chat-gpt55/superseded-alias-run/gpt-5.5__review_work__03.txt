It’s not “wrong,” but it’s too generic to be effective. Phrases like **“results-driven professional”** and **“10+ years of experience”** could apply to almost anyone, and they don’t tell employers what you actually do, what you’re good at, or what results you’ve delivered.

A stronger resume summary should include:

- Your target role or field  
- Key skills or specialties  
- Specific achievements or measurable impact  
- Industry or functional experience  

Here are better versions depending on your background:

**General improved version:**  
> Experienced professional with 10+ years of success in [industry/function], specializing in [key skill], [key skill], and [key skill]. Proven ability to deliver measurable results, improve processes, and support business growth.

**More achievement-focused:**  
> [Job title/field] professional with 10+ years of experience leading [type of work], improving operational efficiency, and driving measurable business outcomes. Skilled in [skill], [skill], and [skill], with a track record of delivering results in fast-paced environments.

**Example for project management:**  
> Project management professional with 10+ years of experience leading cross-functional teams, streamlining operations, and delivering complex projects on time and within budget. Skilled in stakeholder management, process improvement, and strategic planning.

**Example for sales:**  
> Sales professional with 10+ years of experience building client relationships, exceeding revenue targets, and expanding market share. Skilled in consultative selling, account management, and pipeline development.

If you want the strongest version, replace the vague wording with your actual role, industry, top skills, and one or two measurable wins.
