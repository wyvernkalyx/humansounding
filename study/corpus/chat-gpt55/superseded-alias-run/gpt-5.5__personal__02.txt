You can be honest while keeping it kind and brief. The key is to avoid over-explaining or debating the business idea.

Here are a few options:

**Simple and polite:**
> “Thank you for thinking of me. I’m not comfortable investing, so I’m going to pass. I really wish you success with it.”

**If you want to preserve the friendship clearly:**
> “I value our friendship, and for that reason I don’t think it’s a good idea for me to mix money and the business. I’m going to pass on investing, but I’m rooting for you.”

**If she asks why:**
> “I’ve thought about it, and I don’t feel confident enough in the business to invest. I don’t want to give you a half-hearted yes or create tension between us later.”

**If you want to offer non-financial support:**
> “I can’t invest, but I’d be happy to help by giving feedback, sharing your launch, or introducing you to someone who might be useful.”

Try not to say, “I don’t think it will work,” unless she specifically asks for candid feedback. If she does, say it gently:

> “Since you’re asking honestly, I do have some concerns about the market/plan/numbers, and that’s why I’m not comfortable investing. But I respect that you’re pursuing it.”

A good final answer could be:

> “I’ve given it some thought, and I’m going to pass on investing. I value our friendship and don’t want money to complicate it. I’m wishing you the best and I’m happy to support in other ways if I can.”
