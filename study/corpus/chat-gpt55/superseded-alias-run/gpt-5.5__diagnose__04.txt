Use a structured “is it real, where is it happening, what changed?” investigation.

## 1. Confirm the drop is real

Before looking for product causes, rule out measurement issues.

Check:

- Did the activation definition change?
- Did event tracking break?
- Did the signup event or activation event get renamed?
- Are there missing events from a platform, browser, or app version?
- Did bot/spam filtering change?
- Is the drop visible in raw counts and rates?

Example:

> Signups are steady, activation dropped from 40% to 28%.  
> Is that because fewer users are completing the activation action, or because the event is no longer being recorded?

Compare multiple sources if possible: analytics tool, database, CRM, backend logs.

---

## 2. Clarify the exact metric

Write the activation metric in a precise way:

> Activation rate = % of new signups who complete `[activation event]` within `[time window]`.

For example:

- Create a project within 7 days
- Invite a teammate within 3 days
- Complete onboarding within 24 hours
- Connect an integration within 14 days

Then break activation into:

- Numerator: number activated
- Denominator: number signed up
- Time window: when you count activation

A common mistake is comparing users who have had the full activation window with users who have not.

For example, if activation is “within 7 days,” don’t judge users who signed up yesterday.

---

## 3. Break the funnel into steps

Activation usually isn’t one event. Map the path:

1. Signup
2. Email verification
3. Onboarding started
4. Onboarding completed
5. First key action
6. Activation achieved

Find where the drop happened.

Example:

| Step | Previous Month | Last Month |
|---|---:|---:|
| Signup → Email verified | 85% | 84% |
| Email verified → Onboarding started | 78% | 77% |
| Onboarding started → Completed | 70% | 45% |
| Completed → Activated | 60% | 59% |

In this case, the issue is likely inside onboarding.

---

## 4. Segment aggressively

A 30% activation drop is rarely uniform. Break it down by:

### Acquisition source

- Paid search
- Organic
- Referral
- SEO
- Affiliates
- Product-led invites
- Campaigns

Maybe signups are steady, but traffic mix changed.

Example:

| Channel | Signup Share Before | Signup Share Last Month | Activation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Organic | 50% | 30% | 45% |
| Paid social | 10% | 35% | 12% |

Overall activation drops even if each channel behaves normally.

### User type

- Company size
- Role/persona
- Use case
- Geography
- Device
- Browser
- Free vs trial
- Self-serve vs sales-assisted

### Product/platform

- Web vs mobile
- iOS vs Android
- App version
- Browser
- Operating system

### Signup cohort

Look at users by signup week or signup day. Did the drop start suddenly or gradually?

A sudden drop usually suggests:

- Product release
- Tracking issue
- Marketing campaign change
- Outage
- Pricing/paywall change

A gradual drop suggests:

- Traffic quality shift
- Market/seasonality
- onboarding degradation
- compounding technical issues

---

## 5. Look for changes around the drop date

Create a timeline of everything that changed before and during the decline.

Check:

- Product releases
- Onboarding changes
- Pricing or trial changes
- Signup flow changes
- Email verification changes
- Authentication changes
- New permissions or compliance screens
- Payment requirement changes
- Marketing campaigns
- Paid channel budget changes
- SEO landing page changes
- Sales qualification changes
- Analytics/instrumentation changes
- Major bugs or incidents
- Email deliverability issues

Ask: “What changed right before activation started falling?”

---

## 6. Compare cohorts before and after the drop

Take two cohorts:

- Users who signed up before the drop
- Users who signed up after the drop

Compare:

- Time to first key action
- Completion of onboarding steps
- Error rates
- Email open/click rates
- Session frequency
- Number of support tickets
- Rage clicks or dead clicks
- Drop-off pages
- Feature adoption

You want to find a behavioral difference, not just a metric difference.

---

## 7. Check user quality

Since signups are steady, activation may have dropped because signup quality changed.

Look at:

- Lead source
- Campaign
- Keywords
- Landing pages
- Geography
- Email domains
- Company size
- Job title
- Intent signals
- Spam/fake accounts
- Disposable emails

Questions to answer:

- Did a low-intent campaign replace a high-intent campaign?
- Did paid traffic increase while organic decreased?
- Did one country or segment start driving more signups?
- Did a new landing page overpromise or attract the wrong audience?

---

## 8. Inspect the onboarding/product experience

If the drop is in the product funnel, investigate friction.

Look for:

- Increased page load times
- More errors
- Broken integrations
- Confusing UI changes
- Required steps added
- Email verification delays
- Calendar/integration/API failures
- Permissions issues
- Mobile responsiveness issues
- Empty states with no clear next step

Use:

- Session recordings
- Heatmaps
- Support tickets
- In-app feedback
- Error logs
- Customer success notes
- New-user interviews

---

## 9. Check lifecycle emails and notifications

Activation often depends on emails.

Investigate:

- Did onboarding emails stop sending?
- Did deliverability decline?
- Did open/click rates drop?
- Did SPF/DKIM/DMARC change?
- Were emails delayed?
- Did links break?
- Did a nurture sequence get paused?
- Did email verification land in spam?

A silent email issue can easily create a large activation drop.

---

## 10. Quantify impact by segment

Once you find suspicious segments, measure contribution to the drop.

For each segment:

> Impact = change in activation rate × number of signups

You’re looking for the segment that explains most of the total decline.

Example:

| Segment | Signups | Activation Before | Activation After | Lost Activations |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Paid social | 2,000 | 25% | 10% | 300 |
| Organic | 3,000 | 45% | 43% | 60 |
| Referral | 1,000 | 55% | 52% | 30 |

Paid social explains most of the problem.

---

## 11. Form hypotheses and test them

Examples:

### Hypothesis 1  
“Activation dropped because paid social traffic increased and has lower intent.”

Test:

- Compare activation by channel before/after
- Compare landing pages and campaigns
- Check keyword/ad changes

### Hypothesis 2  
“Activation dropped because onboarding step 3 broke after a release.”

Test:

- Funnel by onboarding step
- Error logs
- Session recordings
- App version comparison

### Hypothesis 3  
“Activation dropped because verification emails went to spam.”

Test:

- Email delivery rate
- Open/click rate
- Support tickets mentioning verification
- Signup → email verified conversion

---

## 12. Prioritized investigation order

If you need to move fast, do this:

1. Validate tracking and metric definition.
2. Plot activation rate by signup day/week.
3. Break activation funnel into steps.
4. Segment by acquisition channel.
5. Segment by platform/device/geography.
6. Overlay product/marketing changes on the timeline.
7. Inspect the biggest drop-off segment with session recordings, logs, and support tickets.
8. Estimate which segment explains most lost activations.
9. Fix or run a targeted experiment.
10. Monitor recovery by cohort.

---

## Simple diagnostic question set

Ask these in order:

1. Is the activation event still firing correctly?
2. Did the drop happen suddenly or gradually?
3. Which funnel step got worse?
4. Which segment got worse?
5. Did traffic mix change?
6. Did product/onboarding change?
7. Did email or integration behavior change?
8. Which issue explains the most lost activations?
9. Can we reproduce the problem as a new user?
10. What is the smallest fix or experiment to validate the cause?

---

## Likely causes to check first

Given “signups steady, activation down 30%,” the most common causes are:

1. Tracking/instrumentation issue
2. Acquisition mix changed to lower-intent users
3. Onboarding or signup flow bug
4. Email verification or lifecycle email problem
5. Recent product release introduced friction
6. Activation window/cohort calculation issue
7. New users from a specific platform, device, or geography are failing
8. Landing page or campaign mismatch

Start by proving whether the drop is real, then isolate it by funnel step and segment.
