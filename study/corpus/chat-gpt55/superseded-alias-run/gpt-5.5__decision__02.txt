If the work is **core to your product and likely to continue for 6–12+ months**, hire a second developer. If the work is **temporary, specialized, or unpredictable**, use contractors.

My default recommendation: **hire the second developer if you have enough ongoing work to keep them busy and you need product/context ownership. Use contractors only to supplement specific projects.**

### Hire a second developer if:
- You have a steady backlog of engineering work.
- The work requires deep knowledge of your codebase, product, customers, or architecture.
- You are losing momentum because one developer is a bottleneck.
- You need better continuity, maintenance, support, and accountability.
- You want to build long-term engineering capacity.

**Pros:** better knowledge retention, stronger ownership, easier collaboration, lower long-term cost per hour.  
**Cons:** slower hiring process, fixed cost, management overhead.

### Use contractors if:
- The workload is temporary or uncertain.
- You need specialized skills: DevOps, security, UI polish, data migration, mobile, etc.
- You need speed now but cannot commit to a full-time salary.
- The work can be well-scoped and handed off cleanly.
- You lack enough ongoing work for a full-time person.

**Pros:** flexible, fast, good for bursts or specialized projects.  
**Cons:** context switching, less ownership, possible quality/maintenance issues, higher hourly cost.

### Simple decision rule

Ask: “Will we still need this person full-time 9 months from now?”

- **Yes** → hire.
- **No / not sure** → contractors.
- **Mostly yes, but also some specialist needs** → hire one developer and use contractors selectively.

### Cost comparison

A contractor may cost more hourly, but avoids benefits, payroll tax, recruiting, onboarding, and long-term commitment. A full-time developer usually becomes cheaper if utilization is high.

A rough break-even:

> If you need more than ~25–30 hours/week of ongoing development for many months, hiring usually wins.

### Best practical option

For many teams, the strongest approach is:

1. **Hire a second developer for core product work.**
2. **Use contractors for contained, specialized, or deadline-driven projects.**
3. Make sure the employee owns the architecture/codebase and contractors contribute under their guidance.

That gives you long-term capacity without losing flexibility.
