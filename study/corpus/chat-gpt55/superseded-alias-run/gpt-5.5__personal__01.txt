Don’t lie, but you also don’t need to give details. The best answer depends on whether you might stay.

### If you’re open to staying
Say something like:

> “Yes, I’ve been exploring an opportunity. I wasn’t planning to raise it until I had clarity, but since you asked directly, I want to be transparent. I’ve received an offer and I’m taking some time to evaluate it. I value my role here, but I’m also thinking carefully about growth, compensation, and next steps.”

If you want to invite a retention conversation:

> “I’m not looking to create pressure, but if there’s a path here that addresses some of the things I’m weighing, I’d be open to discussing it.”

### If you’ve decided to leave
Keep it simple:

> “Yes, I’ve been in discussions with another company and I’ve received an offer. I haven’t made any formal announcement yet because I wanted to handle it professionally. I’ll keep you updated once I’ve made my final decision.”

Or, if you’re ready to resign:

> “Yes, I’ve accepted another offer. I wanted to speak with you directly and handle the transition professionally. My last day would be [date].”

### If you don’t want to disclose much
You can be honest but vague:

> “I have been considering my longer-term options. I’m not ready to discuss specifics yet, but I’ll be transparent with you once there’s something final.”

### What not to say
Avoid:
- “No” if you are — that can damage trust.
- Sharing the company name, offer amount, or details unless you intentionally want a counteroffer.
- Sounding apologetic. Exploring opportunities is normal.

A strong middle-ground answer is:

> “Yes, I have been exploring an opportunity and I’ve received an offer. I’m still evaluating it, and I wanted to be thoughtful before bringing it up. I appreciate you asking directly, and I’m happy to discuss what I’m weighing if that would be useful.”

That keeps you honest, professional, and in control of the conversation.
