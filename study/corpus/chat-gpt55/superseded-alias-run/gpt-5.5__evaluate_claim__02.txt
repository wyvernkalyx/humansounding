Not exactly. **Standing desks can help, but they’re not a magic health upgrade by themselves.**

What the evidence generally suggests:

- **They reduce sitting time** (often by ~30–120 minutes/day), especially when paired with reminders or habits.
- **Standing burns only a little more energy than sitting**, so it’s unlikely to cause meaningful weight loss on its own.
- **They may help some people with back/neck discomfort**, especially if they alternate positions and improve ergonomics.
- **They can modestly improve blood sugar spikes after meals** compared with sitting all day, but walking breaks work better.
- **Standing all day isn’t ideal either**—prolonged standing can cause foot/leg pain, fatigue, and may worsen some vein issues.

The best version is a **sit-stand-move desk** approach:

- Alternate sitting and standing.
- Take short walking/mobility breaks.
- Aim for movement every 30–60 minutes.
- Use good ergonomics: monitor at eye level, elbows around 90°, wrists neutral, feet supported/comfortable mat if standing.

So your coworker is partly right if they mean **“standing still for hours won’t transform your health.”** But they’re wrong if they mean **“standing desks do nothing.”** They can reduce sedentary time and improve comfort for some people—just not as much as regular movement does.
