Yes — it’s generally true: **don’t put a hot laptop battery or a hot laptop in the fridge/freezer.**

Why not:

- **Condensation can form** when you take it back out, and moisture can short electronics or cause corrosion.
- **Rapid temperature changes** can stress the battery and internal components.
- A fridge/freezer doesn’t fix the cause of overheating and may make the device unsafe to use afterward.
- If the battery is damaged, you don’t want a potentially leaking or failing lithium-ion battery near food or in an enclosed appliance.

What to do instead:

1. **Shut the laptop down** and unplug the charger.
2. Put it on a **hard, nonflammable surface** with good airflow.
3. Let it cool naturally at room temperature; a **fan** is fine.
4. Don’t charge or use it again until it has cooled.

If the battery is **swollen, hissing, smoking, smells sweet/chemical, leaking, or extremely hot**, stop using it immediately. Move it away from flammable materials if safe, and contact the manufacturer/repair shop or emergency services if there’s smoke/fire risk.

If it’s just warm from normal use, that’s usually fine. If it’s repeatedly getting very hot, the battery or cooling system may need service.
