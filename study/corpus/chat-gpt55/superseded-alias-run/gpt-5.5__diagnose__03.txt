A drop from “all day” to ~3 hours in a month usually means one of two things:

1. **Something is consuming much more power than before** — most common if the change was sudden.
2. **The battery has degraded or developed a fault** — more likely if the laptop is older, runs hot, or the battery health is poor.

### Common causes

#### 1. A background app or update is draining power
Recent OS updates, browser tabs, cloud sync, antivirus scans, game launchers, AI/photo indexing, or stuck processes can massively reduce battery life.

**Check:**
- **Windows:** Settings → System → Power & battery → Battery usage  
- **macOS:** System Settings → Battery, and Activity Monitor → Energy

Look for apps with unusually high battery/energy usage.

#### 2. Power settings changed
Your laptop may have switched to a higher performance mode.

Check whether it’s using:
- “Best performance” instead of “Balanced” or “Battery saver”
- High screen brightness
- Keyboard backlight on
- Discrete GPU enabled
- High refresh rate display mode, e.g. 120Hz/144Hz

#### 3. Battery health has dropped
Batteries wear out over charge cycles and heat exposure. If the battery’s maximum capacity has fallen significantly, runtime will drop.

**Check battery health:**

**Windows:**
1. Open Command Prompt
2. Run:
   ```cmd
   powercfg /batteryreport
   ```
3. Open the generated report and compare:
   - **Design Capacity**
   - **Full Charge Capacity**

If Full Charge Capacity is much lower than Design Capacity, the battery is worn.

**macOS:**
- System Settings → Battery → Battery Health  
or hold Option and click the battery icon on older macOS versions.

If it says **Service Recommended**, the battery likely needs replacement.

#### 4. Heat is accelerating drain
If fans are running more than usual or the laptop feels hot, the CPU/GPU may be working hard or the battery may be stressed.

Common causes:
- Dust buildup
- Blocked vents
- Malware or runaway processes
- Using laptop on a bed/blanket
- Recent driver or firmware issue

#### 5. Battery calibration/reporting issue
Sometimes the battery estimate becomes inaccurate. A calibration cycle can help the percentage reading, though it won’t restore actual lost capacity.

You can try:
1. Charge to 100%
2. Use normally until it drops low, around 5–10%
3. Charge uninterrupted back to 100%

Don’t do this frequently; it’s just for recalibration.

### Warning signs
Stop using the laptop and get it serviced if you notice:
- Swelling or bulging
- Trackpad/keyboard lifting
- Battery smell, hissing, or heat while off
- Sudden shutdowns at high percentage
- Charger or battery getting abnormally hot

### What I’d do first
1. Check battery health/capacity.
2. Check battery usage by app.
3. Restart and install pending OS/driver updates.
4. Set power mode to Balanced/Battery Saver.
5. Reduce brightness and disable high refresh rate if available.
6. If battery health is poor, plan for a battery replacement.

If the battery health still shows good capacity, this is probably a software or power-management issue rather than the battery itself.
