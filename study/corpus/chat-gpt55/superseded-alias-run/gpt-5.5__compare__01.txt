Practically speaking, both can make your traffic appear to come from another IP address, but they work at different levels and protect different amounts of traffic.

## VPN

A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and the VPN provider.

### Practical effects
- **Covers most or all apps on your device**: browser, games, email apps, messaging apps, etc.
- **Encrypts traffic between you and the VPN server**: useful on public Wi‑Fi or untrusted networks.
- **Hides your IP address from websites and services**: they see the VPN server’s IP.
- **Can hide browsing activity from your ISP/local network**: they can see you are using a VPN, but not the specific sites as easily.
- **Usually handles DNS through the VPN** if configured properly.

### Common uses
- Safer browsing on public Wi‑Fi.
- Privacy from ISP or local network monitoring.
- Accessing region-restricted content.
- Routing all device traffic through another country or network.

### Downsides
- Can reduce speed.
- Some sites block VPNs.
- You must trust the VPN provider, because they can potentially see a lot of your traffic metadata and sometimes destination info.

## Proxy

A proxy is an intermediate server that your traffic goes through, usually for a specific app or protocol.

### Practical effects
- **Often only affects one app**, like your browser, unless configured system-wide.
- **May or may not encrypt traffic**:
  - An HTTP proxy does not inherently encrypt everything.
  - A SOCKS proxy just forwards traffic and usually does not encrypt by itself.
  - HTTPS traffic remains encrypted between your browser and the website, but the proxy still sees where you connect.
- **Hides your IP from the destination site** for traffic using the proxy.
- **Does not necessarily protect other apps** on your device.
- **DNS may leak** depending on the app/proxy setup.

### Common uses
- Changing IP for a specific browser or app.
- Scraping, automation, testing, or routing only certain traffic.
- Bypassing simple network restrictions.
- Using a company or school web proxy.

### Downsides
- Less comprehensive protection.
- Often weaker privacy/security than a VPN.
- Easy to misconfigure so some traffic bypasses it.

---

## Simple comparison

| Feature | VPN | Proxy |
|---|---|---|
| Covers whole device | Usually yes | Usually no |
| Encrypts tunnel | Yes | Usually no, unless combined with encryption |
| Hides IP | Yes | Yes, for proxied traffic |
| Protects on public Wi‑Fi | Better | Limited |
| App-specific routing | Less convenient, though possible | Good |
| Speed | Can be slower | Often faster |
| Setup | Usually simple app | Depends on app/system |

---

## Short version

Use a **VPN** when you want broad privacy/security for your whole device or network.

Use a **proxy** when you only need to route a specific app or browser through another IP and do not need full-device encryption.

Neither makes you completely anonymous. You still have to trust the VPN/proxy provider, and websites can still track you through logins, cookies, browser fingerprints, and other identifiers.
