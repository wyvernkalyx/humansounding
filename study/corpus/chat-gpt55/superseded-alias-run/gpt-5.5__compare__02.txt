For someone who moves every four years, **renting is often financially safer and simpler**, but buying can still make sense in certain markets or personal situations.

## Key comparison

| Factor | Renting | Buying |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Usually security deposit + first month’s rent | Down payment, closing costs, inspections, moving costs |
| Flexibility | High — easier to move | Lower — selling takes time and money |
| Monthly cost | Rent, utilities, renter’s insurance | Mortgage, taxes, insurance, HOA, maintenance |
| Maintenance | Landlord usually responsible | Owner pays repairs and upkeep |
| Equity building | None | Some equity, but early mortgage payments are mostly interest |
| Transaction costs | Low | High — buying and selling can cost thousands |
| Risk | Rent can rise; no ownership upside | Home value can fall; unexpected repairs |
| Time horizon | Good for short stays | Usually better for longer stays |

## Why four years matters

Buying a home has large transaction costs:

- Buyer closing costs: often **2%–5%** of purchase price  
- Seller costs when you move: often **5%–8%**, largely agent commissions and closing costs  
- Maintenance: commonly estimated at **1%–2% of home value per year**
- Early mortgage payments mostly go toward interest, not principal

Because of these costs, homeowners often need several years of appreciation and equity-building to break even. A common rule of thumb is that buying tends to make more sense if you stay **5–7+ years**, though this varies by market.

For a four-year stay, you may sell before the financial benefits of ownership outweigh the costs.

## When renting is likely better

Renting is usually better if:

- You expect to move in about four years or less
- Home prices are high relative to rents
- You may need flexibility for work, family, or lifestyle
- You do not want repair or maintenance risk
- You would invest the down payment elsewhere
- The local housing market is flat or uncertain

Renting also avoids the risk of needing to sell during a weak market.

## When buying could still make sense

Buying may be better if:

- The mortgage payment is close to or lower than rent
- You buy in a strong appreciation market
- You can afford all transaction costs comfortably
- You might keep the property and rent it out after moving
- You value stability, control, or customization
- You qualify for favorable financing
- You are buying below market value or in a very desirable location

But if you plan to sell after four years, the home likely needs to appreciate enough to cover closing costs, selling costs, repairs, taxes, and the opportunity cost of your down payment.

## Simple rule of thumb

If you move every four years:

- **Renting is usually the better default choice**
- **Buying only makes sense if the numbers are clearly favorable**, or if you are willing to become a landlord after moving

## Best way to decide

Compare the total four-year cost of each option:

### Renting cost
Rent × 48 months  
+ renter’s insurance  
+ expected rent increases  
− investment returns on money you would have used for down payment/closing costs

### Buying cost
Mortgage interest  
+ property taxes  
+ homeowners insurance  
+ HOA fees  
+ maintenance/repairs  
+ buying closing costs  
+ selling costs  
− equity built  
− expected appreciation

If buying does not clearly beat renting after all costs, renting is probably the wiser choice for a four-year mover.
