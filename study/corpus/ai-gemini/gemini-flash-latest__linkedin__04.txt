Here are a couple of options, depending on your preferred style:

### Option 1: Story-driven & Thought Leadership (High Engagement)

You can teach a skill in 30 days. 
You can’t fix a bad attitude in 3 years.

Early in my career, I made the mistake of hiring strictly for the resume. 

I looked for the exact keywords: 
• 5+ years of tool X
• Prestigious past companies
• Perfect pedigree

The result? Someone who had all the answers, zero curiosity, and completely drained team morale.

Since then, my hiring philosophy has completely flipped. 

Today, I look for:
1. **Coachability:** How do they handle constructive feedback?
2. **Curiosity:** Do they ask insightful questions, or just wait for their turn to speak?
3. **Resilience:** How do they talk about past failures?
4. **Energy:** Do they lift the room up, or pull it down?

Hard skills have an expiration date—especially with how fast technology is moving. 

A candidate who is hungry, adaptable, and a culture-add will outperform a complacent "expert" every single time.

Resumes tell you what someone *did*. 
Attitude tells you what they *will do*.

Hire for character. Train for skill.

Agree or disagree? How do you assess "attitude" in an interview?

### Option 2: Short, Punchy & Direct

A reminder for hiring managers this week:

Skills = The Baseline
Experience = The History
Attitude = The Multiplier

A candidate with:
❌ 10/10 Experience + 2/10 Attitude = A toxic work environment.
✅ 6/10 Experience + 10/10 Attitude = A future leader.

You can train someone on software, processes, and industry jargon. 

You cannot train someone to:
• Care about their teammates
• Take accountability for mistakes
• Be genuinely curious
• Bring positive energy to work

Stop looking for the "perfect" resume. Start looking for the right mindset.
