**Subject:** The 5:00 AM myth (and how to actually fix your mornings)
**Preview:** Why most morning routines fail by Thursday—and the 3-step framework to build one that lasts.

We’ve all been there. On Sunday night, we are inspired. We set our alarms for 5:30 AM, determined to become a completely new person by sunrise. 

By Tuesday, we’re hitting snooze. By Thursday, we’re scrolling TikTok in bed, running late, and washing down a piece of burnt toast with cold coffee while feeling like a failure.

Here is the truth: **Your morning routine keeps failing not because you lack discipline, but because you designed it for a robot, not a human.**

If you want a morning routine that actually sticks for months (and years), you need to stop optimizing for *intensity* and start optimizing for *sustainability*.

Here is the 3-step framework to build a routine that survives real life:

---

### 1. Identify Your "Anchor Habit"
Most people try to change five things at once. Instead, pick **one** non-negotiable anchor that sets the tone for your day. 

An anchor habit is simple, high-leverage, and makes everything else easier. 
* It could be drinking a full glass of water before looking at your phone.
* It could be sitting on the porch for 5 minutes of quiet before the kids wake up.
* It could be writing down your Top 3 priorities for the day.

Master *one* anchor for two weeks before you earn the right to add a second step.

### 2. Build a "Minimum Viable Routine" (MVR)
Life happens. You’ll have late nights, sick kids, bad sleep, or early meetings. If your routine requires 90 minutes of uninterrupted peace, it will break the moment life gets messy.

Create two versions of your routine:
* **The Ideal Routine (45–60 mins):** What you do on normal, smooth days.
* **The Minimum Viable Routine (5–10 mins):** What you do on low-energy, chaotic days.

Your MVR might just be: *Splash cold water on face, drink water, write down the single most important task of the day.* 

By having an MVR, you never break the streak. You preserve the habit of showing up for yourself, even when you only have five minutes.

### 3. Move the Starting Line to the Night Before
Your morning routine doesn’t start when your eyes open; it starts when you turn off the lights the night before.

Friction is the enemy of morning motivation. You have the least willpower of your entire day in the first 10 minutes after waking up. Reduce decision fatigue by preparing the night before:
* Set your coffee machine on a timer (or prep the beans).
* Put your workout clothes directly in your line of sight.
* Put your phone across the room so you have to stand up to turn off the alarm.

---

### The Takeaway
The goal of a morning routine isn't to join the "5 AM Club" or win a productivity contest. 

**The goal is simply to start your day on offense instead of defense.**

You don't need a 2-hour ritual. You just need 10 intentional minutes that belong entirely to you before the demands of the world rush in.
