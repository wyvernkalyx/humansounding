### Option 1: Bold & Results-Driven (Best for high engagement)

We cut 20% of our working hours. 

Our productivity didn’t drop. It went up. 

A few months ago, our team made the leap to a **4-day work week** (100% pay, 80% time, 100% output). 

Here is why we did it—and what actually happened:

**1. Hours worked $\neq$ value created**
The 40-hour work week was designed for factory floors a century ago. In knowledge work, peak creativity and problem-solving don't happen when people are running on fumes. 

**2. It forced us to eliminate "work about work"**
We didn’t just squeeze 40 hours into 32. We audited our calendar. We killed pointless status meetings, cut down on Slack noise, and prioritized asynchronous communication. 

**3. Well-rested teams solve problems faster**
When Friday became a recovery day, Mondays stopped feeling dreadful. Our team comes back energized, sharper, and ready to tackle complex challenges.

The biggest lesson? Output is about **focus, not endurance.**

A 4-day week isn't a perk. It’s an operating model that respects human energy.

### Option 2: Culture & People-First (Best for talent attraction/recruitment)

Burnout is not a badge of honor. 

We talk a lot in the corporate world about "work-life balance," but balance is hard to achieve when work consumes 5 out of 7 days every single week.

That’s why our team officially transitioned to a **4-day work week**. 🌿

Why we made the change:
* **Trust over micromanagement:** We trust our team to manage their time and hit their goals, regardless of the hours logged.
* **Mental health matters:** An extra day off gives our team real time to unplug, be with their families, pursue hobbies, and recharge.
* **Creativity needs breathing room:** Our best ideas rarely happen when we’re exhausted at 4:30 PM on a Friday.

The result? 
Higher team morale, zero drop in output, and a team that genuinely loves showing up on Monday morning. 

Work should support life, not the other way around.
