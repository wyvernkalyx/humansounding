## Summary

Beginning **[Month Day, Year]**, all staff based at or assigned to a [Company] office will work on-site a minimum of **three days per week**. This replaces our current [two-day / flexible / fully remote] arrangement.

## Why we're making this change

Over the past [X months], we've heard consistent feedback — and seen it in our own project data — that certain work goes better when we're together: onboarding new colleagues, cross-team problem-solving, mentoring, and early-stage creative work. A three-day baseline gives us reliable overlap while preserving meaningful flexibility for focused, independent work.

To be clear: this is not a response to concerns about productivity. Our teams have delivered strong results. This is about building the connective tissue that's harder to grow at a distance.

## What this means in practice

**Core days.** [Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday] are designated core days across the company, so you can count on your colleagues being present. Teams with operational reasons to vary this should agree on an alternative pattern with their department head by **[date]**.

**Your third day.** [If only two core days are set: Choose your third day in coordination with your manager, and keep it consistent so teammates know when to find you.]

**Meetings.** Managers are asked to schedule collaborative sessions — planning, workshops, one-on-ones, team meetings — on core days, and to protect remote days for focused work wherever possible.

**Measuring it.** Attendance will be reviewed as a monthly average, not day by day. Travel, client visits, offsite work, and approved leave all count toward your on-site days. We're not looking to police badge swipes; we're asking for good-faith consistency.
