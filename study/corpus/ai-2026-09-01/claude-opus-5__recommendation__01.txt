**A few notes for strengthening it:**

- **Specifics beat adjectives.** One concrete anecdote ("she found the error in the published dataset") does more work than three sentences of praise.
- **Comparative statements carry weight.** Committees read hundreds of letters; "top 3 of the ~200 students I've taught" is far more informative than "outstanding."
- **Name the relationship's depth honestly.** If you only had the student in one large lecture, say so — an over-claimed letter reads as hollow. It's better to be a credible modest voice than an implausible superlative one.
- **Address the specific program.** A sentence naming faculty or resources signals that the application is considered rather than scattershot.
- **Length:** one to two pages. Two full pages is standard for a research-oriented Ph.D. application.
