## Tips for customizing this

**Do this before you send it:**
- **Replace every bracket.** A letter with `[Company Name]` left in it goes straight to the reject pile.
- **Mirror the job posting's language.** If they ask for "React and TypeScript," use those exact words rather than "front-end frameworks." Many companies screen with keyword-matching software.
- **Lead with a real result in paragraph two.** Numbers beat adjectives: "reduced load time by 40%" is far stronger than "improved performance significantly."
- **Make paragraph four specific.** If your reason for wanting the job could apply to any company, cut it and research something real. Their engineering blog, GitHub org, or recent product launch are good sources.

**Common pitfalls for junior applicants:**
- Don't apologize for lacking experience ("Although I only have six months of..."). State what you *have* done.
- Don't just restate your résumé. Use the letter to add context and narrative.
- Keep it to one page — roughly 250–400 words.
- Always link your GitHub or portfolio. For junior roles, demonstrated code is often the strongest signal you can send.
