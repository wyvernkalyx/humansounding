Here's a cover letter you can adapt. I've used brackets for details to swap in — the more specific you get, the stronger it reads.

---

[Your Name]
[City, State] | [Phone] | [Email] | [LinkedIn or portfolio]

[Date]

[Hiring Manager Name]
[Organization Name]
[Address]

Dear [Hiring Manager Name],

When I read that [Organization] served [specific number/population — e.g., "3,200 families through its housing stability program last year"], I thought about [brief, genuine connection — a volunteer experience, a personal tie to the cause, or why the work matters to you]. I'd welcome the chance to bring that same commitment to the Marketing Coordinator role, along with [X] years of experience turning mission-driven work into stories that move people to act.

In my current role as [Title] at [Organization], I [concrete accomplishment with a number — e.g., "grew our email list 38% in a year by rebuilding the welcome series and segmenting donors by giving history"]. I also [second accomplishment — e.g., "managed the content calendar across four social channels, increasing average engagement rate from 1.2% to 3.4%"]. Working with a [small team/limited budget] taught me to prioritize ruthlessly and to measure what actually matters: not vanity metrics, but donations, sign-ups, and program enrollment.

What draws me to [Organization] specifically is [specific detail — a recent campaign, a program model, a strategic plan priority, their approach to community voice]. [One sentence on how your skills connect to that.] I'm comfortable writing donor appeals one hour and pulling a Google Analytics report the next, and I understand that nonprofit marketing means serving program staff, development, and the communities you work with — often all in the same week.

My toolkit includes [list 4-6 relevant tools: e.g., Canva, Adobe Creative Suite, Mailchimp, Salesforce NPSP, WordPress, Meta Business Suite, Google Analytics]. I've attached [portfolio/writing samples] so you can see the work directly.

Thank you for considering my application. I'd love to talk about how I can help [Organization] reach [specific goal from the job posting or their strategic plan].

Sincerely,
[Your Name]

---

**A few tips for customizing:**

- **Replace the opening hook with something real.** Generic mission enthusiasm is the most common weakness in nonprofit cover letters. A specific program, statistic, or personal connection sets you apart immediately.
- **Numbers beat adjectives.** "Grew engagement" is forgettable; "grew engagement 3x in six months" isn't.
- **Mirror the posting's language.** If they say "storytelling" or "brand voice," use those words — both for the human reader and any applicant tracking system.
- **Keep it to one page.** Aim for 300–400 words.
- **Find the hiring manager's name** on LinkedIn or the staff page if the posting doesn't list one. "Dear Hiring Team" is an acceptable fallback; "To Whom It May Concern" isn't.
