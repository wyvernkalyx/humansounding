**"Hire for attitude, train for skill" is good advice that most people apply badly.**

The mistake? Treating enthusiasm as attitude.

Enthusiasm is what candidates perform in interviews. Attitude is what shows up in month four, when the project is behind and nobody's watching.

What I actually mean by attitude:

→ How someone responds when they're wrong
→ Whether they ask when they're lost, or guess and hope
→ What they do with feedback in week 2 vs. week 20
→ Whether they make the people around them better, or just make themselves look good

Experience tells you what someone has already survived. Attitude tells you how they'll handle the thing none of us saw coming.

The catch: attitude is *harder* to hire for, not easier. A résumé is legible. Character isn't. So we default to the proxy we can measure and call it rigour.

A few things that help:
• Ask for a story about a failure — then listen for who they blame
• Give a real problem, not a hypothetical, and watch how they handle not knowing
• Ask their references what the person was like on a bad week

And the honest caveat: experience isn't nothing. For some roles, pattern recognition earned over a decade is the whole job. The point isn't to ignore experience — it's to stop letting it be the only thing you can see.
