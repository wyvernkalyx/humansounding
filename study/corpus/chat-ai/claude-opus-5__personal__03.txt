Eleven years doesn't settle it either way — some people are eleven years into work that keeps opening up, and some are eleven years into a loop.

A few things that tend to distinguish them:

Stability usually feels like a floor. Stuck usually feels like a ceiling. If the thought of year twelve is mostly neutral or fine, that's different from a small dread, or a flinch.

Another tell: are you still learning things that are hard, or have you mostly gotten very good at things that used to be hard? Both are real, but the second one can start to feel like performing competence rather than building it.

And there's the fear question. If nothing were at stake — no mortgage, no ramp-up, no starting over as the new person — would you still be there? If yes, that's a real choice you keep making. If no, the staying is being done by something other than preference.

Worth saying too: the question itself is data, though not conclusive. People who are genuinely settled don't usually turn it over. But sometimes the question gets triggered from outside — a friend's promotion, a birthday, someone asking what's next at a party — and it doesn't actually reflect anything wrong.

What kicked this up? And when you imagine still being there in three years — what's the first feeling, before you start reasoning about it?
