It's doing very little work for you, and it's occupying the single most-read line on the page.

Three specific problems:

**"Passionate experts" is unfalsifiable.** No company describes itself as a team of indifferent amateurs. Because the opposite claim is unthinkable, the claim itself carries no information — a reader's eye slides right off it. Same class of phrase as "quality service" and "innovative solutions."

**The founding date is doing the wrong job.** 2019 is useful evidence (you've survived six years, you're not a weekend project), but as an *opener* it answers a question nobody asked yet. Dates work better as supporting proof further down, or in a timeline.

**It's about you, not the reader.** Worth remembering why people visit an About page: it's almost always a trust check. They're mid-consideration and want to know whether you're real, competent, and likely to still exist next year. Adjectives don't settle that. Specifics do.

Also, minor: "Founded in 2019, we are…" technically says *you* were founded in 2019. Defensible for a team, but plenty of editors would flag it.

Some patterns that work better:

- **Lead with a number that implies expertise instead of claiming it.** "We've moved 4,000 households across the Pennines. About 30 of them in snow."
- **Lead with the origin problem.** "Our founder spent three years trying to buy a decent replacement part for a 1994 tractor. That turned out to be a business."
- **Lead with a position.** "Most project management tools are built for the person running the project. We build for everyone else on it."

All three demonstrate passion and expertise rather than asserting them, which is the move you want. The reader concludes it themselves, so it sticks.
