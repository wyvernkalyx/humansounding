It's not fine. Here's the problem, bluntly:

**"Results-driven professional"** says nothing. Nobody writes "process-indifferent amateur." It's a phrase that survives on resumes because it *sounds* like content while committing to zero information. Same with "10+ years of experience" — experience doing what? A recruiter scanning for six seconds learns only that you've been employed for a decade, which they can get from your work history anyway.

A summary earns its space at the top of the page only if it tells the reader three things fast:

1. **What you are** (specific role/discipline, not "professional")
2. **What you've actually done** (a number, a scope, a named outcome)
3. **What you're aiming at** (implicitly — the job you're applying to)

**Template:**
> [Specific title] with [X] years in [industry/domain], specializing in [1–2 concrete skills]. [Achievement with a number]. Seeking [type of role] where I can [specific contribution].

**Examples of the same person, rewritten:**

- *Supply chain:* "Supply chain manager with 12 years in consumer goods logistics. Cut fulfillment costs 18% across a 40-site network by renegotiating carrier contracts and rebuilding the routing model."

- *Marketing:* "B2B content marketer with 10 years in SaaS. Grew organic traffic from 15K to 220K monthly sessions and built the demand-gen function at two Series B startups."

- *Nursing:* "ICU nurse with 11 years in Level I trauma centers. Precepted 30+ new graduate nurses and led the unit's sepsis protocol rollout, cutting time-to-antibiotics by 22 minutes."

Notice none of them use an adjective about personality. The numbers do that work.

**Two other things worth knowing:**

- If your resume is one page and tight, consider cutting the summary entirely. A weak summary is worse than none — it burns prime real estate. A strong one is worth it mainly when you're career-changing, or when your title history doesn't obviously match the target job.
- Don't reuse one summary for every application. It's the easiest section to tailor, and the two or three keywords you swap in are often what gets you past applicant tracking filters.
