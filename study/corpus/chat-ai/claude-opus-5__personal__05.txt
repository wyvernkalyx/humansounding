A few things worth naming first: saying yes doesn't lock you into the version of the event that's currently in your head. Most reunion regret comes from scope, not from the commitment itself. And renegotiating now is far cheaper socially than doing it in three months.

Your realistic options, roughly from least to most disruptive to the plan:

**Shrink the format.** A catered weekend at a rental house and a potluck at a park pavilion are both "the family reunion." Pick the version that costs you the least labor and just declare it. Most people care that it happens, not that it's elaborate.

**Become the coordinator, not the host.** Set the date and place, make a shared doc, and let everyone handle their own lodging, travel, and food contribution. You're the person with the spreadsheet, not the person cooking for forty.

**Delegate in specific assignments.** "Who wants to help?" produces nothing. "Can you handle drinks and ice?" and "Can you run the photo slideshow?" produce results. Break it into five or six discrete jobs and hand each one to a named person.

**Recruit a co-organizer.** One specific person you ask directly, not a general appeal. Splitting it changes the experience more than splitting the work would suggest.

**Buy your way out of the labor.** A restaurant's private room or a venue that handles setup and cleanup costs money but eliminates the parts that are actually grinding. Worth pricing before you assume it's out of reach.

**Postpone it a year.** If the problem is that this particular season of your life is packed, moving it is a small ask.

**Hand it back.** Tell people now, plainly: "I said yes too fast and I can't do this. Someone else should take it." This is a legitimate option, not a moral failure. It gets more expensive the longer you wait, and the reunion may simply not happen — which is a real outcome you'd have to be okay with.

Which one fits depends on what's actually driving the regret. If it's time and logistics, delegating or shrinking solves it. If it's money, split costs upfront or go potluck. If it's dread of particular family dynamics, none of the above helps much — for that, the fix is a shorter event with a defined end time and your own transportation.
