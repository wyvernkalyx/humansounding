That hesitation is worth paying attention to rather than feeling guilty about. "I love my child" and "I don't want to live with my child" are completely compatible positions, and plenty of parents get stuck because they treat the second one as evidence against the first.

A few things that might help you sort out what you're actually feeling:

**What specifically are you dreading?** The answers are pretty different depending on whether it's losing the quiet and privacy you've built, worry that he'll get too comfortable and stall out, old conflicts you don't want to relive, the financial hit, or the effect on your relationship with a partner. Some of those are solvable with clear terms. Some aren't.

**What's driving his request?** Moving home to save for a down payment on a defined timeline is a very different proposition from moving home because things are falling apart and there's no plan. The first is a project with an end date; the second can become open-ended in a way that's hard on everyone.

**What would make it tolerable, if you said yes?** Rent or a contribution, a target move-out date, specific household responsibilities, expectations about guests and noise and shared space. Vague generosity tends to breed resentment; explicit terms — awkward as they are to state out loud — usually protect the relationship better than unspoken assumptions do.

And "no" is a real option. So is "not right now," or "yes, for four months," or "I can help you with a deposit instead." You're allowed to weigh your own life at this stage against his convenience, especially if he has other workable paths.

What's his situation, and what's the part that's giving you the most pause?
