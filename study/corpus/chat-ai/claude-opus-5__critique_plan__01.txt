In the meantime, here's what most commonly gets missed on a weekend cutover of this size. You can use it as a self-check while you re-send:

**Things that break Monday morning**
- **Non-human senders.** Multifunction printers/scanners, CRM, ERP, monitoring/alerting, backup jobs, ticketing systems, anything using SMTP relay or an app password. These are the #1 forgotten item.
- **Mobile devices.** 400 users often means 500+ devices. Do they reconfigure themselves, or is that 500 support interactions? What's your instruction plan for BYOD?
- **Outlook profile rebuilds.** Cached mode means OST re-download for everyone at once — check your internet egress bandwidth against 400 simultaneous syncs Monday at 8am.

**Data and objects beyond user mailboxes**
- Shared mailboxes, resource/room calendars, equipment mailboxes
- Distribution lists, dynamic groups, mail-enabled security groups
- Aliases / secondary SMTP addresses (users will notice fast if one is dropped)
- Delegate permissions, calendar sharing, "send as"/"send on behalf"
- Server-side rules, forwarding rules, out-of-office messages
- Archives, PSTs, retention/legal hold, journaling for compliance

**Mail flow and DNS**
- MX TTL lowered days in advance (not during the cutover)
- SPF, DKIM, DMARC updated for the new sending infrastructure — a DMARC miss means your mail starts landing in spam
- Autodiscover records
- Inbound spam filter re-pointed, allow/block lists migrated

**Timing math**
- What's your actual throughput ceiling? Total mailbox data ÷ realistic ingest rate (migration services throttle aggressively). Many weekend plans fail here — run the number and add 50%.
- Are you doing pre-staged syncs with a final delta over the weekend, or a cold copy? A cold copy of 400 mailboxes rarely fits in 48 hours.

**Process gaps**
- **Pilot group.** Did 10–20 users go first, a week or two earlier, with real work? If not, you're finding your surprises at scale.
- **Go/no-go checkpoint** with a defined time — e.g., "if we're not at X by Saturday 6pm, we roll back."
- **Rollback plan** that's actually been tested, including what happens to mail delivered to the new system before you roll back.
- **Monday support surge.** Extra staff, floor-walkers, a known escalation path. Expect 15–30% of users to file a ticket.
- **Comms:** notice ahead, an "it's done, here's what to do" message, and a channel that works when email doesn't (Teams/Slack/SMS/phone tree).
