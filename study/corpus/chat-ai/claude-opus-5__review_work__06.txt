Yes, it's perfectly acceptable. "Circle back" is standard business English at this point — it means "return to this topic later," and virtually everyone in an office setting will understand it without a second thought.

That said, a few considerations:

**It has a reputation.** It regularly tops lists of most-hated corporate jargon, alongside "synergy," "touch base," and "leverage." Some people find it grating precisely because it's so ubiquitous. Nobody will think less of you professionally for using it, but a certain type of reader will roll their eyes.

**Plainer alternatives exist** and often read better:
- "I'll follow up on Thursday."
- "Let's revisit this after the budget is approved."
- "I'll get back to you once I hear from Legal."

These have the advantage of being more specific — "circle back" is vague about *when* and *why*, which is sometimes exactly why people reach for it. If you're using it to soften an indefinite delay, a reader may notice the fuzziness.

**Context matters.** In an internal Slack message or a routine email to colleagues, it's unremarkable. In a formal proposal, a message to a client who values directness, or writing for a non-native English audience, the plainer version is safer — idioms like this don't always translate cleanly.

Short version: use it if it comes naturally. But if you find yourself writing "let's circle back and touch base offline to leverage our synergies," that's the point to reconsider.
