"Everyone thinks it's a bad idea" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and it matters a lot *which* bad idea they think it is.

If the objection is basically "you're 41," that's not an argument. You'll be 45 in four years whether or not you have the degree. Plenty of people finish programs in their 40s and work another 20-25 years afterward — that's a longer career runway than most people realize. And adult students often do *better* than 22-year-olds, because they actually know why they're there.

But sometimes "that's a bad idea" is a clumsy way of saying something more specific and more legitimate:

- **The debt math doesn't work.** $120k for a field that pays $55k is a bad deal at any age, and it's worse at 41 because you have fewer years to amortize it.
- **The credential isn't the bottleneck.** Some career changes need a degree (nursing, law, accounting, teaching). Others just need a portfolio, a certification, or someone willing to hire you. It's worth being sure you're buying the thing that actually unlocks the door.
- **The household would take the hit.** If a partner absorbs more income pressure or childcare for three years, their skepticism deserves a real conversation, not just reassurance.
- **They're projecting.** Sometimes people who feel stuck get uncomfortable when someone near them tries to get unstuck. That one you can mostly disregard, though it stings.
