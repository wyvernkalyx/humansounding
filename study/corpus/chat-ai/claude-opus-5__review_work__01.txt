Honestly? It's not good — and the reason is diagnostic: you could paste this sentence onto the website of a payroll software company, a management consultancy, an office furniture supplier, or a corporate catering firm, and nobody would notice anything was off. A mission statement that could belong to any company tells a reader nothing about yours.

**The specific problems:**

- **"Empower"** — one of the most worn-out words in business writing. It means "we help," but with an inflated feeling attached.
- **"Unlock their full potential"** — a cliché that's been unmoored from meaning. Potential to do what? Grow revenue? Reduce waste? Enter new markets?
- **"Innovative solutions"** — this is the phrase equivalent of a blank space. Nearly every company claims innovation, and "solutions" is a placeholder for the actual thing you sell.
- **"Businesses"** — all of them? A two-person bakery and Unilever have almost nothing in common. Naming a real segment costs you nothing and gains you credibility.

**What's missing:** who you serve, what problem you solve, and how you're different from the alternative. A good mission statement usually contains at least one thing a competitor couldn't honestly claim.

**Try answering these plainly:**
1. Who specifically is your customer?
2. What's the painful, concrete problem they have before you show up?
3. What does their world look like after?
4. What do you do that the obvious alternative doesn't?

**For contrast, here's the shape of a stronger version:**

> "We help mid-sized manufacturers cut unplanned downtime by predicting equipment failures before they happen."

Still plain, but it tells you the customer, the problem, and the mechanism. Nobody could swap their own name into it.
