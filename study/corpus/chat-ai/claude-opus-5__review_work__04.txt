Probably not as written — but the fix is small.

Two problems with it:

**"Things have been crazy here"** tells the client you're overloaded, which reads as "you weren't a priority" and quietly raises doubts about whether you can handle their work. Internal chaos isn't their problem, and mentioning it doesn't buy you goodwill.

**There's no substance.** An apology with no new timeline or deliverable attached just restarts the waiting. The most reassuring thing you can send isn't a better apology — it's a date.

Some alternatives depending on the situation:

If you have the thing now: *"Thanks for your patience — here's the [X]."* Short, no grovelling, moves on.

If you don't yet: *"Apologies for the slow reply. I'll have [X] to you by Thursday."* Commit to something specific, then hit it.

If it's been genuinely long and the relationship matters: *"I'm sorry for the delay on this — that was longer than it should have been. Here's where things stand: [status]. You'll have [X] by [date]."* Own it plainly, no excuse, then pivot to facts.

One caveat: if this is a client you've worked with for years and the delay was two days, your original line is perfectly fine. Warmth and informality are assets in an established relationship, and over-correcting into stiff professionalism can read as odd or distant. The advice above matters most with newer clients, or when the delay was long enough that they noticed and wondered.
