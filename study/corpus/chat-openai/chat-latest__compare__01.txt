Practically, both can make your internet traffic appear to come from another IP address, but they work at different levels.

- A proxy usually handles traffic from a specific app or protocol. For example, you might configure your browser to use a proxy, while Spotify and other apps still connect normally. Many proxies don't encrypt traffic themselves.
- A VPN typically routes most or all network traffic from your device through the VPN provider and encrypts the connection between your device and the VPN server. It's generally more comprehensive.

For everyday use:
- Want to change the IP/location for one browser or application? A proxy can be simpler.
- Want protection on public Wi-Fi or to route your whole device through another network? A VPN is usually the better fit.
- Want anonymity? Neither guarantees it. The VPN/proxy operator can potentially observe information about your connections, so you're shifting some trust from your ISP/network operator to that provider.

One important nuance: HTTPS already encrypts the contents of connections to HTTPS websites. A VPN adds an encrypted tunnel around your traffic until it reaches the VPN server; it doesn't replace HTTPS or make an untrustworthy website safe.
