Keep it about the recurring hazard and the solution, rather than whose tree or responsibility it is. A friendly conversation before the next storm is usually better than raising it immediately after dealing with another branch.

You could say: “Hey, I wanted to mention the tree by our property line. We’ve had branches come down in our yard during several storms, and I’m getting concerned that a larger one could cause damage or hurt someone. Would you be willing to have an arborist take a look at it? I’d like to figure out a solution that works for both of us.”

A few things can keep it from escalating:

- Assume they may not realize how often branches are falling.
- Take a few dated photos so you can explain the pattern without making accusations.
- Ask for an arborist assessment rather than immediately demanding removal; pruning may be enough.
- Don’t start by arguing about legal liability or threatening to cut the tree. Those are options to investigate if cooperation fails.
- If cost seems to be the sticking point, you can discuss sharing the cost of an assessment or relevant pruning, if you're comfortable doing so.

If they dismiss the concern and the tree appears hazardous, document the condition and conversations and check your local rules before doing any trimming yourself. Tree law varies considerably by location, especially for branches crossing property lines and visibly dangerous trees.
