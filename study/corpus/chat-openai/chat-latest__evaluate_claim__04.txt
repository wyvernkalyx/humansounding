Treat “cut onboarding time in half” as a hypothesis until the vendor defines exactly what they measured.

Ask them to pin down:

- “Onboarding time”: Is that time to account creation, initial setup, first productive use, or full proficiency?
- “Half”: Half the elapsed calendar time, employee hours, training hours, or something else?
- Baseline and comparison: 50% versus what process, and how similar was it to yours?
- Evidence: How many customers/users were measured, over what period, and were unsuccessful implementations included?
- Attribution: Was the improvement caused by the tool, or did customers also change staffing, workflows, training, or integrations?
- Distribution: Ask for median and range, not just an average; a few dramatic successes can distort the mean.
- Conditions: What implementation work, integrations, data cleanup, consulting, or process changes are required to achieve the result?

For purchasing purposes, translate the claim into your own metric. If onboarding currently takes 20 calendar days and 12 staff-hours per hire, ask them to commit to which number they expect to become 10 or 6, under what conditions.

A useful response to the vendor is: “Please show us how you define onboarding time, the baseline behind the 50% claim, the underlying customer results, and what assumptions would need to hold for us to expect a similar outcome.” Ideally, validate it with a pilot using a pre-agreed metric before putting the 50% figure into your business case.
