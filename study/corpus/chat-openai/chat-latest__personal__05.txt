You have several options, and they don’t all involve either suffering through it or canceling the reunion.

- Scale it down. Change the plan from a big production to something manageable: a picnic, restaurant reservation, potluck, or one-afternoon gathering.
- Share ownership. Ask specific relatives to take whole pieces off your plate: “Can you handle food?” “Can you manage RSVPs?” “Can you organize activities?” It works better than a general request for help.
- Become the coordinator, not the provider. Set a budget, deadline, and basic plan, then make attendees responsible for contributing money, food, lodging arrangements, or setup.
- Hand it over. Tell the family you underestimated the commitment and need someone else to lead. You can offer to help with one defined task rather than running everything.
- Postpone or cancel. If there are financial, health, work, or personal reasons that make organizing it unrealistic, you’re allowed to change the plan. Earlier notice is usually better than pushing yourself until the last minute.

If you want to renegotiate without creating unnecessary drama, something like this is enough: “I agreed to organize the reunion before I realized how much I’d be taking on. I still want us to get together, but I can’t manage the whole event myself. I can handle [specific part], but I need other people to take responsibility for [parts], or we’ll need to simplify the plan.”
