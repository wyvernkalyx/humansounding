It’s reasonable to be conflicted. Your son can genuinely need support while you can genuinely value the independence, privacy, or stability you have now. You don’t have to decide from guilt or give an immediate yes or no.

Before agreeing, it may help to separate two questions: “Do I want to help him?” and “Do I want him living in my home?” Those aren’t the same. You could offer other kinds of help if living together isn’t something you want.

If you’re considering saying yes, make it a specific arrangement rather than an open-ended return to the old parent-child dynamic. Agree in advance on things like:
- Why he’s moving back and what he’s working toward
- A target move-out date or a date when you’ll formally reassess
- Rent or contributions to groceries and utilities
- Chores, guests, privacy, noise, parking, and use of shared spaces
- Expectations around employment, school, saving money, or whatever is relevant
- What happens if the arrangement isn’t working

Putting those agreements in writing can prevent a lot of resentment later.

And if what you actually feel is, “I love him, but I don’t want to live together again,” that’s a legitimate answer. You could say something like: “I want to support you, and I’ve thought carefully about this. I’m not comfortable having you move back in. I’d like to talk about other ways I might be able to help you figure out your next step.”
