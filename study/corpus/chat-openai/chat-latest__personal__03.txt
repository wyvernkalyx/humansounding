Eleven years can be either stability or being stuck; the length itself doesn’t tell you much. A better test is whether the job is still giving you something you value in exchange for what it costs you.

Consider a few questions:
- Are you still learning, gaining responsibility, or increasing your earning power?
- If this exact job were offered to you today, at its current pay and conditions, would you take it?
- Do you stay mainly because you like the work and life it supports, or because leaving feels risky or overwhelming?
- Are there things you want—more money, autonomy, purpose, flexibility, challenge—that realistically aren’t available there?
- When you imagine being in the same position three years from now, do you feel content, indifferent, or disappointed?

Stability is valuable when it’s a choice that serves your priorities. It starts looking more like stuckness when inertia or fear is making the choice for you.

You also don’t need to quit to find out. Updating your résumé, looking at current openings, talking to a few people in your field, or even interviewing can tell you what eleven years of experience is worth elsewhere. Exploring your options is not a commitment to leave; it gives you enough information to decide whether staying is actually the better option.
