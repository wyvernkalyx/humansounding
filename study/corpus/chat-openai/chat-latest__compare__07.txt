For a small brand, the simplest distinction is:

- Trademark protects your brand identity—things that tell customers who made or sells something. This can include your brand name, logo, slogan, and sometimes distinctive packaging. For example, if you run “Blue Pine Coffee,” trademark law can help stop another coffee company from using a confusingly similar name or logo.
- Copyright protects original creative works—things you create, such as website copy, product photos, illustrations, videos, music, or sufficiently creative graphic designs. It generally does not protect a brand name, short slogan, idea, or business concept.

A few practical differences, especially in the U.S.:

- Trademark rights can arise from actually using a mark in commerce, but federal registration provides significant additional protections and benefits. Rights can potentially last indefinitely as long as the mark remains in qualifying use and required renewals are made.
- Copyright generally exists automatically once an original work is created and fixed in a tangible form. Registration isn't required for copyright to exist, but U.S. registration provides important enforcement benefits. Copyright eventually expires.
- A logo can sometimes have both protections: trademark law can protect it as a source identifier, while copyright can protect its original artistic elements.

For many small businesses, trademark is the more important issue when choosing a company or product name. Before investing heavily in a name, it's worth checking for existing confusingly similar trademarks—not merely whether the domain name or business registration is available.
