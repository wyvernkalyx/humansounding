Not quite. Standing desks can be useful, but the benefits are often overstated.

The strongest evidence is that sit-stand desks help reduce prolonged sitting, especially during the workday. Alternating between sitting and standing may also reduce lower-back discomfort for some people. They’re not a substitute for exercise, though, and there’s limited evidence that simply standing substantially improves weight, cardiovascular health, or longevity.

Standing all day isn’t ideal either; prolonged standing can cause foot, leg, or back discomfort. A better approach is to change positions regularly: sit, stand, and—most importantly—move. Brief walking or movement breaks every 30–60 minutes likely matter more than whether you spend those minutes at a sitting or standing desk.

So your coworker is partly right if they mean “standing desks aren’t a health miracle,” but wrong if they mean they have no benefit.
