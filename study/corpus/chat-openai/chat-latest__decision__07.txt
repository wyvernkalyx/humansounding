Probably not in the traditional sense. For a two-person company, annual ratings, lengthy forms, and formal review cycles usually create more bureaucracy than value. But you should still have a deliberate process for giving feedback and discussing performance.

A lightweight approach is usually better:

- Hold a recurring conversation every 1–3 months, separate from day-to-day problem solving.
- Discuss what’s working, what isn’t, priorities for the next period, and what each person needs from the other.
- Review a few concrete goals or responsibilities rather than assigning ratings.
- Include two-way feedback, especially if one person is the founder/manager.
- Write down key decisions, goals, compensation changes, or performance concerns afterward.

Some formality becomes more important if one person is an employee rather than a cofounder—particularly when compensation, promotions, equity, or possible termination are involved. Documentation can prevent misunderstandings and provide a useful record.

So: skip the corporate-style performance review, but don't skip structured performance conversations. A 30–45 minute quarterly check-in plus a short written summary is generally plenty for a two-person company.
