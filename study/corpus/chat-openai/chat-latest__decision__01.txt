For a booking system with ~50,000 records, I’d recommend PostgreSQL.

The record count is small for either database, so the important factor is the nature of booking data. Bookings usually involve relationships and consistency requirements: users, resources/rooms, availability, payments, cancellations, and preventing double bookings. PostgreSQL is particularly well suited to those.

Key advantages:
- Transactions and constraints make it easier to prevent conflicting or invalid bookings.
- Foreign keys naturally model relationships between customers, bookings, resources, etc.
- SQL is excellent for queries such as availability, date ranges, utilization, revenue, and reporting.
- PostgreSQL has strong indexing and date/time support.
- 50,000 records is trivial for Postgres; millions of records would generally still be routine with sensible schema/indexes.

MongoDB can make sense if your records are highly variable documents with few relational constraints, but that usually isn't the core shape of a booking system. MongoDB supports transactions too, but you’d generally be giving up some of the natural benefits of a relational schema without gaining much here.

A typical Postgres design might have `users`, `resources`, `bookings`, and `payments` tables, with constraints/indexes around booking times. Depending on what you're booking, PostgreSQL exclusion constraints can even enforce that two bookings for the same resource don't overlap at the database level.

So unless you have a specific MongoDB-driven requirement, use PostgreSQL.
