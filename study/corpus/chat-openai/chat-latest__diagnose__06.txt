You likely have two related meeting-design failures: weak time control and weak decision capture. Common causes are:

- No outcome-driven agenda: topics like “discuss X” expand indefinitely because nobody knows what must be decided.
- No timeboxes or active facilitation: one issue consumes the meeting while later items get squeezed.
- Too much status reporting: information that could be async takes time away from decisions and problem-solving.
- Unclear decision rights: discussion continues because it’s unclear who recommends, who provides input, and who ultimately decides.
- Decisions aren’t explicitly closed: the group moves on without someone stating, “The decision is X.”
- No live record: relying on everyone’s memory produces different versions of what happened afterward.
- Actions lack owners and dates: “we should do X” gets mistaken for a commitment.

A simple fix is to make every agenda item specify an owner, desired outcome (“decide,” “get input,” or “inform”), and timebox. Assign a facilitator and a note-taker; capture decisions and actions visibly during the meeting. When a timebox ends, either decide, delegate, or explicitly schedule follow-up rather than silently running over.

Finish five minutes early by reading back only: decisions made, action items with owner/deadline, and unresolved issues. Send that same record afterward. After 2–3 weeks, you’ll be able to tell whether the underlying problem was meeting structure, facilitation, or genuinely difficult decision-making.
