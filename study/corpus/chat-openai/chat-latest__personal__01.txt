You don’t need to disclose the offer just because your boss asked. The best response depends on whether you’d consider staying, but avoid lying; a denial can hurt you later if they learn you were interviewing.

A good neutral answer is:

“I’ve had some conversations about opportunities, but I haven’t made any announcements or final decisions. I’m focused on handling things professionally. If there’s anything that would affect my role here, I’ll make sure we discuss it directly.”

If you’d genuinely stay for the right circumstances, you can be a little more open:

“Yes, I’ve been exploring an opportunity. I haven’t wanted to create unnecessary uncertainty, but I’m also thinking seriously about my next career step. I’m happy to talk about what my future here could look like.”

If you’ve already decided to accept the competitor’s offer, don’t use the question as an informal resignation unless you’re ready to give notice now. You can say you’ve been evaluating opportunities and that you’ll communicate formally once you’ve made your decision.

I would not reveal the competitor’s name, compensation, or offer details unless doing so serves a specific purpose. Also check the new offer for contingencies before giving notice—background checks, references, approvals, start-date conditions, and so on.
