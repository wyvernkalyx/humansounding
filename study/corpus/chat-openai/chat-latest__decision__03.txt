Potentially, yes—but 15% is large enough that the remote job should give you meaningful value beyond just working from home.

A useful way to decide is to compare the pay cut with what remote work actually saves you:

- Commuting: fuel/transit, parking, tolls, car depreciation.
- Time: If you eliminate 1–2 hours of commuting per day, that's roughly 250–500 hours a year. Put a value on that time.
- Work expenses: lunches, clothing, childcare/pet care, etc.
- Taxes and benefits: Compare total compensation, not just salary—bonus, retirement match, health insurance, PTO, equity, and location-based taxes.
- Quality of life: flexibility, sleep, ability to exercise, family time, and where you can live.
- Career impact: promotion prospects, job stability, learning, networking, and whether the remote employer has a healthy remote culture.

For example, going from $100k to $85k costs $15k gross, but perhaps only around $9k–$11k in after-tax income depending on your situation. If remote work saves $5k in annual expenses and 400 commuting hours, you're effectively trading perhaps $4k–$6k of spendable money for 400 hours of your life. That can be an attractive trade.

I'd be more inclined to take the cut if the commute is substantial, you strongly value flexibility, your finances still work comfortably at the lower salary, and the new job has comparable benefits and career prospects. I'd hesitate if the current commute is short, the cut would reduce savings or create financial stress, or "fully remote" isn't guaranteed in writing.
