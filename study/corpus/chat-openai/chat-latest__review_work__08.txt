“Synergize” is weak as a product name unless the product has a very specific reason for using it.

The main problems:
- It sounds like corporate jargon: “synergy,” consulting decks, team-building workshops.
- It’s generic. The name doesn’t create much curiosity or a distinctive mental image.
- It feels dated—more 2000s enterprise software than a modern product.
- It’s difficult to own as a brand because “synergize” is an ordinary word and is already used by numerous businesses/products.
- The “-ize” ending gives it a slightly buzzword-generator quality.

There are situations where it could work. If you’re deliberately leaning into corporate aesthetics, or the product literally combines separate tools/teams/systems, the meaning is immediately understandable. But even then, I’d look for something more distinctive.

If you tell me what the product does, who buys it, and the vibe you want, I can suggest stronger names.
