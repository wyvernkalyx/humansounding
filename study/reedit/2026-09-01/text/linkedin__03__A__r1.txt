**Most of you know how this one ended. So I'll skip the recap and go to the part I've been sitting with.**

The failure wasn't the shutdown. It was month six.

That's when I had enough information to call it, and didn't. I told myself I needed more data. What I actually needed was to not be the person who'd spent a year selling everyone on a thing and then walked it back. Sunk cost gets framed as a finance problem. Mine was pure ego.

The rest of it — the polite customer calls we read as interest, the discovery phase we compressed because we were "already sure" — those were real mistakes, but they were survivable ones. Plenty of projects recover from bad early assumptions. Fewer recover from a lead who won't say the quiet thing out loud.

What I'd tell myself at month six: the team can absorb a change of direction. They're a lot less good at absorbing six more months of watching you not make one.

To everyone who worked on it — you built exactly what I asked for, and you built it well. That part was never in question.
