**A few tips for adapting this:**
- **Quantify at least two accomplishments.** Numbers ("cut cycle time 40%") are far more persuasive than adjectives ("hardworking").
- **Map to the job description.** Pull two or three requirements from the posting and give direct evidence for each — internal committees screen for exactly this.
- **Include one honest growth area with evidence of improvement.** It makes the praise credible; pure superlatives read as boilerplate.
- **Address the "backfill" question if you're the current manager.** Signaling that you support the move despite the cost to your own team carries real weight.

If you share the employee's actual accomplishments and the role details, I'm happy to draft a fully specific version.
