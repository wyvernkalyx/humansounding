Assuming this is for an email body or an application-form text box — places where a formatted letter looks out of place and people skim. I kept the warm-reader framing and cut to about 125 words.

---

**Subject: Marketing Coordinator — [Your Name]**

Hi [First Name],

I'm applying for the Marketing Coordinator role, and I'd want to spend the first few months on three things: [opportunity #1], [opportunity #2], and documenting the content workflow so it doesn't live in one person's head.

Those come from what I've seen [volunteering / contracting] here since [year] — including [one specific project in four or five words]. I know the constraints, and I'd rather do a few things consistently than announce ten.

Résumé attached. Happy to talk whenever it's useful.

[Your Name]
[Phone] • [Email]

---

**What I cut and why:**

- **The "since you already know the background" opener.** Clever framing costs a sentence you no longer have. Lead with the plan.
- **The specific-moment anecdote.** It was the emotional core of the longer version, but at this length the concrete plan does more work.
- **The bulleted list.** Three items in a sentence read faster on a phone than three bullets with sub-clauses.
- **The 90-day line.** It was redundant once the plan moved to the top.

What survived: the plan, the proof you've been inside, and the constraint acknowledgment. Those are the three things a warm reader can't get from your résumé.

**If the placement is different than I guessed:**

- **LinkedIn message** — drop the subject line and "Résumé attached," add "I've applied through the portal."
- **Form field with a hard character limit** — cut the second paragraph to just "This comes from [volunteering] here since [year]."
- **Still a formal letter, just shorter** — say so and I'll restore the salutation and sign-off block.
