Here's the same offer written for someone who already knows you — a lapsed member or a former trial user. That means cutting the equipment list, the hours, the "what makes us different" pitch, and the tour of your value proposition. They've seen the place. Re-explaining it reads as if you've forgotten them.

**Subject line:** Your spot's still here

**Alternate subject lines:**
- Come back in January — no joining fee
- We kept your locker warm
- It's been [X] months. Let's fix that.

**Preview text:** Rejoin by Jan 31 and we'll waive the fee. No re-onboarding, no speech.

---

Hi [First Name],

You know how this works. You know where the squat racks are, you know [Coach Name] talks too much during [class name], and you know the parking lot gets rough at 6pm on Tuesdays.

So no pitch. Just the offer:

**Rejoin by January 31 and we'll waive the joining fee — and give you your first month at $[XX].** Same no-contract terms as before. Cancel whenever.

If it's been a while, [Coach Name] will put together a re-entry plan so you're not walking in cold and trying to pick up where you left off in [month/year]. Twenty minutes, free, no upsell.

👉 [BUTTON: Reactivate My Membership]

That link pulls up your old account — nothing to re-enter.

If you're not coming back, that's genuinely fine, and you can [unsubscribe here] with no hard feelings. But if it's been on your list since October, this is the cheapest week to do something about it.

[Name]
[Gym Name] · [Phone]

*Offer valid for previous members reactivating by January 31, [Year]. [Terms.]*

---

**What changed and why:**

- **The opening does the work of establishing familiarity** — specific, slightly self-deprecating details prove you remember them. Generic warmth ("we've missed you!") doesn't.
- **The offer moves to the top.** A cold reader needs context before a price. A warm reader is only deciding yes or no.
- **"Re-entry plan" replaces "free consultation."** Names the actual barrier for lapsed members, which is usually embarrassment about lost fitness, not lack of information.
- **The explicit opt-out** is a trust move and it cleans your list. On win-back campaigns it tends to lift response from people who *are* still interested.
- **Send timing shifts later** — around Jan 8–15. Lapsed members often wait out the New Year crowd, so the second wave converts better than Jan 1.
