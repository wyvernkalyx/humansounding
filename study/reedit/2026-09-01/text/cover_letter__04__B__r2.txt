# Cover Letter — School Administrator (Short Version)

Roughly 175 words, built for an email body, an application text field, or any placement where the reader sees the first two lines and decides whether to read the rest. The ask moves to sentence one; the diagnosis leads the body.

---

**Subject: [Your Name] — Candidate for [Assistant Principal], [School Name]**

Dear [Dr./Ms./Mr. Last Name],

I'm applying for the [Assistant Principal] position at [School Name]. You know my work, so here's what I'd do with the role.

Our gap isn't teacher effort—it's inconsistency between classrooms in [subject/grade band]. I'd move to short, frequent observations with same-week feedback, and protect PLC time for student work instead of logistics. First concrete step: [e.g., common formative assessment in K–2 literacy by October].

Second priority is retention. We're losing teachers in years two and three. I'd formalize the mentoring that currently happens by accident and start retention conversations in spring, not August.

One honest caveat: [genuine gap, e.g., my budget experience is department-level and I'd need support in year one]. And moving me out of [current role] leaves a hole in [area] worth planning for before any announcement.

Happy to talk it through whenever useful.

**[Your Name]** | [Phone]

---

### What I cut

- **Culture and operations sections.** Two priorities land harder than four in a short format. Save the master-schedule argument for the interview.
- **The closing paragraph about knowing the building.** Implied by the diagnosis itself—showing beats saying.
- **Hedging and connective tissue.** "I'd rather use this letter to…" is a sentence about the letter, not about the job.

**Kept deliberately:** the caveat and the succession note. They're short, and dropping them would make the letter read as a pitch rather than a straight conversation with someone who knows you.
