# Sunsetting the [Tool] Classic Integration

**Posted [Date] · [Category: Product Updates]**

**TL;DR:** The Classic [Tool] integration will stop working on **[Sunset Date]**. If you're one of the [~X%] of workspaces still using it, switch to the new [Tool] integration before then — migration takes about [5 minutes] and preserves your existing [syncs/mappings/data].

---

## What's changing

We're retiring the Classic [Tool] integration, which we first shipped in [Year]. It will be fully disabled on **[Sunset Date]**.

The new [Tool] integration, available since [Month Year], replaces it entirely and adds:

- **[Two-way sync]** instead of one-directional pushes
- **[Field-level mapping]**, including custom fields
- **[Real-time webhooks]** rather than [15-minute] polling
- **[OAuth-based auth]** — no more managing API keys manually

## Why we're doing this

The Classic integration was built on [Tool]'s [v1 API], which [Tool] is deprecating on [Date]. Rather than patch a version that would keep degrading, we've focused our engineering time on the new integration — which is faster, more reliable, and something we can actually keep improving.

Maintaining both versions also meant every new feature had to be built twice. Consolidating means fixes and improvements reach everyone sooner.

## What you need to do

If you see a **"Classic"** label under **[Settings → Integrations → [Tool]]**, you're affected. Here's how to migrate:

1. Go to **[Settings → Integrations]** and click **Connect** next to [Tool].
2. Authorize with your [Tool] account. (You'll need [admin permissions] in [Tool].)
3. Review the imported field mappings — we carry over your Classic configuration automatically, but it's worth a quick check.
4. Click **Activate**, then disconnect the Classic integration.

Your historical [synced data / records / logs] stays exactly where it is. Nothing is deleted during migration.

### Notes for adapting this

- **Give at least 60–90 days' notice** for anything customers have built workflows around; 30 days is the bare minimum for a minor integration.
- **Lead with the date and the action**, not the reasoning. People skim.
- **Be specific about what's *not* affected** — most sunset anxiety is about data loss.
- **Don't over-explain the "why."** One honest paragraph beats three defensive ones. If the real reason is low usage, it's fine to say "fewer than 1% of workspaces use it" — that's more credible than a vague appeal to focus.
- **Match the channel to the impact**: in-app banner + email to affected admins for anything that breaks workflows; changelog entry alone for low-impact removals.
