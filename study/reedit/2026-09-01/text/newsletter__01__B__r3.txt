**THE SLOW BUILD**
*Issue #48 — Small changes, stacked over time*

---

**Subject:** Your morning routine isn't broken. It's expired.

**Preview:** For people on their fifth attempt: the failure modes that come *after* you know how to build one.

---

If you've read the books, you already have the anchors and the two-minute rule. Knowing more about habit formation isn't your constraint and hasn't been for years.

Here's the actual problem: **routines don't break, they get outgrown** — and the skill that builds one is not the skill that maintains it. Everything below assumes you can already build. These are the failure modes on the other side.

---

**The floor became the ceiling.**
You shrank it until it was stupid. It worked. Eighteen months later you're still doing one page. The minimum viable version was supposed to be a ramp, but nothing in the system ever tells you to leave it — perfect adherence to something that stopped mattering.

*Fix:* hold two numbers. A **floor** (never below, protects the chain, never changes) and a **default** (a normal day when nothing has gone wrong). Review the default quarterly. If it's been static for a year, you're maintaining a streak, not a habit.

**The reason expired.**
The 6am writing block was built to finish a book. The book came out in 2023. This is the most common failure in people who are *good* at habits — the routine outlives the goal, gets absorbed into identity, and becomes hard to kill precisely because you're proud of keeping it.

*Fix:* expiration dates. Every element gets one, six or twelve months out. On that date it has to be re-argued for, not automatically renewed.

**You don't know what's load-bearing.**
Six elements, and one or two are doing nearly all the work. You've never run it without any given piece, so you're guessing.

*Fix:* remove one element for two weeks. Note what degrades. Restore, move to the next. A quarter to get through six items, and you'll cut at least one permanently.

**You designed for your good weeks.**
Normal Tuesdays aren't where routines die. The flu is. The newborn is. Anyone can hold a system at full capacity.

*Fix:* three named tiers. **Full** (75 min). **Compressed** — load-bearing element only, for travel and deadlines (20 min). **Survival** — ten breaths outside, for crisis. The content matters less than the naming. Dropping to Survival should be a move within the system, not evidence it failed.

---

**Re-entry is a separate skill**

Never-miss-twice handles a bad Tuesday. It does nothing for the six-week gap, where you stopped for legitimate reasons and now there's a wall around restarting. What I've learned doing this badly, repeatedly:

- **Restart below your floor.** You'll want to resume at your old default. You'll fail in four days and add another layer to the wall.
- **Don't diagnose first.** That's procrastination in a lab coat. Restart tiny, *then* diagnose with current data.
- **Kill the debt narrative.** There's no catch-up. Nothing is owed. The chain was a useful fiction until it started charging interest.
- **Expect two weeks that feel worse than starting from zero.** The comparison to your old competence is the entire cost, and it's temporary.

---

**The metric you've outgrown**

You're tracking whether you did the thing. Right metric for month one.

Better question now: **is the downstream thing moving?** Once a quarter, ignore the calendar of Xs and ask what the hour is *for*. "Feeling like myself" is a legitimate answer. Just say it out loud, because if that's the goal, half your elements are optimized for something else.

---

**This week: an audit, not a habit**

1. List every element with its honest duration.
2. Mark each **load-bearing**, **scaffolding**, or **unexamined**.
3. Note the goal it was built for, and whether that goal is still live.
4. Write your Compressed and Survival tiers.
5. Remove the one unexamined element you're least willing to lose, for two weeks.

Step five is the whole exercise. The resistance you feel toward it is the information.
