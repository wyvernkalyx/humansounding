**Six months in on the four-day week. The update nobody asks for but everyone wants.**

The headline is boring: it works. [Delivery held / revenue up X%]. That part is settled and I'm not going to relitigate it.

What's less settled:

**Friday coverage is still a compromise, not a solution.** We're on our third model. Rotating on-call is the least-bad option so far, but "least-bad" is doing real work in that sentence. Anyone who tells you they solved this in one go is selling something.

**The meeting cuts didn't hold on their own.** We removed [X] recurring meetings in week two. By month four, six had quietly grown back under new names. Compression isn't self-sustaining — it needs someone whose actual job is to defend it.

**Role parity is the unresolved one.** [Engineering / product] absorbed the change easily. [Client-facing / support] absorbed it by working harder inside four days, which is not the same thing and we knew it and did it anyway for a while. Fixing that has been the real project.

**And the leadership thing is true.** If you're answering Slack on Fridays, you've quietly repriced the whole arrangement for everyone below you. I've been bad at this. I'm less bad now.

The thing I'd tell my six-months-ago self: the four-day week isn't a policy you install. It's an ongoing argument with entropy.

Ask me again at twelve months.
