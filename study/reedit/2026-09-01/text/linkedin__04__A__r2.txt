**"Attitude over experience" fails because attitude isn't scored and experience is.**

Everyone agrees with the principle. Then the shortlist gets built on years-in-seat anyway — because hiring managers default to whatever they can defend in a debrief.

Three things worth scoring, because they predict something and you can hear them in an interview:

**How fast they say "I don't know."** Not humility as a trait — speed. The lag between not knowing and admitting it is the best proxy I've found for how someone behaves when a project goes sideways.

**What they do with a correction.** Give real feedback in the interview and watch. Do they adjust, or defend the original position?

**Whether they've thought about the problem before asking about it.** Curiosity without homework is just enthusiasm.

Write the criteria down before the interview. Otherwise "attitude" quietly becomes "someone I'd enjoy working with," and you've swapped a visible bias for an invisible one.
