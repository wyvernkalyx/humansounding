**I knew at month six. I didn't call it until month twelve. That's the failure — not the shutdown.**

I told myself I needed more data. What I needed was to not be the person who'd spent a year selling everyone on a thing and then walked it back. Sunk cost gets framed as a finance problem. Mine was ego.

The bad early assumptions were survivable. Plenty of projects recover from those. Fewer recover from a lead who won't say the quiet thing out loud.

What I'd tell myself at month six: the team can absorb a change of direction. They're much worse at absorbing six more months of watching you not make one.

To everyone who worked on it — you built exactly what I asked for, and you built it well. That part was never in question.
