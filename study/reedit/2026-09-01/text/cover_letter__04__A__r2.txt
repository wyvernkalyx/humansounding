Dear Dr. Broussard,

I want the principalship at Willow Creek Middle School, and I have spent four years eleven miles away solving the problem Willow Creek has not yet solved: middle-grades literacy.

As assistant principal at Eastview Middle, I supervise 62 faculty, own the master schedule and MTSS process, and evaluate the ELA and social studies departments. We rebuilt teacher observation around short, frequent visits with same-day feedback — eight to ten touchpoints a year instead of two formal walkthroughs. Seventh-grade reading proficiency went from 41 to 58 percent in three years. We replaced suspension-first discipline with tiered interventions and restorative conferences; out-of-school suspensions fell 44 percent. Our disproportionality gap narrowed but did not close, and that remains unfinished.

I also treat operations as instructional work. A schedule that protects a real 40-minute intervention block, a budget aligned to priorities rather than legacy line items, systems that keep teachers out of busywork. I built all three at Eastview — including one schedule redesign that cost teachers common planning time and that I had to undo midyear and rebuild with a faculty committee.

What I would bring to Willow Creek is presence: classrooms daily, dismissal, games, hard conversations early rather than stored up for an evaluation. I also speak Spanish, which matters in a building where a third of families do.

References include Principal Dana Okafor at Eastview, two department chairs, and our campus advisory council chair. I would welcome a conversation.

Sincerely,
