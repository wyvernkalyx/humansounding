**A few notes for adapting this:**

- **Specificity beats superlatives.** One vivid anecdote outweighs three paragraphs of "hardworking and dedicated."
- **Establish your basis for comparison.** "Top 5% of students I've taught in 15 years" is far more useful to a committee than "excellent."
- **State your relationship and its duration up front** — committees discount letters from writers who clearly don't know the student well.
- **Address weaknesses briefly if they're visible** on the transcript; silence can look like avoidance.
- **Tailor the fit paragraph to each program.** Naming specific faculty signals that the application is considered, not scattershot.

If you tell me the student's field, your relationship to them, and two or three specific things they've done, I can produce a tighter, fully customized draft.
