Dear Dr. Broussard,

I am applying for the principalship at Willow Creek Middle School. I have spent the last four years as assistant principal at Eastview Middle, eleven miles down the road, and I have watched Willow Creek work through the same pressures we have — a fast-changing student population, a young faculty, and a middle-grades literacy problem that no one has fully solved. I would like to lead that work.

At Eastview I supervise 62 faculty across all three grade levels and own the master schedule, the MTSS process, and teacher evaluation for the ELA and social studies departments. Three results matter most:

We rebuilt the observation cycle around short, frequent visits with same-day feedback rather than the twice-yearly formal walkthrough. Teachers now get eight to ten touchpoints a year. Seventh-grade reading proficiency moved from 41 to 58 percent over three years, and our approaches-grade-level rate in math rose 12 points.

We replaced a suspension-first discipline system with tiered interventions and a restorative conference protocol. Out-of-school suspensions fell 44 percent in two years. Our disproportionality gap for Black and Hispanic students narrowed but did not close, and I want to be straightforward that this remains unfinished work.

We built a new-teacher mentorship structure with protected weekly time. Of the 19 first-year teachers who came through it, 17 returned for year two and 14 are still with us.

I also know the operational side of the job is not an afterthought. A master schedule that protects a real 40-minute intervention block, a budget that funds instructional priorities instead of legacy line items, and systems that keep teachers out of administrative busywork — these are the conditions that make good instruction possible. I have built all three at Eastview, and I have made mistakes doing it. My first attempt at a schedule redesign cost teachers common planning time, and I had to undo it midyear and rebuild it with a faculty committee. It was the right correction and a better schedule.

What I would bring to Willow Creek is presence. I am in classrooms daily, at dismissal, and at games. I have hard conversations early rather than storing them up for an evaluation. And I speak Spanish, which matters in a building where roughly a third of families do.

I would welcome the chance to talk with you and the committee. My references include Principal Dana Okafor at Eastview, two department chairs, and the parent who chaired our campus advisory council.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
