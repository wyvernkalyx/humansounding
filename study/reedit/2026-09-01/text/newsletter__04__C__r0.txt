# WORK IN PROGRESS
### *A newsletter about doing good work without disappearing into it*

**Issue #47 — "The Tank Is Empty and the Light Has Been On for Miles"**

---

Hi friends,

A reader wrote in last month with a line I haven't been able to shake:

> *"I don't hate my job. I just can't feel anything about it anymore."*

That's the sentence that made me want to write this issue. Because most people wait for burnout to look like a dramatic collapse — the crying-in-the-parking-lot moment — when it usually shows up as something much quieter. Flatness. A four-line email that takes forty minutes. Sunday afternoon dread that starts creeping into Saturday night.

Let's talk about it properly.

---

## First: burnout is not the same as being tired

This distinction matters more than it sounds like it does.

The World Health Organization classifies burnout in the ICD-11 as an *occupational phenomenon* — not a medical condition — resulting specifically from **chronic workplace stress that hasn't been successfully managed**. Researcher Christina Maslach, who has studied this for four decades, describes it along three dimensions:

1. **Exhaustion** — depletion that sleep doesn't fix
2. **Cynicism / detachment** — mentally checking out, growing distance from the work and the people in it
3. **Reduced sense of efficacy** — the creeping feeling that nothing you do matters or is any good

Notice that only the first one is about energy. If you're just tired, a vacation helps. If you're burned out, you come back from vacation and feel the dread return somewhere around baggage claim.

**That's the tell.** Rest that doesn't restore is the signature symptom.

---

## The more useful question: *which mismatch is yours?*

Here's what I think most burnout advice gets wrong. It treats burnout as one undifferentiated blob to be solved with sleep and boundaries. But Maslach and Michael Leiter identified six areas where a person and their job can fall out of alignment — and the fix depends entirely on which one is broken.

Read these and notice which makes your stomach drop:

**⚖️ Workload** — Too much, for too long, with no recovery periods built in. The most obvious one, and often not the real one.

**🎛️ Control** — You're accountable for outcomes you have no authority to influence. Being responsible without being empowered is uniquely corrosive.

**🏅 Reward** — Not just money. Recognition, meaningful feedback, the sense that good work is *noticed*. Invisible effort burns people out fast.

**🤝 Community** — Isolation, unresolved conflict, a team where trust has quietly evaporated.

**🧭 Fairness** — Favoritism, opaque promotions, inconsistent standards. Injustice at work is exhausting in a way that mere volume isn't.

**💭 Values** — You're being asked to do things that conflict with what you believe is right or important. This is the one people are most reluctant to name, and it's often the deepest cut.

If your burnout is a *values* mismatch, no amount of meditation app usage is going to touch it. If it's a *community* problem, more time alone at home will make it worse. Diagnose before you treat.

## What actually helps

### This week (stop the bleeding)

- **Protect one recovery ritual.** Not a whole self-care overhaul — one thing. A walk without your phone. Lunch away from your desk. Non-negotiable.
- **Reintroduce one non-work identity.** Burnout is partly a narrowing of self. Anything that reminds you that you're a person who does other things counts.
- **Downgrade something on purpose.** Pick one task this week and do it at 80%. Notice what happens. (Usually: nothing.)
- **Say the word out loud** to one person you trust. Naming it breaks the "I'm just weak" spiral.

### This month (change the conditions)

- **Audit your calendar for one week.** Mark each block as energizing, neutral, or draining. Patterns will appear that you already half-knew.
- **Have the conversation with your manager.** More on that below.
- **Re-establish one real workplace relationship.** Social connection at work is one of the strongest protective factors we know of. A twenty-minute coffee is a legitimate intervention.
- **Get one thing off your plate for good.** Not deferred — *removed*. Delegated, declined, or killed.

### This quarter (the structural stuff)

- **Ask whether the role is fixable.** Some jobs are recoverable with adjustments. Some are not. Both answers are okay.
- **Rebuild competence somewhere.** Reduced efficacy is a real symptom, and small, completable projects are unusually good medicine.
- **Consider professional support.** Burnout and depression overlap significantly, and they're not the same thing. A therapist or physician can help distinguish them. This is not an escalation — it's a reasonable thing to do.

## The part nobody wants to say

**Burnout is usually an organizational problem being experienced as a personal failure.**

If four people on your team burned out this year, that is not a coincidence of four individuals with poor boundaries. That's a system producing a predictable result.

Which means: yes, do the yoga, protect the evenings, take the walk. But hold onto the knowledge that individual coping strategies are a tourniquet, not a cure. You are allowed to conclude that the job is the problem. You are allowed to conclude that the job is the problem *and* you're going to stay for now for reasons of your own — money, timing, health insurance, a market that's rough right now. That's a strategy, not a failure. Just be honest with yourself that that's what you're doing, and set a date to revisit it.

## Scripts, because the hardest part is opening your mouth

**To your manager:**
> "I want to keep doing good work here, and I'm running at a pace I can't sustain. Can we look at priorities together? If everything on this list stays, I need help understanding what's genuinely first — and what can slip."

Note what this does: it's about *sustainability and prioritization*, not complaint. It hands them a solvable problem.

**To decline something:**
> "I can take that on if we move X, or I can get it to you the week of the 14th. Which is more useful?"

**To a colleague you're worried about:**
> "You've seemed pretty stretched lately. No agenda — just wanted to check in. How are you actually doing?"

Then be quiet and let the silence do its work.

---

## 👔 If you manage people

Three things, and they're all more effective than a wellness webinar:

1. **Cut work, don't add coping.** Resilience training in an over-loaded team is an insult with a slide deck.
2. **Make effort visible.** People withstand a great deal when they believe someone noticed.
3. **Model the behavior.** If you email at 11pm, your "protect your evenings" speech is noise. Schedule-send is free.

## 📬 Reader question

**"How do I know if I'm burned out or just in the wrong career?"**

Honestly? Often you can't tell until you've rested. Exhaustion is a terrible vantage point for making large life decisions — everything looks intolerable from the bottom of the well. If you can, restore first, decide second. Take the leave. Take the two weeks. If clarity returns and the answer is still "wrong career," it'll still be the answer in a month, and you'll be in far better shape to act on it.

## 🪶 One small thing this week

Find the one recurring meeting you dread most and ask whether it needs you. Not whether it's important — whether it needs *you*. Half the time it doesn't, and nobody's ever going to volunteer that information.
