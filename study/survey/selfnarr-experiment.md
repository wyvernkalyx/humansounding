# The framing line does not work, and here is what does happen

2026-08-29. Experiment run to answer two questions: does self-narration live in
post-correction revision rather than in first drafts, and would promoting the
"you are writing to a reader, not an evaluator" line to the top of the skill
change the output.

**Answers: yes to the first, no to the second.**

## What was already known

The August 28 measurement found self-narration at essentially zero across all
nine corpora and concluded the habitat was multi-turn revision, not single-turn
generation. I rebuilt the instrument today with six regex families, one per form
in rule 13, and confirmed it:

| corpus | ANY self-narration, per 1,000 words |
|---|---|
| Claude genre corpus | 0.14 |
| GPT-5.5 genre corpus | 0.05 |
| Claude chat corpus | 0.12 |
| GPT-5.5 chat corpus | 0.06 |
| Gemini, both corpora | 0.00 |
| ChatGPT, both corpora | 0.00 |
| Human Medium | 0.00 |
| Human newsletters | 0.00 |

The chat corpora are conversational in register but still single-turn: one fresh
answer to a prompt nobody has pushed back on. So they were never a test of the
hypothesis. The habitat had to be built.

## The corpus that did not exist

Fifteen post-correction revision sessions. Each one: a writing task, a draft, a
specific user correction, and then the assistant's reply. The reply is the
measured document, including whatever it says alongside the revised text, because
that is where the behaviour lives.

Five scenarios, chosen so the correction is natural and two of them correct a
factual error: a wrong ship date, a self-congratulatory LinkedIn post with a
wrong duration, reversed causation in a technical explanation, an invented
statistic, and a note that is too long.

Three conditions, five sessions each, all generated by Claude Sonnet:

- **A** — no skill. Control.
- **B** — the skill exactly as it ships today.
- **C** — the skill with a new "Who you are writing to" section promoted above
  Tier 1, stating the evaluator point as a framing stance rather than as a
  rationale buried inside rule 13.

Scoring was done by a separate model, blind to condition, with the files
shuffled and renamed. It was given the six forms and told to quote exact spans.

## Result 1: the habitat is real, and it is not close

| corpus | self-narration per 1,000 words |
|---|---|
| every single-turn corpus, nine of them | 0.00 to 0.14 |
| **post-correction replies, all conditions** | **14.3 to 19.9** |

Two orders of magnitude. The behaviour the rule describes is almost entirely
absent from first drafts and pervasive the moment someone says "that's wrong."

Thirty-seven instances in 2,208 words. Every one of them is form 3 (processing
the correction), form 4 (describing the revision), or form 6 (commenting on
prose). **Forms 1, 2 and 5 — rating your own confidence, announcing your
integrity, telling the reader what matters — did not appear once.** Those are
the forms that show up in long analytical prose, which is what I write, and not
in a two-line reply handing back a fixed draft.

## Result 2: the framing line did nothing

| condition | docs | words | self-narration | per 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, no skill | 5 | 841 | 12 | 14.3 |
| B, skill as written | 5 | 665 | 11 | 16.5 |
| C, skill + framing line | 5 | 702 | 14 | 19.9 |

C is the worst of the three. With five documents per cell that difference is
noise, and I am not claiming the framing line makes things worse. What I am
claiming is that there is no evidence it helps, and on this evidence it should
not ship.

The honest reading: the behaviour is not caused by the model failing to
understand who it is writing to. It is caused by the correction itself. The
model is answering the person who just corrected it, and that person genuinely
is an evaluator at that moment. Telling it to imagine a reader does not change
the fact that a real evaluator just spoke.

## What the skill did do

Closing offers — "let me know if you want me to expand on that", "say the word",
"Better?" — went from **4 in the control to 0 with the skill and 1 with the
framing line.** That is rule 4's banned-phrase list working exactly as designed.

Which is the pattern worth taking away. The skill is good at removing things it
can name as strings. It is not good at removing a move that has unbounded
surface forms.

## The instrument failure, which is the best part

My six regex families were validated first: 13 of 13 hand-built positives caught,
0 of 12 negatives. Then I ran them over these fifteen documents.

**They found zero of the thirty-seven instances a reader found.**

Because the real forms are things like: "Right." "Fair." "Fixed:" "Good catch."
"Try this:" "Shorter:" "Here's the plainer version." "dialed back the
victory-lap tone." No regex reaches those without also flagging half of ordinary
English.

That is the checker-versus-skill post, measured. A pattern-matcher scores all
fifteen documents clean. A reader finds thirty-seven instances in 2,208 words.
The dividing line between what belongs in the checker and what belongs in the
skill is not importance or severity. It is whether the tell has a bounded set of
surface forms.

## What to actually do instead

Do not add the framing line. The evidence does not support it.

The gap the experiment exposes is different and more fixable: **the skill has no
rule about handing back a revision.** It is written for drafting. Every one of
the 37 instances is a revision-transaction move — accepting the correction,
announcing what changed, characterising the old draft's tone.

A concrete rule to test next: *when returning a revised draft, return the draft.
No acknowledgement of the correction, no summary of what you changed, no
characterisation of the previous version.* That is a bounded instruction with a
testable prediction, unlike a stance about audience. It would need the same
three-condition test, and it should be tested before it ships, on the same
principle that stopped the framing line from shipping today.

## Limits

Five documents per condition. One generating model. One judging model, though
blind to condition and asked for exact quotes, which I spot-checked. One round,
one set of five scenarios. The condition differences are not significant and I
am not presenting them as significant. The 100x habitat difference is large
enough to survive all of that; the framing-line comparison is a null result, not
a refutation.
