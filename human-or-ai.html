Human or AI?

Ten passages, one at a time · no signup, no cookies

Everyone has a theory about how to spot AI writing. This site has spent months measuring those theories against real text, and fourteen of the fifteen most popular rules failed. That leaves an obvious question unanswered: the rules are bad, but is the instinct bad?

That is what this measures. You will see one passage at a time and guess who wrote it. After each guess you say what tipped you off — and that second answer is the interesting one, because it is how we find out whether people are reading style or reading content.

Exactly half the passages are human and half are AI. You are told this on purpose. It means guessing gets you 50%, and knowing the split cannot help you beat it.

The human half was written before ChatGPT existed — blog posts from 2004 and newsletters from 2017 to 2022. Nobody in that half had a model to write with. The AI half was prompted to write about the same years, so "this mentions something recent" will not give it away.

One thing you should know about these results before you add to them. You found this page on a site about AI writing tells, so you are primed to look for them in a way an ordinary reader is not, which should push a score up. Working against that: nothing rides on your answer here. Someone deciding whether to accuse a student or an employee reads with a consequence attached, and that is not what this measures. Two pulls, opposite directions, and we have not measured either. So take what this group scores as a number about people like you, clicking through with nothing at stake, and not as a ceiling or a floor on what anyone else could do.

No account, no email, nothing stored that identifies you. What gets recorded is your guess, your reason, and how long you took.

The method was written down and locked before any answer was collected, including what would count as a result and what would count as a failure. It is public, in the repo, along with every change made to it and why.
